Opinion
We have audited the accompanying Consolidated 
Financial Statements of Apollo Micro Systems Limited 
(here in after referred to as the “Holding Company”) and 
its subsidiaries (Holding Company and its two subsidiaries 
and two step down subsidiaries together referred to as 
‘the Group’), which comprise the Consolidated Balance 
Sheet as at 31 March 2026, the Consolidated Statement 
of Profit and Loss (including Other Comprehensive 
Income), the Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity 
and the Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows for the 
year then ended, and notes to the Consolidate Financial 
Statements, including material accounting policies and 
other explanatory information (hereinafter referred to as 
‘the Consolidated Financial Statements’).
In our opinion and to the best of our information and 
according to the explanations given to us, the aforesaid 
Consolidated Financial Statements give the information 
required by the Companies Act, 2013 (‘the Act’) in the 
manner so required and give a true and fair view in 
conformity with the Indian Accounting Standards (‘Ind 
AS’) prescribed under section 133 of the Act read with 
the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015

accepted in India, of the consolidated state of affairs of 
the Group as at 31 March 2026, of its consolidated profit 
and other comprehensive income (comprising of profit and 
other comprehensive income), consolidated changes in 
equity and consolidated cash flows for the year then ended.

4. Accounting for Business Combination – Recognition of 
Goodwill on Provisional Basis
	
During the year, the Group acquired 100% of the equity 
shareholding of [ IDL Explosives Limited] (“IDL”) through a 
share acquisition, whereby equity shares of the Company 
were allotted to the shareholders of the IDL in consideration 
for the entire share capital of the IDL. Consequent to this 
transaction, the IDL became a wholly-owned step-down 
subsidiary of the Company, and the transaction has been 
accounted for as a business combination under Ind AS 
103, Business Combinations.
	
At the acquisition date, the IDL identifiable liabilities 
exceeded its identifiable assets (i.e., negative net assets), 
while the fair value of consideration transferred was 
positive. This has resulted in the recognition of goodwill 
amounting to H 14,517.66 lakhs, being the excess of 
consideration transferred over the net identifiable assets 
(negative) acquired, in accordance with paragraph 32 of 
Ind AS 103.
	
As at the balance sheet date, the Company has not carried 
out a fair valuation of the identifiable assets acquired 
and liabilities assumed of the Target. Management 
has proposed that the Purchase Price Allocation (PPA) 
exercise, including fair valuation of assets and liabilities and 
consequent reassessment of goodwill, will be completed 
within the measurement period of twelve months from the 
acquisition date, as permitted under paragraphs 45–50 of 
Ind AS 103. Accordingly, the goodwill recognized as at the 
balance sheet date is provisional in nature and is based on 
the book values of assets and liabilities of the Target rather 
than their fair values, pending completion of the PPA.
	
This matter has been determined to be a Key Audit Matter 
because:
 
 
The determination of the fair value of consideration 
transferred involves significant estimation and judgment.
 
 
The quantum of goodwill recognized is material to 
the financial statements, and any subsequent fair 
valuation during the measurement period could result 
in a significant retrospective adjustment to goodwill, 
with consequential impact on the opening balance 
sheet and comparatives.
	
Given the materiality of the amount involved, in net 
negative assets the judgment inherent in valuing the 
consideration transferred, and the fact that the purchase 
price allocation remains incomplete as at the reporting 
date, we have identified this as a Key Audit Matter.
	
Refer note 47 of consolidated financial statements.
Our audit procedures in relation to this matter included, 
among others:
 
 
Understanding and evaluating the terms of the share 
agreement and the basis on which the transaction 
has been accounted for as a business combination 
under Ind AS 103.
 
 
Evaluating the identification of the acquirer under Ind 
AS 103, confirming that the legal acquirer (the Group 
Company) is also the accounting acquirer.
 
 
Accounting of provisional goodwill recognized based 
on the consideration transferred and the book value of 
net assets (negative) acquired and agreeing this to the 
underlying acquisition-date balance sheet of the IDL.
 
 
Testing the journal entries recorded for the acquisition, 
recognition of the investment/net assets of the Target, 
and the resultant goodwill.
 
 
Evaluating the adequacy of disclosures in the financial 
statements as required under Ind AS 103, specifically:
 
 
The fact that the initial accounting for the business 
combination is incomplete for price allocation;
 
 
The assets, liabilities and goodwill recognized on a 
provisional basis.
 
 
Evaluating 
management’s 
future 
profitability 
assumptions used to support the commercial rationale 
for the acquisition purchase, by:
 
 
Comparing projected cash flows/profitability as per 
management estimates; and
 
 
Assessing the reasonableness of key assumptions 
(revenue growth, margins, synergies) with reference 
to industry data and past forecasting accuracy.
Obtained a written representation from management 
regarding its assessment that no impairment indicators 
exist for the provisional goodwill as at the reporting date.

Meaning of Internal Financial Controls with 
reference to Consolidated Financial Statements
A company's internal financial control with reference to 
consolidated financial statements is a process designed 
to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability 
of financial reporting and the preparation of consolidated 
financial statements for external purposes in accordance 
with 
generally 
accepted 
accounting 
principles. 
A 
company's internal financial control with reference to 
consolidated financial statements includes those policies 
and procedures that (1) pertain to the maintenance of 
records that, in reasonable detail, accurately and fairly 
reflect the transactions and dispositions of the assets 
of the company; (2) provide reasonable assurance 
that transactions are recorded as necessary to permit 
preparation of consolidated financial statements in 
accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, 
and that receipts and expenditures of the company are 
being made only in accordance with authorisations of 
management and directors of the company; and (3) provide 
reasonable assurance regarding prevention or timely 
detection of unauthorised acquisition, use, or disposition 
of the company's assets that could have a material effect 
on the consolidated financial statements.
Inherent 
Limitations 
of 
Internal 
Financial 
Controls 
with 
reference 
to 
Consolidated 
Financial Statements
Because of the inherent limitations of internal financial 
controls 
with 
reference 
to 
consolidated 
financial 
statements, including the possibility of collusion or 
improper management override of controls, material 
misstatements due to error or fraud may occur and not be 
detected. Also, projections of any evaluation of the internal 
financial controls with reference to consolidated financial 
statements to future periods are subject to the risk that 
the internal financial control with reference to consolidated 
financial statements may become inadequate because of 
changes in conditions, or that the degree of compliance 
with the policies or procedures may deteriorate.

2.6	 Measurement of fair values
	
Accounting 
policies 
and 
disclosures 
require 
measurement of fair value for both financial and non-
financial assets.
	
Fair value is the price that would be received to sell 
an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly 
transaction between market participants at the 
measurement date. The fair value measurement is 
based on the presumption that the transaction to sell 
the asset or transfer the liability takes place either:
	
-	
In the principal market for the asset or liability; or
	
-	
In the absence of a principal market, in the most 
advantageous market for the asset or liability.
	
The principal or the most advantageous market must 
be accessible by the Company. The fair value of an 
asset or a liability is measured using the assumptions 
that market participants would use when pricing the 
asset or liability, assuming that market participants 
act in their economic best interest.
	
A fair value measurement of a non-financial asset 
takes into account a market participant's ability to 
generate economic benefits by using the asset in 
its highest and best use or by selling it to another 
market participant that would use the asset in its 
highest and best use.
	
The Company uses valuation techniques that are 
appropriate in the circumstances and for which 
sufficient data is available to measure fair value, 
maximising the use of relevant observable inputs and 
minimising the use of unobservable inputs Fair values 
are categorised into different levels in a fair value 
hierarchy based on the inputs used in the valuation 
techniques as follows:
	
Level 1: quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets 
for identical assets or liabilities.
	
Level 2: inputs other than quoted prices included in 
Level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, 
either directly (i.e. as prices) or indirectly (i.e. 
derived from prices).
 
Level 3: inputs for the asset or liability that are not based 
on observable market data (unobservable inputs).
	
When measuring the fair value of an asset or a liability, 
the Company uses observable market data as far as 
possible. If the inputs used to measure the fair value 
of an asset or a liability fall into different levels of the 
fair value hierarchy, then the fair value measurement 
is categorised in its entirety in the same level of the 
fair value hierarchy as the lowest level input that is 
significant to the entire measurement.
	
The Company recognizes transfers between levels 
of the fair value hierarchy at the end of the reporting 
period during which the change has occurred.

Financial assets
	
Initial recognition and measurement
	
All financial assets are recognised initially at fair value

Purchases or sales of financial assets that require

Trade receivables generally do not contain any

Classification and Subsequent measurement
	
For purposes of subsequent measurement, financial 
assets are classified in two categories:
	
* Debt instruments at amortised cost
	
* Equity instruments
	
Financial assets at amortised cost (Debt instruments)
	
Financial asset’ is measured at the amortised cost if 
both the following conditions are met:
	
a.	
The asset is held within a business model 
whose objective is to hold assets for collecting 
contractual cash flows, and
	
b.	
Contractual terms of the asset give rise on 
specified dates to cash flows that are solely 
payments of principal and interest (SPPI) on the 
principal amount outstanding.
	
After initial measurement, such financial assets are 
subsequently measured at amortised cost using the 
effective interest rate (EIR) method. Amortised cost 
is calculated by taking into account any discount or 
premium on acquisition and fees or costs that are 
an integral part of the EIR. The EIR amortisation is 
included in finance income in the profit or loss. The 
losses arising from impairment are recognised in 
the profit or loss. This category generally applies to 
trade receivables.
 
Financial assets at fair value through OCI (FVTOCI) 
(debt instruments)
	
Financial assets are classified at FVTOCI when they 
are held within a business model whose objective is 
achieved by both collecting contractual cash flows and 
selling the financial assets, and the SPPI test is met.
