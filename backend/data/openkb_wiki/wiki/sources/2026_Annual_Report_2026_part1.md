India’s defence sector stands at an inflection point. 
The Government’s vision of ‘Viksit Bharat @ 2047’, 
anchored in Aatmanirbhar Bharat and accelerated 
indigenisation, has created a structural opportunity 
of a scale our industry has never seen. The Union 
Budget for FY 2026–27 allocated H7.8 lakh crore to 
the Ministry of Defence, including H2.19 lakh crore 
of capital outlay, with around 75% of the capital 
acquisition budget reserved for domestic industry. 
Policy, procurement and purpose are now aligned 
and companies with proven technology and the 
discipline to execute will define this era.
Apollo Micro Systems Limited enters this era with 
41 years of earned credibility. Our electronics 
and mission-critical subsystems serve some of 
the nation’s most sensitive programmes   nuclear 
submarines, torpedoes, underwater mines, 
decoys, UAVs, aerial bombs, artillery systems 
and missiles. That experience has given us more 
than technological depth; it has taught us the 
responsibility that comes with defending a nation.
A YEAR OF PURPOSEFUL 
TRANSFORMATION
FY 2025–26 was a landmark year in which Apollo 
moved decisively from subsystems to platforms. 
We strengthened our foundations, expanded our 
capabilities and repositioned the Company as 
an integrated defence technology and platform 
enterprise. Our portfolio now spans unmanned 
systems, counter-drone technologies, autonomous 
underwater platforms, missiles, torpedoes, land 
systems and advanced electronic warfare, supported 
by new infrastructure, manufacturing capacity, 
strategic licences and partnerships.

FY 2025–26 was a defining year for Apollo Micro 
Systems a year of decisive execution and strategic 
transformation. Revenue grew 61% year-on-year to 
H904 crore from H562 crore, while PAT rose 91% to 
H107 crore from H56 crore. These numbers matter, 
but what they represent matters more: the growing 
scale of our capabilities and the deepening trust 
placed in us as a technology partner to India’s 
defence and strategic sectors.
We invested H72 crore which is 8% of revenue in R&D, 
among the highest in the industry, and our R&D team 
recorded zero attrition. This translated into tangible 
outcomes: the Mini Torpedo, advanced sensor suites 
for underwater autonomous vehicles, FOG-based 
INS systems and a pipeline of next-generation 
technologies built for the evolving demands of 
modern warfare.

Global Economic Review1 
The global economy remained resilient in CY 2025, growing 
at 3.4% despite shifting geopolitical dynamics and evolving 
trade conditions. Although the macroeconomic environment 
saw policy adjustments, steady global trade activity and 
strong growth in technology-led exports helped sustain 
overall momentum and offset weaker performance in certain 
sectors. Developments in the Middle East led to more cautious 
market sentiment and pushed crude oil prices higher, raising 
inflationary concerns and prompting companies worldwide 
to revise their margin expectations. Geopolitical tensions 
have driven measurable shifts in economic priorities, with 
governments allocating more resources toward defence. 
While private consumption and broader capital investment 
have moderated, public sector defence spending has 
remained steady to support national security needs.
Emerging Markets and Developing Economies (EMDEs) 
were a key driver of global growth, expanding by 4.4% in 
CY 2025 on the back of strong domestic demand, rising 
industrial production, and stable exports. Advanced 
economies, meanwhile, stabilised with steady growth of 
1.9% for the year.
Global headline inflation remained contained at 4.1% in 
CY 2025, with advanced economies at 2.5% and EMDEs 
at 5.2%. Despite periodic disruptions from supply chain 
realignments and trade shifts, global trade volumes 
remained largely stable as sourcing networks adapted to 
changing geopolitical conditions.
Outlook
The global economy is projected to witness stable but 
measured growth ahead. Global GDP growth is projected 
at 3.1% in CY 2026 and 3.2% in CY 2027, reflecting a 
gradual slowdown as financial conditions and geopolitical 
factors continue to evolve. EMDEs are expected to 
maintain stronger momentum, with growth projected at 
3.9% in CY 2026 and 4.2% in CY 2027, supported by strong 
domestic consumption, growing infrastructure investment 
and favourable policy frameworks. 
While global inflation may fluctuate in the near term due to 
supply-side factors, price trends are expected to stabilise 
gradually over the medium term. Inflation trends remain 
closely tied to trade conditions, geopolitical developments, 
commodity prices and fiscal policy, indicating the increased 
importance of resilient global supply chains.
