# Apollo Micro Systems Limited — Annual Report 2025-26 (Part 2) Summary

## Document Scope
This part of the annual report covers Indian government space and defence initiatives, Apollo Micro Systems Limited's (AMS) opportunities and threats, company overview, FY 2025-26 milestones, financial performance, risk management, and the notice and explanatory statement for the 29th Annual General Meeting.

## Government Initiatives
- **Technology Development Fund (TDF)**: Ministry of Defence instrument supporting public and private enterprises, especially startups and MSMEs, for critical defence technologies. Grants up to ₹50 crore per project. As of June 2026, 80 projects worth ₹334 crore are under implementation; two project systems have flown on PSLV missions.
- **Chandrayaan**: After Chandrayaan-3 achieved the first soft landing near the Moon's south pole in 2023, Chandrayaan-4 is scheduled for 2027 to collect and return lunar samples. Chandrayaan-5 will explore water and other volatiles near the lunar south pole.
- **GAGAN**: GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation, an indigenous Satellite-Based Augmentation System co-developed by [[entities/isro]] and the Airports Authority of India. It enhances GPS accuracy and integrity for aircraft navigation, is globally certified, and supports precise satellite-based landings.
- **National Space Station**: The Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS), a key pillar of Space Vision 2047, is India's planned five-module Low Earth Orbit space station for long-duration human spaceflight and advanced research. The Union Cabinet approved first module BAS-01 by 2028 in September 2024 under the expanded Gaganyaan program.
- **Mission DefSpace**: Operates under the iDEX framework to promote indigenous dual-use technologies for the defence-space ecosystem. It has introduced 75 Defence Space Challenges across launch, satellite, communication/payload, ground, and software systems, enabling startups, MSMEs, and research institutions to co-develop with the Defence Space Agency and armed forces.
- **Private Sector Incentivisation**: Liberalised FDI policy, IN-SPACe Seed Fund Scheme, Pre-incubation Entrepreneurship Programme, ₹1,000 crore Venture Capital Fund, and ₹500 crore Technology Adoption Fund.

## Opportunities and Threats
**Opportunities**: Sustained growth in India's defence budget and capital acquisition; government target of ₹3 lakh crore defence production and ₹50,000 crore exports by 2029; indigenisation through the Positive Indigenisation Lists, the DcPP framework and DAP reforms; entry into explosives and energetics via [[entities/idl_explosives]]; growing export opportunities; expanding demand for space-grade electronics and dual-use technologies under Mission DefSpace.

**Threats**: Dependence on government procurement cycles; competition from DPSUs and private entrants; volatility in raw material, component, and energy prices; rapid technology evolution requiring continuous R&D; cybersecurity and data-protection risks; geopolitical supply chain and procurement disruption.

## Company Overview
[[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] is a Hyderabad-based defence technology company established in 1985. It designs, develops, and manufactures advanced electronic, electro-mechanical, and engineering systems for India's Armed Forces. AMS holds a Defence Industrial Licence, is AS9100 Rev D certified, and has delivered mission-critical systems to [[entities/drdo]] and the Indian Armed Forces over a 41-year track record. It has developed more than 700 technologies, with a focus on weapon electronics for ground, missile, and naval applications.

AMS is involved in over 150 indigenous defence programmes, with solutions qualified for production-phase supply under the Development cum Production Partner (DcPP) framework. It is an approved vendor for DRDO and DPSUs. It operates two Hyderabad facilities (Mallapur and Adibatla) and is building a new integrated facility at Hardware Park-II to expand capacity for fuzes, mines, and [[concepts/inertial-navigation-systems]].

## FY 2025-26 Milestones
- Completed integration of IDL Explosives Limited through a 100% acquisition by [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] on 15 November 2025.
- Advanced a greenfield expansion in Telangana, including a new integrated facility at Hardware Park-II.
- Received the Company's first-ever export order.
- Entered a tripartite alliance with the [[entities/indian_navy]] and [[entities/iit_madras]], and signed an MoU with [[entities/grse]].
- Obtained an industrial licence for high explosives including TNT and RDX through IDL Explosives, and secured a DPIIT licence covering UAVs, INS systems, radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, ATGMs, decoys, chaffs and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles, and loitering munitions.
- Received [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]] for advanced electro-optical tracking systems and for [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]], including laser-based variants.
- Vehicle-mounted [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]] are advancing, with field trials scheduled for FY 2026-27.

## Financial Performance
AMS operates in a single reportable segment of electromechanical components and systems and allied components and services. Consolidated operations additionally include industrial explosives through IDL Explosives Limited from 15 November 2025.

Standalone revenue from operations was ₹76,485.32 lakhs in FY26, up 36.08% from ₹56,206.92 lakhs in FY25. Consolidated revenue stood at ₹90,432.38 lakhs. PBT was ₹17,462.69 lakhs versus ₹8,380.43 lakhs. PAT was ₹12,057.89 lakhs versus ₹5,724.16 lakhs. EBITDA grew 73.50% YoY to ₹23,122.03 lakhs, with EBITDA margin at 30.23%. Total borrowings as of 31 March 2026 were ₹48,504.50 lakhs versus ₹29,069.20 lakhs. Equity capital stood at ₹3,572.92 lakhs. Tangible assets rose 54.07% to ₹27,091.52 lakhs. Cash and cash equivalents increased sharply to ₹2,024.62 lakhs from ₹21.58 lakhs.

Key ratios: gross profit margin 34.99%, EBITDA margin 30.23%, EBIT margin 27.68%, PAT margin 15.76%, RoE 12.49%, net debt-to-equity 0.73, interest coverage 5.71, current ratio 1.90. Significant ratio changes included net profit ratio up 54.80% and return on equity up 23.06%, while debt service coverage ratio declined 18.27%.

## Risk Management, HR, and Internal Controls
AMS uses a structured risk management framework reviewed annually by executive leadership. Key risks include order and customer concentration, execution and technology risk, raw material and supply chain volatility, working capital intensity, regulatory and licensing obligations (including the Explosives Act, 1884 and Explosives Rules, 2008), and cybersecurity. Following the IDL acquisition, safety systems extend to explosives manufacture, storage, and transport.

As of 31 March 2026, the workforce consisted of over 434 employees. Internal controls are overseen by the Board, with an independent internal audit team reporting to the Chairman of the Audit Committee.
