## Question-and-Answer Session (pages 4–22)

Summary: The partial document is an earnings call transcript of Apollo Micro Systems Limited for Q1 FY26, covering management’s opening remarks and a detailed Q&A session.

Key points covered include:

- PAT margin improved by 400 basis points year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter to 13% in Q1 FY26 from 9% in Q1 FY25.
- Received a maiden export order of $13.37 million (~₹113.81 crore) for developing an advanced avionics system for dual civil and military aircraft platforms.
- ACUITE upgraded the company’s credit rating by two notches; long-term rating now stands at ACUITE A- and A2+.
- Capex is progressing on schedule, and Phase 2 of the capex has started.
- Working capital cycle days are expected to reduce by 100–120 days from FY27 onwards.
- Received in-principle approval from stock exchanges for preferential allotment of equity shares.
- Revenue is expected to grow at a CAGR of 45–50% over FY26 and FY27, driven only by the core business, excluding the recent acquisition.
- Operating margins are expected to improve in H1 FY26 due to operating leverage and product mix, but ongoing capex may moderate margin expansion in H2 FY26 and into FY27.

In the Q&A, main updates included:

- Current order book stands at around ₹735 crore.
- MIGM: Apollo and BDL are development-cum-production partners; ToT documentation is being completed; the formal order is expected within the financial year; the company is preparing full manufacturing capabilities, including warhead through IDL Explosives.
- QRSAM: Apollo supplies four critical subsystems, including onboard computer, navigation system, power distribution units, and actuation system; orders are expected before December after BDL receives its order.
- Moored mine/deep sea mine: Apollo has developed the system and offered it to DRDO; the company expects a significant share of the DAC-approved order.
- Apollo claims to contribute around 63% of electronics and electromechanical work for DRDO missile programs.
- Project Kusha: Apollo is involved in onboard systems, is the only industry supplier to have delivered systems, and trials are planned within a few months.
- Torpedo programs: Updates on EHWT, ALWT, and Varunastra were provided, including Apollo’s supply of homing systems and signal processing, with user trials and expected orders in coming months.
- IDL acquisition formalities are nearing completion; consultants, including ex-DRDO scientists, are working on technology transfer and explosives manufacturing for missiles and MIGM.
- Promoter pledge reduction is on track toward zero by FY26.
- Export strategy: The company is developing full-fledged defense products, including mines, aerial bombs, submarine bombs, rockets, and kamikaze boats; ₹100 crore is earmarked for export-qualified product development.
- GRSE collaboration exists for joint product development, but details are not disclosed.
- BrahMos: Apollo is doing a couple of subsystems and recently received an order; the company expects further contributions.
- Pinaka: Apollo supplies guidance electronics to MIL and EEL; production orders are expected in the next 3–4 months, and export execution is underway.
- Fundraising: 50% of the amount mobilized has been received; warrants worth ₹400+ crore include ₹100 crore received and ₹325 crore expected in four months; proceeds are mainly for working capital, not capex.
- RF business: Apollo is building in-house RF capabilities and considering an acquisition after consolidating IDL.
- Drones: Apollo is developing defense cargo drones, with a glimpse shown at Aero India and final model to be released after internal trials.
- Management declined to disclose sensitive order values, citing regulatory and national security considerations.

The document ends with closing remarks thanking investors, reaffirming commitment to defense indigenization, and directing investors to contact the Investor Relations team for further queries.
