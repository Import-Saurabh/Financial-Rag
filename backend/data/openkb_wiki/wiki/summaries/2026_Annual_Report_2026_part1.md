# Apollo Micro Systems Limited — Annual Report 2025-26 (Part 1: Corporate Overview & MD&A)

This is the first part of the Annual Report of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] for the financial year 2025-26, comprising the corporate overview (pages 02–43) and the Management Discussion and Analysis report (pages 44–49). The accompanying letter to BSE and NSE announces the 29th Annual General Meeting, to be held on 30 September 2026 via video conferencing, and confirms submission of the Annual Report under Regulation 34(1) of the SEBI Listing Regulations.

The report's central theme is captured in the tagline **"Sovereign by Design. Global by Ambition."** — the positioning of Apollo as a company moving from being a Tier-I supplier of critical defence electronics to a recognised global Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) of complete weapon systems and platforms, a transition captured under [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]].

## Company Profile

- Established in 1997 in Hyderabad; the report cites **41 years** of accumulated credibility (tracing heritage to 1985 electronic CAD design services).
- Operates as an integrated defence technology company spanning explosives, design, development, manufacture, integration and lifecycle support.
- Claims capability across **more than 700 on-board technologies** covering weapon electronic systems for Ground, Missile and Naval Defence.
- Everything "under one roof": R&D, design, development, assembly, testing, mass manufacturing, plus custom Commercial Off-the-Shelf (COTS) solutions.
- Evolution traced from individual sub-systems → systems → complete weapons and weapon integration.
- Mission: "Convert Your Concepts into Reality"; vision of "Total Solutions Under One Roof."

### Four Pillars of the Vision
1. **A Global OEM, Made In India** — Apollo's name on the platform, not only inside it.
2. **Indian Deepening + Global Growth** — deepen domestic procurement presence and convert the first export order into sustained international revenue.
3. **Revenue Across Land, Air & Sea** — diversified streams so no single platform, programme or customer defines the company.
4. **Conviction + Futuristic R&D** — research strengthened in RF, AI, Autonomy and Inertial Navigation.

## Strategic Transformation: Licences and Acquisitions

- **Lifetime Arms Manufacturing Licence from DPIIT** — with no expiry, authorising manufacture, assembly, integration and proof-testing of complete strategic weapon systems: Missiles, ATGMs, Torpedoes, Underwater Mines, Aerial Bombs, Rockets, Loitering Munitions, Chaffs, Flares and Decoys — alongside licensed Unmanned Aerial Systems, Inertial Navigation Systems and Radar Equipment. See [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]].
- **Acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]]** — [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] (ADIPL), a subsidiary, completed a 100% equity acquisition for **₹107 crore in an all-cash transaction** (completed 15 November 2025). IDL manufactures packaged and bulk explosives for mining and infrastructure, plus cartridge explosives, with plants across India's key mining belts and relationships with Coal India subsidiaries. This marks entry into [[concepts/defence-energetics]] (high explosives).
- **Industrial Licence for HMX and TNT** (and RDX referenced by the MD) through IDL — entry into specialised defence energetics.
- **Greenfield Expansion** — additional land allotted by [[entities/tsiic]] Hardware Park Phase-II adjacent to the existing facility, taking the footprint to ~**10.68 acres**, with an investment of **₹300 crore** to manufacture, integrate and test Grad Rockets, ASW Rockets, Anti-Tank Mines and Artillery Munitions (Unit III).
- **MoU with the Government of Telangana** for a greenfield Defence and High-Energetic Explosives project with proposed investment of ~**₹1,500 crore**.
- **First export order** received during the year — an early signal of the global OEM ambition, consistent with [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]].

## Products and Platforms

- **Naval mines / underwater weapons**: Approved production agency for the Multi-Influence Ground Mine (MIGM), "Vighna," under DcPP by [[entities/drdo]] — see [[entities/migm]]. Product features on Moored Mines, Land Mines (PRACHAND anti-tank), and Limpet Mines; combat trials for the [[entities/indian_navy]] completed. See [[concepts/underwater-warfare]].
- **Aerial Bombs**: Nine variants of aerial drop bombs for release from aerial platforms such as drones; anti-personnel, anti-tank and anti-bunker roles; also configured as loitering munitions on UAVs.
- **Medium Range Rockets**: Compact form factor for vehicle-mounted launch systems; ground-to-air launch system developed to target drones and drone swarms.
- Diversified portfolio: Missiles, Underwater Weapons, Explosives, Drones, Vehicle-Mounted Counter-Drone Systems, Direct Energy Weapons, Rockets, Aerial Bombs, Ammunitions.

## Technology and Innovation

Three technology pillars:
1. **Radio Frequency Systems** — RF tracking and EW sensors, communication suites, Multi-Channel 55 kW Laser Directed Energy Weapon (DEW). See [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]].
2. **Artificial Intelligence** — embedded AI for sensor fusion, autonomous targeting, threat classification, battlefield analytics.
3. **Inertial Navigation** — MEMS-based INS, Fiber Optic Gyro (FOG) and Ring Laser Gyro systems. See [[concepts/inertial-navigation-systems]].

FY26 R&D outcomes: the **Mini Torpedo**, sensor suites for underwater autonomous vehicles, FOG-based INS systems, an indigenously designed **Safety and Detonation Device (SDD)** for the Indian Navy, advanced fuzes and Safety & Arming (S&A) mechanisms, jamming-resistant terminal guidance with anti-jam GNSS and Controlled Reception Pattern Antennas (CRPA), plus torpedo homing systems and autonomous maritime platforms.

A dedicated section addresses **Counter-Drone / Counter-UAS** as a layered detect-identify-decide-defeat problem — acoustic and passive RF sensing, direction finding, radar and electro-optical cueing, soft-kill RF jamming, extending to hard-kill integration on a common command-and-control layer. See [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]].

On **[[concepts/transfer-of-technology]]**, the report describes a five-part industrialisation chain for converting DRDO ToT into deployable systems: design-for-manufacture, qualified manufacturing and test infrastructure, a defence-grade supply chain, quality/certification systems (DGQA/DGAQA/DGNAI), and lifecycle support.

## Operational Excellence

- Acquisition of IDL expanded manufacturing capacity to **six plants**.
- Logistics: indigenisation-first procurement aligned with Atmanirbhar Bharat and Make in India; dual-sourcing, strategic inventory of critical semiconductors, localisation clauses with foreign OEMs, and backward integration into critical raw materials. See [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].
- Unified governance across Hyderabad electronics/systems facilities and Group explosives plants; common quality management system; SAP S/4HANA digital thread for batch traceability.
- Torpedo homing highlighted as one of the toughest qualification environments in defence electronics (underwater acoustics, sonar signal processing, shock-hardened packaging, naval armament inspection and sea trials).
- FY27 priorities: armament electronics, fire control systems, battle tanks, infantry combat vehicles, artillery platforms and vehicle-mounted counter-drone systems; the BM-21 Grad rocket; guidance and navigation systems including the [[entities/pinaka]] programme.

## People, ESG and Governance

- **Talent**: focus on RF/microwave engineering, embedded systems, signal processing, mechanical and energetics engineering; leadership pipeline built through stretch assignments; expanded safety training for explosives handling.
- **ESG**: new Unit III incorporates energy-efficient design, LED lighting, high-efficiency HVAC, rooftop solar provision, rainwater harvesting, water recycling and waste segregation. Environmental governance covers Pollution Control Board consents, PESO licences and hazardous-waste authorisations, with a baseline for Scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions per rupee of revenue under BRSR.
- **Social**: [[concepts/corporate-social-responsibility]] centred on community value; human rights embedded in the Code of Conduct; gender-neutral recruitment; POSH compliance, whistle-blower/vigil mechanism overseen by the Audit Committee.
- **Governance**: see [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]] — Board committees include Audit, Nomination and Remuneration, Stakeholder Relationship, Risk Management, Corporate Social Responsibility, Executive, and Securities Allotment.

## Industry Context (MD&A)

- **Global economy**: grew 3.4% in CY2025; projected 3.1% (CY2026) and 3.2% (CY2027). EMDEs grew 4.4%.
- **Indian economy**: real GDP growth 7.7% in FY2025-26; projected 6.6% for FY2026-27; CPI inflation moderated to 3.7%. Gross inward FDI rose 18.1% to $88.3 billion.
- **Global defence**: military spending reached **$2,887 billion** in 2025 (2.5% of global GDP); Europe's spending grew fastest since 1953 (+14% to $864 billion); the US spent $954 billion; Asia and Oceania $681 billion; India's spend cited at $92.1 billion, with military burden at 2.3%. See [[concepts/global-military-spending]].
- **India's defence sector**: production reached ~**₹1.78 lakh crore** (+15.6% YoY); defence exports hit a record **₹38,424 crore** (+62.66% YoY) to over 80 countries; DPSU exports up 151% to ₹21,071 crore and private sector up 14% to ₹17,353 crore. Targets: ₹3 lakh crore production by 2029 and ₹50,000 crore exports by 2029. The draft **Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2026** proposes higher indigenous content requirements of up to 60%. See [[concepts/defense_indigenization]].
- **Union Budget FY2026-27**: ₹7.8–7.85 lakh crore to the Ministry of Defence (~14.28–14.7% of the national budget), with ₹2.19 lakh crore capital outlay and ~75% of the capital acquisition budget reserved for domestic industry.
- **Energetics market**: India is among the largest producers/consumers of industrial explosives; defence energetics (TNT, RDX, HMX, PETN, propellants, warheads, fuzes) are a strategic priority under import embargo lists — see [[concepts/defence-energetics]].
- **Space sector**: India's space economy estimated at $8.4 billion; 15 missions in FY2025-26 including Gaganyaan crew module missions and NISAR; 400+ foreign satellites launched since 2014; plans for 52 surveillance satellites by 2030.
