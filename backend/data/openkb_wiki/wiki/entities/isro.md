# Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is India's national space agency and the principal government body responsible for the country's space programme. It appears in the document [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]] in the context of India's expanding civil and strategic space ambitions, which form part of the wider [[concepts/indian-space-programme]].

## Key Facts from the Document

### Chandrayaan Missions
After the success of Chandrayaan-3, which made India the first country to achieve a soft landing near the Moon's south pole in 2023, the nation's lunar ambitions have continued to expand:
- **Chandrayaan-4** is scheduled for 2027, with the objective of collecting lunar samples and returning them to Earth.
- **Chandrayaan-5** will explore water and other volatile materials near the lunar south pole.

These missions represent the civil-science dimension of India's space strategy and are cited in the report as evidence of the country's deepening technical capability.

### GAGAN
GAGAN (GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation) is an indigenous Satellite-Based Augmentation System co-developed by ISRO and the Airports Authority of India. The platform enhances GPS accuracy and provides integrity information for safer aircraft navigation. It is globally certified, facilitates precise satellite-based landings, and supports operations beyond commercial aviation. GAGAN reflects the theme of [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] applied to sovereign navigation infrastructure and technological self-reliance.

### PSLV and Technology Development Fund Linkage
The document notes that, under the Ministry of Defence's [[concepts/government-defence-funding-schemes]] (Technology Development Fund), two project systems have been flown in space as part of **PSLV missions**. PSLV, operated by ISRO, therefore serves as a carrier for dual-use and indigenously developed defence technologies.

### Bharatiya Antariksh Station
ISRO's institutional context also encompasses the planned [[concepts/indian-space-programme]] goals surrounding the Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS), India's planned five-module Low Earth Orbit space station under Space Vision 2047. The Union Cabinet approved development and launch of the first module, BAS-01, by 2028 as part of the expanded Gaganyaan programme.

## Significance

ISRO sits at the intersection of India's civil space science, sovereign navigation infrastructure and strategic space capability. Its programmes are referenced in the report as a pillar of national technological self-reliance and as a source of demand for space-grade electronics and dual-use technologies that benefit domestic defence suppliers such as [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]]. Related institutional actors include the defence research establishment [[entities/drdo]] and the Indian armed forces such as the [[entities/indian_navy]].
