## Overview

Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) is the Indian state-owned enterprise responsible for designing, building, commissioning and operating nuclear power reactors in the country. It sits under the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) and is the principal counterparty through which India's civil nuclear generation programme is procured. In MTAR's disclosure universe it appears as the end customer behind the civil nuclear order pipeline described in [[summaries/2026_Aug_2026_Transcript]].

## Role in MTAR's Civil Nuclear Business

Per the Q1 FY27 earnings call ([[summaries/2026_Aug_2026_Transcript]]), NPCIL and the DAE are the source of MTAR's reactor order flow, with management stating that [[entities/mtar-technologies]] holds a **60–70% wallet share** of orders for NPCIL/DAE-supplied reactors. This makes NPCIL the single most important demand driver for the [[concepts/civil-nuclear-power]] vertical.

Key programmes referenced:

- **Kaiga 5 & 6** — two reactors that generated MTAR's highest-ever order inflows in the segment during Q1 FY27. Execution timelines range from 1 to 3–3.5 years, with execution commencing in H2 FY27.
- **Mahi Banswara** — four 700 MW reactors being developed through an NTPC–NPCIL partnership. Tenders have been floated; MTAR would participate through EPC vendors rather than contracting directly. Because this is four reactors versus two at Kaiga, management expects the opportunity to be materially larger, with orders likely landing in the next financial year. See [[entities/mahi-banswara]].
- **Refurbishment of existing reactors** — an opportunity of roughly INR200+ crore across the installed fleet, with orders expected through FY27 and execution within two years.
- **Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor fleet orders** — the segment order book stood at INR684 crore at end-Q1 FY27, with a further ~INR130–140 crore expected in the following quarter, taking the total to ~INR800–815 crore, described as unprecedented for MTAR.

## Strategic Context

The Government of India's target of **100 GW of nuclear capacity by 2047** underpins the long-term demand outlook that NPCIL is tasked with delivering. MTAR's management characterised the vertical as having transitioned to a more consistent, less cyclical growth trajectory than 5–10 years ago, precisely because programme visibility now runs through NPCIL's multi-reactor build plan. This supports the broader [[concepts/order-book-and-revenue-visibility]] narrative for the company.

## Boundary With the Fast Breeder Programme

NPCIL should be distinguished from the fast breeder track. The [[entities/pfbr]] programme, which recently achieved criticality, is contracted **directly with the government** — MTAR supplied the majority of critical assemblies for IGCAR and Bhavini — not through NPCIL. MTAR therefore runs two parallel nuclear relationships: NPCIL/DAE for PHWR-class reactors (largely via EPC vendors) and the DAE fast-reactor establishment directly.
