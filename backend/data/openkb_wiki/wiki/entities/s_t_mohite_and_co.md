# S.T. Mohite & Co

**S.T. Mohite & Co** is a firm of Chartered Accountants registered with the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) under Firm Registration Number **011410S**. It serves as the **statutory auditor** of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], issuing the Independent Auditor's Report on both the Company's standalone financial statements and its consolidated financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026, and signing the accompanying Notes forming part of those statements.

## Key facts from the FY 2025-26 audit

- **Signing partner**: CA. [[entities/hima_bindu_sagala]], Partner, Membership No. 231056.
- **Place and date of report**: Hyderabad, 18 May 2026 — the same date on which the Board approved and authorised the financial statements for issue.
- **ICAI UDIN**: 26231056YFSVJL3298 (standalone report); 26231056QQVHAH6926 (consolidated report).
- **Opinion**: Unqualified (clean) on both the standalone and consolidated financial statements. The firm concluded the statements give a true and fair view of the state of affairs as at 31 March 2026, complying with the Companies Act, 2013 and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS).
- **Audit framework**: Standards on Auditing (SAs) specified under section 143(10) of the Act, together with the ICAI Code of Ethics.
- **Signature on the notes**: The firm signs the notes to both the standalone and the consolidated financial statements, alongside the Managing Director [[entities/karunakar_reddy_baddam]], Director [[entities/krishna_sai_kumar_addepalli]], Chief Financial Officer [[entities/sudarshan_chiluveru]] and Company Secretary [[entities/rukhya_parveen]]. In the consolidated statements the firm's signature appears on "Signatures to Note 1 to 55", dated Hyderabad, 18 May 2026, with UDIN 26231056QQVHAH6926.

## Key audit matters identified

### Standalone financial statements

The firm reported five [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] for FY 2025-26:

1. **[[concepts/revenue-recognition]]** — judgements around identification of distinct performance obligations, transaction price determination and the basis for recognising revenue over time; testing of Ind AS 115 implementation, internal controls, sample contracts and delivery records.
2. **[[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]]** — ₹17,059.72 lakhs of CWIP, comprising ₹2,341.49 lakhs for testing tools, instruments and manufacturing-facility expansion plus ₹14,718.23 lakhs for civil works; ₹7,512.88 lakhs assessed against the recognition criteria of Ind AS 16. The firm found no inappropriately capitalised costs.
3. **Inventories** — ₹76,000.75 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 (50.53% of current assets and 33.13% of total assets), with [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]] risk given customer-specific stock; procedures covered physical verification, ageing, provisioning, net realisable value testing and order-book analysis.
4. **[[concepts/related-party-transactions]]** — unsecured loans, sale and leasing of properties and investments in subsidiary share capital; assessed against Sections 177, 185, 186 and 188 of the Companies Act, 2013 and SEBI LODR 2015, with reliance on experts' reports.
5. **Accounting for business combination** — the acquisition of 100% of [[entities/idl_explosives]], accounted under [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]]. Because the acquired entity's identifiable liabilities exceeded its identifiable assets, provisional goodwill of ₹14,517.66 lakhs was recognised based on book values, pending purchase price allocation within the twelve-month measurement period.

### Consolidated financial statements

The consolidated report sets out four [[concepts/key-audit-matters]]:

1. **[[concepts/revenue-recognition]]** — the same judgements on distinct performance obligations, transaction price and over-time recognition, referenced to notes 23 and 48 of the consolidated financial statements; procedures included testing internal controls, reading sample contracts, evaluating management estimates against historical outturns, and applying audit techniques to establish whether revenue was recognised without corresponding receivables or cash in the general ledger.
2. **[[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]]** — consolidated CWIP of ₹19,629.40 lakhs, comprising ₹2,341.49 lakhs for testing tools, instruments and manufacturing-facility expansion plus ₹17,287.91 lakhs for civil works, of which ₹7,512.88 lakhs was assessed by management against the recognition criteria of [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]] under Ind AS 16 (refer note 4(b)). Procedures covered discussions with management on business plans, review of capital expenditure and controls, sample substantive testing of additions and deletions, scrutiny of general ledgers, review of physical verification reports, and compliance with the Companies Act, Income-tax, Customs duty and GST requirements. The firm's procedures did not identify any inappropriately capitalised costs.
3. **Inventories** — consolidated inventories of ₹79,528.92 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 (47.76% of total current assets and 33.58% of total assets, refer note 7), carrying [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]] risk because they are customer-specific. Procedures included assessing controls over physical verification, obsolescence identification, ageing and provisioning; reviewing management judgements on obsolescence at material procurement; sample testing of orders, purchases and cost accumulation in stock-in-process; comparing cost with estimated net realisable value using actual selling prices around and after year end; and analysis of the order book against work-in-progress and finished goods.
4. **Accounting for business combination — provisional [[concepts/goodwill]]** — the Group acquired 100% of the equity shareholding of IDL Explosives Limited through a share acquisition in which equity shares of the Company were allotted to IDL's shareholders, making IDL a wholly-owned step-down subsidiary accounted for under Ind AS 103 (refer note 47). At the acquisition date IDL's identifiable liabilities exceeded its identifiable assets while consideration transferred was positive, resulting in goodwill of ₹14,517.66 lakhs; the consolidated balance sheet carries goodwill of ₹14,519.55 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 (against ₹1.89 lakhs a year earlier). No fair valuation of identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed had been carried out at the balance sheet date, so the goodwill is provisional and based on book values pending completion of the purchase price allocation within the twelve-month measurement period permitted by paragraphs 45–50 of Ind AS 103. Procedures included evaluating the share agreement, confirming the accounting acquirer, agreeing provisional goodwill to IDL's acquisition-date balance sheet, testing acquisition journal entries, assessing adequacy of Ind AS 103 disclosures, evaluating management's future profitability assumptions against industry data and past forecasting accuracy, and obtaining written representation that no impairment indicators exist for the provisional goodwill.

Observations reported under the Order include:

- PPE and right-of-use asset registers are maintained, though the Company plans to improve the PPE register; physical verification runs on a rolling three-year cycle with no material discrepancies.
- Inventories physically verified with no material discrepancies; working capital limits above ₹5 crores sanctioned against the security of current assets, with bank returns agreeing to the books.
- Loans to subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries of ₹29,769.14 lakhs granted during the year and ₹31,555.75 lakhs outstanding at year end; loans to others of ₹699.55 lakhs. Interest overdue of ₹52.41 lakhs on a loan to a company, against which a provision of ₹58.71 lakhs was made.
- Income-tax disputes totalling ₹834.36 lakhs pending before the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) spanning AY 2015-16 to AY 2018-19.
- Share capital movements: 2,37,59,986 warrants converted into equity shares at a ₹113 premium (₹27,086.38 lakhs) and a preferential allotment of 2,70,42,894 equity shares (₹30,828.90 lakhs).
- No material fraud, no whistle-blower complaints, no cash losses in the year or the preceding year, and no resignation of statutory auditors during the year.
- CSR expenditure of ₹74.80 lakhs against an obligation of ₹105.43 lakhs, offset by prior-year excess; ₹6.54 lakhs carried forward with no unspent amount requiring transfer to a Schedule VII fund.
- The audit trail feature under Rule 11(g) operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions and was not tampered with, and was preserved per statutory record-retention requirements — although the report notes a qualification regarding book-keeping matters under section 143(3)(b) read with Rule 11(g). See [[concepts/audit-trail-rule-11g]]; the Company used Tally accounting software with an audit-trail (edit log) facility that operated throughout the year and was not tampered with.

The consolidated report also records that, based on the CARO reports issued by the firm for the Company and its subsidiaries to which CARO reporting applies, **there are no qualifications or adverse remarks** in those CARO reports. Other consolidated legal and regulatory reporting notes that pending litigations are disclosed (note 34), that the Group had no long-term contracts including derivative contracts with material foreseeable losses, that there was no delay in transferring amounts to the Investor Education and Protection Fund, and that no funds were routed through intermediaries or funding parties to ultimate beneficiaries.
