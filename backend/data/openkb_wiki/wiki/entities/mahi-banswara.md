# Mahi Banswara Nuclear Project

## What It Is

Mahi Banswara is a proposed **four-reactor nuclear power development** in India, each unit rated at **700 MW**, being advanced through an **NTPC–NPCIL partnership**. It is one of the flagship new-build projects intended to move India's civil nuclear programme toward the Government of India's stated target of **100 GW of nuclear capacity by 2047**.

The entity type is recorded as *other* because it is a project/development rather than a company, place, or product in the usual sense.

## Key Facts from the Q1 FY27 Earnings Call

As disclosed by Managing Director [[entities/srinivas-reddy]] on the [[summaries/2026_Aug_2026_Transcript]] call (30 July 2026):

- **Tenders have already been floated** for all four 700 MW reactors.
- MTAR would **not bid directly** — participation is expected **through the EPC vendors** who win the main contracts. MTAR is already qualified on comparable projects such as Kaiga 5 & 6.
- The opportunity is expected to be **materially larger than Kaiga 5 & 6**, because Kaiga involves two reactors while Mahi Banswara involves four.
- Order timing: management expects these orders to arrive **in the next financial year (FY28)**, not FY27, since the tender finalisation is itself a roughly one-year process.
- Management declined to size the TAM, framing the award process as still fluid.

## Why It Matters to MTAR

[[entities/mtar-technologies]] holds an unusually high wallet share in Indian reactor orders — cited on the call at **60–70%** of NPCIL / Department of Atomic Energy order value — so a four-reactor project represents a step-change rather than an incremental addition. It sits alongside:

- **Kaiga 5 & 6** — the source of the company's highest-ever nuclear order inflow, executing over 1–3.5 years.
- Reactor **refurbishment** work (~INR200+ crore segment opportunity, ~INR130–140 crore expected in the current quarter).
- The **PFBR** programme ([[entities/pfbr]]), which recently achieved criticality with MTAR supplying the majority of critical assemblies.

Together these underpin MTAR's claim that the [[concepts/civil-nuclear-power]] vertical has shifted to a more consistent, less cyclical growth trajectory, and support the [[concepts/order-book-and-revenue-visibility]] narrative of a INR5,000+ crore book with further inflows expected across sectors.
