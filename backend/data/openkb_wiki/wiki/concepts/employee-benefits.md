# Employee Benefits

Employee benefits are all forms of consideration given by an entity in exchange for service rendered by employees or for termination of employment. They include short-term benefits such as salaries, wages and social-security contributions; post-employment benefits such as gratuity and provident fund; other long-term benefits such as compensated absences; and termination benefits. For [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] for FY2025-26, employee benefits are presented in Note 18 / Note 17 (Provisions), Note 28 / Note 29 (Employee benefits expense), and Note 38 / Note 40 (Employee benefits) of the standalone and consolidated financial statements respectively, as summarized in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]]. The consolidated disclosures reflect the acquisition of subsidiaries during the year through business combinations, which materially increased the group's employee benefit obligations.

### Consolidated plan disclosures (Note 40 in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]])

The consolidated notes to the financial statements present a further set of gratuity disclosures for the insurer-managed plan. These include a separate set of financial assumptions:

| Assumption | FY2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Discount rate per annum | 0.94% | 6.75% |
| Expected rate of return per annum | 6.33% | 6.00% |
| Salary escalation rate per annum | 6.33% | 6.00% |

These consolidated assumptions differ from the standalone assumptions (notably the FY2026 discount rate), reflecting the basis used for the consolidated group valuation as disclosed. The demographic assumptions disclosed on the consolidated basis are the same as the standalone ones: retirement age of 60 years, mortality as per IALM 2012-14, average past service of 6 years, and withdrawal rate of 1% to 3% depending on age.

The consolidated plan assets are disaggregated by asset category on a weighted-average basis; the plan is funded 100.00% through funds managed by insurers and 0.00% through other assets in both FY2026 and FY2025. Amendments, curtailments and settlements of the plan are not applicable in the period.

Funding and asset-liability arrangements: the company uses a Group Gratuity Insurance Manager to administer the plan liability. The funds of the plan liability are invested by the Group Gratuity Insurance Manager, which pays benefits to members of the enterprise as per its rules; the manager is therefore exposed to liquidity risk of not being able to arrange benefit outgo due to cash liquidity problems. Where the Group Gratuity Insurance Manager purchases a group insurance policy from an insurance company, that insurance company makes payment of all plan benefits as part of the policy rules. All monetary amounts in the note are stated in Indian Rupees (in lakhs).

### Risk exposures

The gratuity plan exposes the company to the following risks:

- **Interest rate risk** — a fall in interest rates increases the ultimate cost of providing the benefit and the value of the liability.
- **Liquidity risk** — the risk of not being able to meet short-term benefit payouts, whether because of insufficient cash/cash equivalents or illiquid assets not being sold in time.
- **Salary escalation risk** — actual salary increases for plan participants differing from the assumed rate will affect the plan liability.
- **Demographic risk** — actual mortality and attrition experience turning out worse than the assumptions used in valuation.
- **Regulatory risk** — benefits are paid in accordance with the Gratuity Act 1972 (as amended from time to time); changes in its provisions (for example, a change in the benefit formula) could require higher plan benefit payouts.
- **Asset-liability mismatching or market risk** — the duration of the liability is longer than the duration of assets, exposing the company to volatility in or falls in interest rates.
- **Investment risk** — the probability or likelihood of losses relative to the expected return on any particular investment.

## Compensated absences

The company provides for accumulation of compensated absences by certain categories of employees. Employees can carry forward a portion of the recognized compensated absences and use them in future periods or receive cash in lieu thereof as per company policy. On a standalone basis, the total liability recorded was ₹40.19 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 and ₹19.34 lakhs as at 31 March 2025. On a consolidated basis, the combined non-current and current compensated absences liability was ₹143.13 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 (₹98.55 lakhs non-current plus ₹44.58 lakhs current) and ₹19.35 lakhs as at 31 March 2025, the increase reflecting the obligations assumed through business combinations. The consolidated note also separately records a total liability towards this obligation of ₹60.32 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 and ₹19.34 lakhs as at 31 March 2025.

## Accounting and disclosure linkages

Employee benefit obligations give rise to deferred tax effects: on a consolidated basis, the employee benefits (and other) temporary difference created a deferred tax asset of ₹39.93 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 (₹48.82 lakhs as at 31 March 2025), with ₹8.90 lakhs credited to other comprehensive income during the year. Remuneration paid to key managerial personnel is disclosed under related-party transactions, with total KMP compensation of ₹263.55 lakhs for FY2026 and ₹263.01 lakhs for FY2025. Employee benefit costs also affect profitability, capital management, and financial ratios.
