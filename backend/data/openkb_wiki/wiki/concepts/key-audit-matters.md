# Key Audit Matters

**Key Audit Matters (KAM)** are those matters that, in the independent auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period. They are selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance and are disclosed individually in the auditor's report, together with a description of how the audit addressed each one.

Because KAMs are important judgemental areas rather than deficiencies, their disclosure does not modify the audit opinion. They are the primary signal a reader gets about where estimation uncertainty, complexity and management judgement are concentrated in a set of financial statements.

## Regulatory basis

Under Standard on Auditing (SA) 701, *Communicating Key Audit Matters in the Independent Auditor's Report*, the auditor must:

- Determine, from the matters communicated with those charged with governance, those matters of most significance to the audit;
- Describe each matter and the auditor's response in sufficient context for the reader to understand why it was significant; and
- State explicitly that KAMs were addressed in the context of the audit as a whole and that no separate opinion is expressed on them.

KAMs are a communication device, not a qualification. A clean, unmodified opinion and a list of significant KAMs routinely coexist.

## Criteria for identifying KAMs

Typical triggers include:

- **Significant management judgement or estimation** in applying accounting policies;
- **Materiality and volume** of the amounts involved;
- **Complexity** of the underlying transactions, often across regulatory regimes;
- **High risk of material misstatement**, whether due to fraud or error; and
- **Pending measurement or valuation work** that could result in retrospective adjustment.

## Key Audit Matters at Apollo Micro Systems (FY 2025-26)

For the year ended 31 March 2026 the auditor, [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]], signed by [[entities/hima_bindu_sagala]], issued two separate reports on [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] — one on the standalone financial statements and one on the [[concepts/consolidated-financial-statements]] — both dated 18 May 2026 and both expressing an **unmodified** opinion. The two reports do not carry identical KAM sets: the consolidated audit adds the group's subsidiaries and drops the related party matter that appears in the standalone report.

### Standalone financial statements — five KAMs

|---|---|---|
| 1 | Revenue recognition | Judgements on distinct performance obligations, transaction price and the basis for recognising revenue over time |
| 2 | Capital work-in-progress | Rs 17,059.72 lakhs, of which Rs 7,512.88 lakhs required judgement against the recognition criteria of Ind AS 16 |
| 3 | Inventories | Rs 76,000.75 lakhs (50.53% of current assets; 33.13% of total assets) and risk of obsolescence |
| 4 | Related party transactions | Volume and materiality of loans, property sale/lease and subsidiary investments, plus Companies Act and SEBI LODR sensitivity |

### Consolidated financial statements — four KAMs

### 1. Revenue recognition

The application of the revenue standard involves judgements about identifying distinct performance obligations, determining the transaction price, and whether revenue is recognised at a point in time or over time (see [[concepts/revenue-recognition]]). Revenue from sale of goods is recognised when control transfers and no unfulfilled obligations remain, and only to the extent a significant reversal is highly probable not to occur. In both audits the auditor reviewed the Company's implementation of the revenue standard, tested controls over completeness, accuracy and timing of revenue, read sample contracts to assess contract classification and the timing of transfer of control, evaluated management's significant judgements against delivery records and prior-period outturns, and applied audit techniques to test whether revenue had been recognised without a corresponding receivable or cash entry in the general ledger.

### 2. Capital work-in-progress

The Group carries substantial capital work-in-progress (see [[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]]). On a standalone basis the balance was Rs 17,059.72 lakhs, comprising testing tools and instruments plus expansion of manufacturing facilities at Rs 2,341.49 lakhs and civil works at Rs 14,718.23 lakhs. On consolidation the balance rose to Rs 19,629.40 lakhs, with the same Rs 2,341.49 lakhs of testing tools and instruments but civil works of Rs 17,287.91 lakhs. In both sets management identified specific costs of Rs 7,512.88 lakhs and applied judgement as to whether they met the recognition criteria for [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]] under Ind AS 16. Because of the significance of the capital expenditure during the year, this was treated as a KAM. The auditor tested the design and operating effectiveness of controls over capital expenditure, held detailed discussions with management on business plans and technology relevance, substantively tested major additions and deletions on a sample basis, scrutinised general ledgers, reviewed physical verification reports for CWIP, checked compliance with the Companies Act, Income-tax, Customs and GST requirements for CAPEX accounting, and concluded that no costs had been inappropriately capitalised.

### 3. Inventories

Inventories are the single largest current asset and a persistent KAM (see [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]]). The standalone balance was Rs 76,000.75 lakhs, being 50.53% of current assets and 33.13% of total assets. On consolidation, inventories of Rs 79,528.92 lakhs represented 47.76% of total current assets and 33.58% of total assets. Because the inventories are specific to customers, there is a heightened risk of obsolescence, and the significant judgement and estimation involved in management's provisioning assessment led to the matter being flagged. Audit procedures covered the design and operating effectiveness of controls over physical verification, identification of obsolete and slow-moving inventory, ageing review and provisioning; review of management's obsolescence judgements taken at the time of material procurement; comparison of the cost of inventory with estimated net realisable value by reference to actual selling prices around and subsequent to year end; and a detailed analysis of the order book to test whether work-in-progress and finished goods were consistent with pending order values.

### 4. Related party transactions (standalone only)

Significant related party transactions during the year included unsecured loans, sale and leasing of properties, and investments in subsidiary share capital (see [[concepts/related-party-transactions]]). The auditor tested controls over capturing such transactions, evaluated arm's-length pricing policies and audit committee approval, agreed disclosed amounts to underlying documentation, and assessed compliance with Sections 177, 185, 186 and 188 of the Companies Act, 2013 and SEBI LODR requirements. This matter does not appear in the consolidated KAM set, where intra-group balances and transactions are eliminated on consolidation.

### 5. Accounting for business combination

During the year the Group acquired 100% of the equity of [[entities/idl_explosives]], which became a wholly-owned step-down subsidiary and was accounted for as a business combination under Ind AS 103 (see [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]] and [[concepts/goodwill]]). At the acquisition date the identifiable liabilities exceeded the identifiable assets, while the fair value of consideration transferred was positive, producing provisional goodwill of Rs 14,517.66 lakhs, being the excess of consideration transferred over the net identifiable assets (negative) acquired, in accordance with paragraph 32 of Ind AS 103. No fair valuation of identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed had been performed by the balance sheet date; management proposed to complete the purchase price allocation, including reassessment of goodwill, within the twelve-month measurement period permitted under paragraphs 45–50 of Ind AS 103. The goodwill recognised is therefore provisional and based on the book values of the target's assets and liabilities rather than their fair values. The auditor evaluated the terms of the share agreement, confirmed the accounting acquirer, agreed the provisional goodwill to the underlying acquisition-date balance sheet, tested the acquisition journal entries, evaluated the adequacy of Ind AS 103 disclosures, and assessed management's future profitability assumptions against industry data and past forecasting accuracy; a written representation confirmed management's view that no impairment indicators existed for the provisional goodwill at the reporting date.

## How this compares with the other audit communications

- The audit opinions on both the standalone and consolidated financial statements were **unmodified**, and the auditor separately reported — in a consolidated Annexure A that also considered the reports of the other statutory auditors of the Indian subsidiaries — that the Group has an adequate system of internal financial controls operating effectively. See [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]].
- The consolidated report was based on the Company's own audit together with the audit evidence obtained from other auditors, except in the case of one subsidiary which is not material.
- With respect to the consolidated financial statements, the auditor reported that the disclosure of the impact of pending litigations (note 34) was made, that the Group had no long-term contracts including derivative contracts with material foreseeable losses, that there was no delay in transferring amounts to the Investor Education and Protection Fund, and that no funds had been routed through intermediaries or funding parties for ultimate beneficiaries in a manner requiring adverse comment.
- The CARO reporting under the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 raised **no qualifications or adverse remarks** at consolidated level.
- In the standalone Annexure, non-KAM but notable matters included income-tax disputes pending before the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) for AY 2015-16 through AY 2018-19, interest overdue on a loan to a company with a provision of Rs 58.71 lakhs, and a shortfall in CSR expenditure carried forward. See [[concepts/corporate-social-responsibility]].
- The audit trail reporting under Rule 11(g) confirmed the feature operated throughout the year and was not tampered with, with the audit trail preserved as required for record retention, although the standalone report recorded an exception relating to books of account. See [[concepts/audit-trail-rule-11g]].

## Interpretation

The FY 2025-26 KAM set is revealing about the Company's growth phase, and the contrast between the two reports is instructive. Four of the five standalone matters — capital work-in-progress, inventories, related party loans and the IDL Explosives acquisition — are balance-sheet and funding matters. At consolidated level, the KAM list narrows to four because intragroup related party matters fall away on consolidation, while the balance-sheet orientation is reinforced: the consolidated balance sheet grew from Rs 1,29,948.86 lakhs to Rs 2,36,812.03 lakhs, with inventories alone nearly Rs 80,000 lakhs. Only one KAM in each report, revenue recognition, concerns the income statement. For readers, the KAM section therefore functions as a map of where the largest estimates sit and where retrospective adjustment risk is concentrated — most acutely in the provisional goodwill of Rs 14,517.66 lakhs, which could be revised significantly once the purchase price allocation is completed — rather than as a list of problems.
