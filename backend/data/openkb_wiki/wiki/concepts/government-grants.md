# Government Grants

## Definition

A government grant is assistance received from the government in the form of a transfer of resources (cash, assets, or concessions such as below-market interest rates) in return for past or future compliance with certain conditions relating to the operating activities of an entity. Under Indian Accounting Standards, grants are recognised only when there is reasonable assurance that the entity will comply with the attached conditions and that the grant will be received.

## Recognition and Measurement

The accounting policy of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] (Note 3.16 of the consolidated financial statements for FY 2025-26) sets out the following framework:

**Recognition conditions** — Grants and subsidies received from governments are recognised only when both of the following are satisfied:

- The Company will comply with the conditions attached to the grant.
- There is reasonable certainty that the grant will be received.

**Revenue-nature grants** — Government grants that are revenue in nature and are towards compensation for qualifying costs incurred by the Company are recognised as **other income** without deducting from the cost of the asset.

**Grants for additional depreciation** — Where a grant compensates for additional depreciation, it is recognised in the profit and loss account on a **systematic basis over the expected useful life** of the related asset, matching the grant income to the periods in which the related depreciation is charged.

## Concessional Government Loans

When loans or similar assistance are provided by governments or related institutions at an interest rate below the current applicable market rate, the benefit of the favourable interest is itself regarded as a government grant. The treatment is:

1. The loan or assistance is initially recognised and measured at **fair value**.
2. The government grant is measured as the **difference between the fair value of the loan and the proceeds received**.
3. The grant is recognised as **deferred income** and released to the statement of Profit & Loss proportionately over the loan tenure, presented within **other income**.
4. The loan is subsequently measured in accordance with the accounting policy applicable to **financial liabilities**.

This split treatment ensures the concessionary element is recognised as income over time rather than as an immediate gain, while the borrowing itself follows the normal amortised-cost model.

## Interaction with Other Policies

- **Property, plant and equipment** — Grants relating to assets are not netted against the cost of the asset; the asset is carried at its full cost consistent with the policy described in [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]].
- **Capital work in progress** — Where grants relate to assets still under construction, income recognition follows the asset's useful life once capitalisation is complete (see [[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]]).
- **Deferred tax** — Differences between the accounting and tax treatment of grants may give rise to temporary differences dealt with under [[concepts/deferred-tax]].
- **Provisions and contingent liabilities** — Where grant conditions create obligations that may require repayment, the exposure may need to be evaluated under the policy on [[concepts/provisions-and-contingent-liabilities]].
- **Revenue recognition** — Grant income is presented within "other income" rather than revenue from contracts with customers; see [[concepts/revenue-recognition]].

## Presentation

The policy is disclosed as part of the significant accounting policies in the [[concepts/consolidated-financial-statements]] of the annual report. Recognised grant income is presented within other income in the statement of profit and loss, while concessions on government loans are deferred and amortised over the loan tenure.
