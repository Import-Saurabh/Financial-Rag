# Business Responsibility & Sustainability Reporting (BRSR)

**Business Responsibility & Sustainability Reporting (BRSR)** is the ESG and responsible-business disclosure framework mandated by the [[entities/securities_and_exchange_board_of_india]] (SEBI) for listed companies in India. It requires an entity to report annually on how it integrates the nine **National Guidelines on Responsible Business Conduct (NGRBC)** principles into its governance, operations, workforce practices and stakeholder relationships. The BRSR is a distinct annexure within the annual report, separate from but complementary to the [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]] report.

## Regulatory Basis

BRSR disclosures are required under **Regulation 34(2)(f)** of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015. Companies listed on [[entities/bse_limited]] and [[entities/national_stock_exchange_of_india]] file the report for each financial year, typically on a standalone or consolidated reporting boundary.

## Structure of the Report

The BRSR is divided into three sections:

1. **Section A — General Disclosures:** corporate identity (CIN, incorporation year, registered office), stock exchange listings, paid-up capital, nature of business and NIC codes, number and location of plants/offices, markets served, employee and worker demographics, holding/subsidiary/associate structure, CSR applicability, and grievance-redressal mechanisms.
2. **Section B — Management and Process Disclosures:** whether Board-approved policies cover each of the nine NGRBC principles and their core elements, whether policies are translated into procedures, whether they extend to value chain partners, adopted certifications/standards, ESG commitments and targets, and the highest authority responsible for oversight.
3. **Section C — Principle-wise Performance Disclosure:** indicator-by-indicator reporting against Principles 1–9, split into **Essential Indicators** (mandatory) and **Leadership Indicators** (voluntary, for entities aspiring to higher responsibility maturity).

## The Nine NGRBC Principles

| Principle | Theme |
|---|---|
| P1 | Ethics, transparency and accountability |
| P2 | Sustainable and safe goods and services |
| P3 | Employee well-being, including value chains |
| P4 | Responsiveness to stakeholders |
| P5 | Human rights |
| P6 | Environmental protection |
| P7 | Responsible public and regulatory policy engagement |
| P8 | Inclusive growth and equitable development |
| P9 | Customer value and responsibility |

## Application: Apollo Micro Systems Limited, FY 2025-26

In [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part5]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]], [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] presents its BRSR as **Annexure-K** for the financial year ended 31 March 2026, prepared on a **standalone basis** with **no external assurance provider**.

### Key General Disclosures
- CIN L72200TG1997PLC026556; incorporated 1997; paid-up capital ₹35,72,92,440.
- Single business activity — manufacture of electronic components (NIC 2610) — accounting for 100% of turnover; three national plants and two offices serving 12 states, with **no exports**.
- Customers are predominantly government organisations including the [[entities/drdo]], Defence Public Sector Undertakings, shipyards and ordnance factories, alongside private firms.
- **434 permanent employees** (347 male / 87 female), including two differently abled employees, and no workers; women constitute 25% of the Board and 50% of Key Management Personnel.
- Group structure disclosed: [[entities/ananya_sip_rf_technologies_private_limited]] (51.01% subsidiary), [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] (76.00% subsidiary), [[entities/apollo_strategic_technologies_private_limited]] (51.00% step-down subsidiary) and [[entities/idl_explosives]] (100% step-down subsidiary).
- CSR applicable under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013, tied to the group's [[concepts/corporate-social-responsibility]] programmes.

### Management and Process
- Policies cover all nine NGRBC principles, are Board-approved and published on the company website; they do **not** extend to value chain partners.
- Certifications mapped to the principles: **ISO 45001:2018**, **ISO 14001:2015**, **AS 9100D** and DRDO registration.
- [[entities/karunakar_reddy_baddam]], Managing Director (DIN 00790139), is the director responsible for business responsibility, sustainability decision-making and oversight.
- No independent external assessment of policy working was carried out; compliance is instead scrutinised by internal, statutory and secretarial auditors. The BRSR's lack of external assurance thus stands in contrast to the audited financial statements in the same annual report, where [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] issued an unqualified opinion, including one on [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]].

### Principle-wise Findings
- **P1 (Ethics):** training on governance, risk, compliance and NGRBC delivered to the Board (4 programmes), KMPs (5) and employees (2) with 100% coverage; an anti-corruption/anti-bribery framework including an integrity pact; fines and penalties disclosed under the materiality threshold of Regulation 30, including an NSE penalty of ₹88,500 for a filing oversight and MCA compounding fees of ₹25,000 plus director-level amounts for a delayed Cost Audit Report. Accounts payable stood at 198 days (FY25: 181 days). Related-party exposure was concentrated in loans and advances (97.83% of total) and investments (100%), a dimension further discussed under [[concepts/related-party-transactions]].
- **P2 (Sustainable and safe goods):** the company reports no sustainable sourcing procedure and no product reclamation or Extended Producer Responsibility, citing the defence and security nature of its products — a notable gap versus the framework's intent.
- **P3 (Employee well-being):** 100% health and accident insurance coverage; PF coverage 95.62%, gratuity 94.23%, ESI 27.88%; well-being spend at 0.17% of revenue; 100% performance and career-development reviews; zero lost-time injuries, recordable injuries or fatalities; all plants assessed for health, safety and working conditions.
- **P4 (Stakeholder responsiveness):** key stakeholder groups identified as employees, investors, customers, suppliers/partners, government authorities and the community, each with defined communication channels and engagement frequency.
- **P5 (Human rights):** 100% of employees trained on human rights; all permanent employees paid above minimum wage; zero complaints of sexual harassment, discrimination, child labour, forced or involuntary labour, or wage issues. Grievance handling is reinforced by the company's whistle-blower policy and its POSH Internal Complaints Committee.
- **P6 (Environment):** total energy consumption of 7,97,528.15 kWh (FY 2024-25: 7,87,512.89 kWh), drawn entirely from non-renewable electricity, with no renewable or fuel consumption; energy intensity per rupee of turnover improved from 0.0001401 to 0.0001043. Water withdrawal and consumption were 3,589.01 kL, all from third-party sources, with nil discharge and no Zero Liquid Discharge mechanism, as the manufacturing process is stated to produce no significant liquid waste. NOx, SOx, particulate matter, persistent organic pollutants, VOCs and hazardous air pollutants are all reported as **Nil**, as are Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions; there is no GHG-reduction project. Plastic and e-waste generation is described as negligible and no other waste streams are reported, with the Company stating that its segment generates no waste or residue. No operations sit in or around ecologically sensitive areas, no Environmental Impact Assessments were undertaken, no sites are Designated Consumers under the PAT scheme, and the Company reports full compliance with India's environmental laws. No independent external assessment of these environmental disclosures was carried out.
- **P7 (Public policy):** the report states **two** affiliations with trade and industry chambers, then lists three — the PHD Chamber of Commerce and Industry, the [[entities/society_of_indian_defence_manufacturers]] (SIDM) and the ICC — an internal inconsistency in the disclosure. All are national in reach, and no corrective action was required on any anti-competitive conduct, as no adverse orders were received from regulatory authorities.
- **P8 (Inclusive growth):** no Social Impact Assessments or ongoing Rehabilitation & Resettlement projects were reported. Community grievances are received through a dedicated feedback form on the company website, supplemented by departmental contact email addresses. Inputs sourced directly from MSMEs/small producers represented 1.44% of total inputs by value, and inputs sourced directly from within India 86.36%. Wage cost was 100% urban, with no rural, semi-urban or metropolitan employment reported.
- **P9 (Consumer responsibility):** complaints and feedback are received through website contact details and resolved without delay; no consumer complaints were received or pending on data privacy, advertising, cyber-security, delivery of essential services, restrictive or unfair trade practices, and no voluntary or forced product recalls occurred. The Company states it has a framework covering cyber security and data-privacy risk — though the BRSR does not disclose a web-link to the policy — and reported nil data breaches, 0% involving personally identifiable information. These cyber and data-privacy disclosures sit alongside the [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]] sensitivities of a defence electronics business, and the standalone financial statements carry several [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] including inventory obsolescence and a business combination under [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]].

## Why BRSR Matters

- It converts ESG performance from voluntary narrative into a **structured, comparable, regulator-supervised disclosure**, complementing financial statements and the governance report.
- The distinction between **Essential** and **Leadership** indicators gives companies a maturity ladder, while the mandatory reporting of gaps (e.g. absence of sustainable sourcing, or reliance on a single third-party water source) improves accountability.
- For defence-sector firms, BRSR intersects with national-security constraints, since material issues such as data privacy and cyber security touch on classified information and [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].
- Because BRSR disclosures in this case carry no external assurance, their reliability depends on internal, statutory and secretarial audit processes — the same assurance stack that produces the financial-statement findings captured in [[concepts/audit-trail-rule-11g]] and [[concepts/key-audit-matters]].
- It links directly to adjacent compliance regimes: [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]], [[concepts/corporate-social-responsibility]], [[concepts/insider-trading]] and [[concepts/credit-rating]] disclosures published alongside it in the annual report.
