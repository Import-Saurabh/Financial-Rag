# Defence Manufacturing Licensing

Defence manufacturing licensing is the system of statutory approvals — principally **arms licences** and **industrial licences** granted under India's Arms Act and administered by the **Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT)** — that authorise a company to manufacture, assemble, integrate and proof-test defence weapons, platforms, sub-systems and energetic materials. Licensing is the formal gate through which a supplier of sub-systems becomes a legitimate **original equipment manufacturer (OEM)** of complete weapon systems.

For a private Indian company, the licence determines *what it is legally permitted to build*, and therefore shapes product strategy, capital investment, export eligibility and competitive positioning. Because the approvals are restricted in scope and granted sparingly, they function as a structural barrier to entry in the defence industrial base. The FY 2025-26 Annual Report of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] describes licensing as the defining strategic event of the year, framing 2025-26 as the year the company "moved decisively from subsystems to platforms."

## Licensing Categories Relevant to Defence

- **Arms Manufacturing Licence (DPIIT)** — authorises manufacture, assembly, integration and proof-testing of weapons and munitions. Can be time-limited or, exceptionally, granted for life.
- **Industrial Licence for Defence Items** — permits production of specified defence equipment, including unmanned systems, navigation and radar equipment. The report's FY26 licence covers UAVs, inertial navigation systems (INS), radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, ATGMs, decoys, chaffs and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions.
- **Explosives and Energetics Licences** — Industrial Licences for high explosives (TNT, RDX, HMX) and associated obligations. Following the acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]], the Group's regulatory footprint widened to cover the manufacture, storage and transportation of explosives under the **Explosives Act, 1884** and the **Explosives Rules, 2008**, alongside process-safety obligations such as permit-to-work controls, mock drills, statutory inspections and safety audits.

Licensing sits alongside related procedural levers such as the **Positive Indigenisation List** and indigenous-content requirements under the **Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP)**, which together convert policy intent into procurement mandates. Modernisation of licensing is therefore closely tied to [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] and the broader *Atmanirbhar Bharat* programme.

## Evidence from the Source Documents

### Lifetime Arms Manufacturing Licence (DPIIT)

- Granted by DPIIT under the Arms Act, **with no expiry** — a lifetime licence, described as "a remarkable achievement" that "opens the way for us to build full-fledged platforms for Indian and global markets."
- Authorises manufacture, assemble, integrate and proof-test **complete strategic weapon systems**: Missiles, Anti-Tank Guided Missiles (ATGMs), Torpedoes, Underwater Mines, Aerial Bombs, Rockets, Loitering Munitions, Chaffs, Flares and Decoys.
- In FY26 the company also secured a comprehensive **DPIIT licence** spanning **UAVs, INS systems, radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, ATGMs, decoys, chaffs and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions** (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]]).
- Runs alongside an existing licensed portfolio in **Unmanned Aerial Systems, Inertial Navigation Systems and Radar Equipment**.
- The scope connects directly to the company's product lines in [[concepts/underwater-warfare]] (including the approved production agency role for the Multi-Influence Ground Mine under DcPP with [[entities/drdo]]) and to [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]].

### Energetics Licensing via IDL Explosives

The **Industrial Licence for high explosives, including TNT and RDX**, was received by [[entities/idl_explosives]], the step-down subsidiary acquired through [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] (100% acquisition completed on 15 November 2025). This marked the Group's entry into specialised defence energetics and is analysed in [[concepts/defence-energetics]].

Combined with the arms licence, the report frames the position as "**Electronics, integration and energetics under one roof — this is the architecture of a complete weapon-system OEM, and very few companies globally possess it.**"

### Unmanned Systems Licensing

A DPIIT industrial licence was awarded to manufacture **Unmanned Aerial Vehicles and allied defence equipment**, positioning the company as a full-fledged OEM of Unmanned Aerial Systems and, prospectively, of Unmanned Ground Vehicles, Counter-Drone Systems and Autonomous Underwater Platforms. The FY26 comprehensive DPIIT licence extends this scope to cover UAVs explicitly alongside navigation, radar and munitions categories.

## Strategic Implications

1. **Vertical completeness.** Licensing across electronics, integration and energetics is what allows the company to claim end-to-end weapon-system delivery rather than component supply — the distinction between an OEM and a supplier developed in [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]].
2. **Barrier to entry.** Restricted and non-expiring approvals make the licensed position difficult to replicate, reinforcing the competitive moat in niches such as naval mines and torpedo homing.
3. **Export eligibility.** A lifetime arms licence is a prerequisite for converting domestic capability into international orders, supporting the ambition described in [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]] (the company received its first export order in FY26).
4. **Industrialisation capability.** A licence is necessary but not sufficient: the report stresses the parallel build-out of manufacturing and test infrastructure, quality systems and supply chains — see [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]] and [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].
5. **Manufacturing footprint.** Licensed capacity is being paired with physical expansion, including a new integrated facility at Hardware Park-II (described in the FY26 report as a 350,000 sq. ft. — and elsewhere a 600,000 sq. ft. — unit) to manufacture critical components including fuzes, mines and inertial navigation systems. The company operates two Hyderabad facilities at Mallapur and Adibatla, with additional land from [[entities/tsiic]] at Hardware Park Phase-II (Unit III) and a proposed greenfield high-energetics complex in Telangana.
6. **Compliance burden.** Widening licensing scope increases regulatory and process-safety obligations — notably under the Explosives Act, 1884 and allied rules — which the company manages through dedicated compliance functions, statutory audits, and an institutionalised safety framework extending across corporate offices and manufacturing sites.
