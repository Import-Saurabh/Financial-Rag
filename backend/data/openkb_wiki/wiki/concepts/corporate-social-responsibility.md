# Corporate Social Responsibility

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) forms an integral part of the corporate governance and community engagement framework for [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]]. It is detailed in [[summaries/2025_Financial_Year_2025_from_bse_part2]] and carried forward in the FY 2025-26 Annual Report ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part3]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part5]], the auditor's certification in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]], the standalone statutory note disclosures in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]], and the consolidated financial statement notes in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]]). Pursuant to Rule 8 of the Companies (Corporate Social Responsibility Policy) Rules, 2014, the company outlines its commitment to sustainable development, societal welfare, and environmental stewardship.

## CSR Policy and Governance

The company maintains a dedicated CSR policy and committee responsible for overseeing initiatives, ensuring statutory compliance, and monitoring fund allocations. The governance structure aligns with the National Guidelines on Responsible Business Conduct (NGRBC) principles concerning inclusive growth and community development. A Corporate Social Responsibility Committee sits alongside the Audit, Nomination and Remuneration, Stakeholder Relationship, Risk Management, Executive, and Securities Allotment Committees as part of the company's board-level committee structure, giving CSR direct oversight at the Board level.

For FY 2025-26 the CSR Committee comprised three members, who together held two meetings during the year and attended both:

* **[[entities/karunakar_reddy_baddam]]** — Chairman; Non-Independent, Managing Director
* **[[entities/raghupathy_goud_theegala]]** — Member; Independent Non-Executive Director
* **[[entities/kavya_gorla]]** — Member; Non-Independent, Non-Executive

The CSR Policy is published under the Investors section of the company's website.

## Statutory Applicability

CSR remains applicable to the company under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013, as confirmed in the BRSR for FY 2025-26 and re-affirmed in the financial statement notes, which state that the company is covered by Section 135 and that its obligation for the year was ₹105.43 Lakhs, with the required disclosures set out in the notes to the standalone financial statements (Note 37). The consolidated financial statements carry the same disclosure as Note 39, confirming that CSR is measured on the basis of 2% of average net profits. The disclosure anchors that applicability to two financial thresholds reported for the year:

* **Turnover:** ₹76,485.31 Lakhs
* **Net Worth:** ₹1,32,233.34 Lakhs

## Financial Computations and Obligations

For the financial year 2024–25, the statutory CSR obligation was calculated based on the average net profit of the preceding three financial years:

* **Average Net Profit:** ₹3,153.23 Lakhs
* **Total CSR Obligation (2%):** ₹63.06 Lakhs
* **Actual CSR Expenditure:** ₹100.00 Lakhs
* **Excess Amount for Set-off:** ₹36.94 Lakhs (available for set-off in succeeding financial years)

For FY 2025-26 the obligation was recomputed on the average PBT of the preceding three financial years (FY 2022-23: ₹2,944.02 Lakhs; FY 2023-24: ₹4,489.90 Lakhs; FY 2024-25: ₹8,380.42 Lakhs):

* **Average Net Profit:** ₹5,271.35 Lakhs
* **Two Percent of Average Net Profit:** ₹105.43 Lakhs
* **Amount Available for Set-off (from FY 2023-24):** ₹3.69 Lakhs
* **Total CSR Obligation for the Year:** ₹68.06 Lakhs
* **Actual CSR Expenditure:** ₹74.80 Lakhs
* **Excess Amount for Set-off into Succeeding Years:** ₹6.74 Lakhs

### Note 37 / Note 39 Reconciliation

The CSR note to the standalone financial statements (Note 37, in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]]) and the equivalent note to the consolidated financial statements (Note 39, in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]]) set out the movement in a format that ties the statutory obligation to the amount actually incurred and the cumulative set-off position. The two notes report identical figures:

* **Amount required to be spent during the year:** ₹105.43 Lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹63.06 Lakhs)
* **Amount incurred:** ₹74.80 Lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹100.00 Lakhs)
* **Excess/(shortfall) at the end of the year:** ₹(30.63) Lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹36.94 Lakhs)
* **Previous years' excess/(shortfall):** ₹37.17 Lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹0.23 Lakhs)
* **Total excess/(shortfall):** ₹6.54 Lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹37.17 Lakhs)

Both notes report no capital asset construction or acquisition through CSR funds, no CSR spending on ongoing projects, Nil related-party transactions in respect of CSR, and no provision made with respect to a liability incurred by entering into a contractual obligation. No reasons were reported for the shortfall, since the shortfall was fully absorbed by the brought-forward excess.

This position was independently corroborated in the auditor's report, where [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] (CA. [[entities/hima_bindu_sagala]]) confirmed in Annexure A to the Independent Auditor's Report that the company incurred CSR expenditure of ₹74.80 Lakhs against the current-year obligation of ₹105.43 Lakhs. The shortfall of ₹30.63 Lakhs was adjusted against the excess CSR expenditure of ₹37.17 Lakhs carried forward from previous years, leaving a balance of ₹6.54 Lakhs to be carried forward to the next year in accordance with Section 135 and its rules. Critically, the auditor confirmed that there is **no unspent amount** requiring transfer to a fund specified in Schedule VII of the Act, and that no unspent amount exists pursuant to ongoing projects under sub-section (6) of Section 135.

Administrative overheads, impact assessment, and the creation or acquisition of capital assets through CSR funds were all Nil. The company confirmed that no capital assets were created or acquired directly through CSR funds during the review periods.

## Key Project Allocations

### FY 2024–25

The total expenditure of ₹100.00 Lakhs was directed toward impactful societal and environmental projects executed through trusted partner organizations:

* **Environmental Sustainability:** Contributions amounting to ₹70 Lakhs were allocated toward environmental initiatives via [[entities/arunodaya_trust]].
* **Education Support:** Contributions amounting to ₹30 Lakhs were directed toward supporting and educating dropout students via [[entities/sarada_educational_society]].

### FY 2025–26

The company spent ₹74.80 Lakhs across six non-ongoing projects, implemented both directly and through registered implementing agencies:

* **Environmental Sustainability** (Telangana, Hyderabad): ₹20.00 Lakhs via [[entities/arunodaya_trust]] (CSR00012742)
* **Educating drop-out students from underprivileged backgrounds** (Telangana, Hyderabad): ₹20.00 Lakhs via [[entities/sarada_educational_society]] (CSR00034657)
* **Promoting Education** (Andhra Pradesh, Krishna): ₹20.00 Lakhs via Jagruti Foundation (CSR00022341)
* **Promoting Healthcare** (New Delhi, Lajpat Nagar): ₹1.00 Lakh via Care Guru Foundation (CSR00086062)
* **Promoting Education** (Odisha, Rourkela): ₹3.80 Lakhs, implemented directly
* **63rd National Roller Skating Championship 2025** (Andhra Pradesh, Visakhapatnam): ₹10.00 Lakhs via the Andhra Pradesh Roller Skating Association (CSR00072890)

In both the audited standalone note and the consolidated note, the full ₹74.80 Lakhs for FY 2025-26 is presented activity-wise under a single Schedule VII head — **Eradicating hunger, poverty and malnutrition, promoting health care including preventive health care** — with the same ₹100.00 Lakhs classified under that head for FY 2024-25. This activity view sits alongside the project-level breakdown above and reflects the primary Schedule VII purpose against which the spend was reported.

## Community as a Key Stakeholder

The BRSR for FY 2025-26 identifies the **Community** as one of the company's key stakeholder groups under Principle 4 (respecting and responding to stakeholder interests). While the community is not classified as a vulnerable and marginalised group, the company engages with it through both physical visits and digital channels on a frequent and need-based basis.

Engagement with the community is framed around giving back to society as a core tenet of the company: the CSR programmes are stated to target **Environmental Sustainability**, **Livelihood Enhancement** and **Women Empowerment**. The company also maintains a grievance redressal mechanism for communities — a dedicated feedback form and departmental contact email addresses available on the company website — and reported **Nil** complaints from communities in both FY 2025-26 and FY 2024-25.

Alongside the community, the key stakeholder groups identified are employees, investors, customers, suppliers and partners, and government authorities, each engaged through channels and frequencies appropriate to the relationship. Stakeholder engagement is described as an ongoing process, with feedback welcomed and management working to improve those relationships.

## Corporate Citizenship Approach (FY 2025-26)

In FY 2025-26 the company framed its corporate citizenship efforts around creating long-term value not only through business operations but also by contributing meaningfully to surrounding communities. The stated approach is to centre activities on areas where the company can create tangible value, with each initiative designed on the basis of on-ground needs and executed against clearly defined goals and measurable outcomes. This outcome-oriented framing complements the statutory obligation-and-set-off mechanics described above.

The BRSR statement by [[entities/karunakar_reddy_baddam]], Managing Director, also sets out the wider ESG commitments within which CSR sits — including employee welfare, diversity and inclusion, stronger governance, and supply chain sustainability.

## Inclusive Growth and Local Sourcing (Principle 8)

The BRSR's Principle 8 disclosures (inclusive growth and equitable development) sit alongside the CSR programme and reinforce the community-facing dimension of the company's responsibility framework. No Social Impact Assessments (SIA) of projects were undertaken during the year, and there are no ongoing Rehabilitation and Resettlement (R&R) projects. On the sourcing side, 1.44% of input material (by value) was sourced directly from MSMEs and small producers, and 86.36% was sourced directly from within India, tying CSR-adjacent inclusive-growth commitments to the wider supply chain. On job creation in smaller towns, 100% of the wage cost was attributable to urban locations, with no wages in rural, semi-urban or metropolitan categories.

## Related Practices

CSR sits within a broader governance and disclosure fabric that includes human rights commitments in the Code of Conduct, POSH compliance through an active Internal Committee, and reporting under the [[concepts/business-responsibility-and-sustainability-reporting]] framework, which also carries the company's community-facing social disclosures. As of 31 March 2026 the company is ranked within the top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation, making the BRSR applicable. Related governance practices and regulatory compliances are further detailed under [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]].

## Development

As the company scales — including through acquisitions such as [[entities/idl_explosives]] and greenfield expansion — its community engagement and environmental stewardship priorities are expected to extend to new plant locations and their surrounding communities. The company has explicitly tied its community engagement to environmental sustainability, livelihood enhancement and women empowerment, which suggests these three themes will continue to anchor future CSR allocations as the statutory obligation grows with profits. With the audited standalone note ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]]) and the consolidated note ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]]) reconciling the obligation, spend and set-off movement on the same figures, and with the auditor confirming that CSR amounts have been fully adjusted and no unspent balance requires transfer to a Schedule VII fund, the set-off mechanics have operated cleanly even as the absolute obligation has risen sharply alongside profits.
