# Counter-Drone and Counter-UAS Systems

Counter-drone (counter-UAS) capability is the layered defence against unmanned aerial systems — from small quadcopters to coordinated drone swarms — spanning detection, classification, tracking, decision-making and defeat. As unmanned platforms become cheaper, more autonomous and more widely deployed, counter-drone systems have moved from a niche electronic-warfare problem to a core requirement of modern air defence.

## The Layered Problem

The source document frames counter-UAS as "a layered problem of detect, identify, decide and defeat," and describes building a solution across the full chain rather than as a single product:

1. **Detection** — acoustic sensing, passive RF sensing and direction finding for early detection and classification of drone datalinks.
2. **Tracking and cueing** — radar and electro-optical cueing to localise and follow the threat.
3. **Soft-kill** — graduated RF jamming and denial to break the operator or GNSS link.
4. **Hard-kill** — integration with kinetic effectors, extending to directed energy.
5. **Command and control** — all sensors and effectors fused into a common command-and-control layer.

This "sensors, effectors and command-and-control" architecture is what distinguishes an integrated counter-drone system from a collection of standalone jammers or radars.

## Apollo Micro Systems' Positioning

As described in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]], Apollo Micro Systems is combining in-house strengths in RF systems, signal processing and ruggedised military electronics with technology partnerships under evaluation to accelerate time-to-market, with a clear intent to indigenise manufacture in India. The stated edge is delivering counter-drone capability as an integrated system built on indigenous hardware, qualified to military standards and scalable across configurations:

- **Vehicle-mounted** systems
- **Fixed-site** installations
- **Man-portable** variants

Lifecycle support — spares, obsolescence management and upgrades — is intended to be sustained within India, consistent with the broader [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] agenda.

Apollo's FY 2025-26 annual report adds a concrete delivery milestone on this front: vehicle-mounted counter-drone systems (VMCDS) are described as advancing, with **field trials scheduled for FY 2026-27**. This shifts counter-drone from a development concept to a near-term qualification and deployment programme, and it appears alongside the Company's stated ambition to ascend the value chain towards becoming a global Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM). See [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]].

## The Vehicle Mounted Counter Drone System

[[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part3]] gives the fullest technical description of the flagship product to date. The **Vehicle Mounted Counter Drone System** is presented as a mobile, integrated solution for the **detection, tracking, identification, and neutralisation of hostile unmanned aerial threats**. Key attributes described in the Directors' Report:

- **Mobility** — mounted on a high-mobility vehicle platform with genuine **on-the-move** capability, making it suited to convoy protection, forward-deployed formations and mobile air-defence gaps.
- **Layered sensor suite** — radar, RF detection and electro-optical sensors combined for broad-spectrum detection and classification.
- **Layered defeat** — both **soft-kill and hard-kill countermeasures**, deliberately combined for graduated, layered protection rather than a single defeat mechanism.
- **Modular, scalable architecture** — configurable to specific user requirements, echoing Apollo's stated product philosophy of modular, scalable technology building blocks.

The report frames the system as reflecting AMS's core strengths in **sensor fusion, real-time processing and system integration** — the engineering disciplines that underpin an integrated counter-UAS architecture. With drone warfare now central to the modern battlefield, the Company positions this product as addressing one of the most urgent operational needs of the Armed Forces.

The VMCDS sits within a widening indigenous technology portfolio being matured in FY 2025-26. Across R&D, the Company invested **₹7,253.39 lakhs** during the year, spanning homing systems for lightweight torpedoes, actuators for swarms and missiles, seeker signal-processing systems, moored-mine signal processing and other critical avionics — investments that the report says have begun converting into orders and deliveries, with meaningful revenue contribution expected from FY 2026-27 and FY 2027-28 onwards. The same report notes that Apollo was **awarded a Make-II project by the Indian Army** and is participating in further Tri-Services Make proposals, an institutional route through which counter-drone and related programmes can progress from development to procurement.

## Related Munitions and Effectors

The annual report describes a parallel munitions route: a compact **Medium Range Rocket** designed for vehicle-mounted launch systems, delivering aerodynamic stability and structural integrity for counter-drone missions, with a **ground-to-air launch system developed to target drones and drone swarms**. Its compact form factor is presented as a fit for mobile launchers — an example of kinetic counter-UAS at the munition level.

Counter-drone effectors also connect to [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]] — the report cites a Multi-Channel 55 kW Laser DEW as part of its technology portfolio, with transfer of technology received for laser-based DEW and electro-optical tracking systems. See [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]].

## Regulatory and Industrial Enablers

Counter-drone activity sits inside India's broader push for self-reliance in defence electronics and platform manufacture. Relevant enablers noted in the report include:

- The **DPIIT industrial licence** covering Unmanned Aerial Vehicles, inertial navigation systems, radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, anti-tank guided missiles, decoys, chaff and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions — alongside a lifetime arms manufacturing licence, and (through the IDL Explosives subsidiary) an industrial licence for high explosives. See [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]] and [[entities/idl_explosives]].
- **Transfer of technology** received for advanced electro-optical tracking systems, strengthening next-generation capability integration — the cueing and tracking half of the counter-drone chain. See [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]].
- Vehicle-mounted counter-drone systems listed among FY27 growth priorities alongside armament electronics, fire control systems, battle tanks, infantry combat vehicles and artillery platforms.
- The role of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] as a Tier-I integrator assembling sensors, effectors and C2 under one roof — see [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]].

## Market and Strategic Context

Counter-drone demand is being driven by the wider rearmament cycle documented in [[concepts/global-military-spending]]: global military spending reached $2,887 billion in 2025, with the fastest European growth since 1953. ReArm Europe directs up to €800 billion into defence, and the EU announced flagship projects including the **European Drone Defence Initiative**, the Eastern Flank Watch, the European Air Shield and the European Space Shield — a direct signal of institutional demand for drone detection and defeat capabilities. Defence self-reliance and technological escalation (AI, unmanned platforms, cyber, space-based defence) are named as structural growth drivers.

At home, the Directors' Report quantifies the momentum: India's indigenous defence production touched an all-time high of **₹1.78 lakh crore in FY 2025-26**, up 15.6% year-on-year and more than double the output of five years earlier, while the **defence budget for 2026-27 stands at ₹7.85 lakh crore**. The Government is targeting **₹3 lakh crore of annual defence production and ₹50,000 crore of exports by 2029**, with deepening indigenisation channelled through the Positive Indigenisation Lists, DAP 2020, the **DcPP framework**, Make-I/II and innovation frameworks such as iDEX and TDF — the report notes these have matured "from announcement to execution, with orders now flowing to the private sector at scale." Programmes such as **Mission DefSpace**, which has introduced 75 Defence Space Challenges across launch, satellite, communication/payload, ground and software systems, extend the same dual-use technology push into adjacent domains where counter-UAS sensors and command layers naturally sit. See [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part3]].

For the private sector, the export dimension matters: India's defence exports reached a record **₹38,424 crore in FY 2025-26**, registering growth of **62.66%** over the previous fiscal, with the private sector contributing a growing share and Indian defence equipment now reaching more than 80 countries. Counter-drone systems are a natural candidate for this external market — and Apollo's first-ever export order in FY 2025-26 marks an initial step onto that runway. See [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]].

A related structural theme is **co-development and co-production** with friendly foreign nations across precision munitions, guided systems, C4ISR and unmanned platforms — positioning Indian firms within global defence supply chains rather than merely alongside them — an opening that also applies to counter-drone sensors, effectors and command layers.

## Supply Chain and Industrialisation Considerations

Counter-UAS systems depend on a narrow set of qualified components — RF front-ends, signal-processing hardware, ruggedised enclosures, electro-optical sensors and militarily qualified electronics. Apollo's procurement approach relies on indigenisation-first sourcing, dual-sourcing for long-lead items and strategic inventory of critical semiconductors to protect delivery schedules. See [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].

Backward integration into **energetics** strengthens the hard-kill half of the counter-drone chain. The Group's acquisitions have built in-house access to qualified energetic materials — relevant to warheads, fuzing and the munition effectors used against drones — and FY 2025-26 saw a further, much larger step: a Share Purchase Agreement to acquire **41.33% of Premier Explosives Limited** for approximately ₹1,550 crore, with a mandatory open offer for a further 26% at ₹698 per share. The Board described the transaction as combining "complementary capabilities in defence systems and energetic materials," enabling operational efficiencies, enhanced R&D and broader participation in defence and space-related programmes. See [[concepts/defence-energetics]] and [[entities/premier_explosives_limited]].

The Company's capacity build-out supports this demand: a new 350,000 sq. ft. (described elsewhere in the report as a 600,000 sq. ft. greenfield project) integrated facility at Hardware Park-II is intended to expand manufacture of critical components including fuzes, mines and inertial navigation systems, and a broader greenfield expansion in Telangana is underway.

Where counter-drone technology is derived from DRDO or other transferred designs, deploying it requires the full industrialisation chain — design-for-manufacture, qualified test infrastructure (environmental stress screening, EMI/EMC, vibration, shock), a defence-grade supply chain and certification aligned to DGQA/DGAQA/DGNAI. See [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]] and [[entities/drdo]].
