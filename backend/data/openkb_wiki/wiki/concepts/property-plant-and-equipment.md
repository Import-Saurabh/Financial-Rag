# Property, Plant and Equipment

**Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE)** are tangible items held by an entity for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes, and that are expected to be used during more than one period. Under Ind AS 16, they are recognised at cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses.

## Measurement Basis

- **Initial recognition at cost**: purchase price including import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, plus directly attributable costs of bringing the asset to working condition and location for intended use (including eligible borrowing costs under Ind AS 23 and expected decommissioning costs).
- **Subsequent measurement**: cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses. The Company does not revalue PPE.
- **Freehold land** is carried at historical cost less impairment and is not depreciated.
- **Componentisation**: where significant parts of an item have different useful lives, they are accounted for as separate major components.
- **Spare parts and stand-by equipment** are classified as PPE when they meet the Ind AS 16 definition.
- **Subsequent expenditure** is capitalised only when future economic benefits are probable and cost is reliably measurable; otherwise repairs and maintenance are expensed.
- **Decommissioning/restoration costs** are included in initial cost where applicable.

These accounting policies (Ind AS 16 recognition and measurement) are applied consistently across the Group in the consolidated financial statements.

## Depreciation

Depreciation is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount (cost less estimated residual value) over the useful life, provided on a **straight-line basis** as prescribed in Schedule II to the Companies Act, 2013, or as per technical assessment.

Usable lives applied by the company (Apollo Micro Systems Limited):

| Class of asset | Useful life (years) |
|---|---|
| Buildings | 30 |
| Plant and machinery | 15 |
| Vehicles | 8 |
| Tools and spares | 15 |
| Furniture and fixtures | 10 |
| Computers | 3 |
| Office equipment | 5 |
| Electrical equipment | 10 |
| Intangible assets | 5 |

Depreciation on additions is on a pro-rata basis from the month of installation or acquisition; on disposals, pro-rata up to the date of disposal.

## Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP) and Capital Advances

- CWIP comprises tangible assets under construction or development not yet ready for intended use, carried at cost including directly attributable acquisition/construction costs, eligible borrowing costs under Ind AS 23, and other directly attributable expenses.
- No depreciation is charged until the asset is available for use; the accumulated cost is then transferred to the appropriate PPE category.
- Advances paid towards acquisition of PPE outstanding at the reporting date are disclosed as **capital advances** under other non-current assets (₹1,682.57 lakhs at 31 March 2026; ₹830.87 lakhs at 31 March 2025, on a consolidated basis).
- CWIP ageing schedules disclose the maturity profile of amounts capitalised.

Capital work-in-progress is treated as a [[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]] matter in its own right and is a recurring focus of audit attention.

## Impairment

The carrying amounts of non-financial assets (other than inventories and deferred tax assets) are reviewed at each reporting date for indications of impairment. Where indicated, the recoverable amount (higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell) is estimated for the asset or the cash-generating unit (CGU). An impairment loss is recognised when the recoverable amount falls below carrying amount, allocated first against any [[concepts/goodwill]] then pro-rata across other assets of the unit. This interacts closely with the measurement of [[concepts/financial-instruments]] (where fair value hierarchy applies) and, at group level, with [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]].

During the year ended 31 March 2026, property, plant and equipment were tested for impairment by the Company's technical executives; no indications of impairment were identified and no impairment loss was recorded.

### Capital Work-in-Progress as a Key Audit Matter

The auditor ([[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]]) identified the appropriateness of recording costs as CWIP as a **key audit matter** (see [[concepts/key-audit-matters]]). As at 31 March 2026 the Group recorded CWIP aggregating to **₹19,629.40 lakhs** (after additions and capitalisation), comprising:

- **₹2,341.49 lakhs** towards various testing tools and instruments and expansion of manufacturing facilities; and
- **₹17,287.91 lakhs** towards civil works.

Management applied judgement to assess whether certain specific costs of **₹7,512.88 lakhs** met the recognition criteria of Property, Plant and Equipment under Ind AS 16. The auditor's procedures included assessing the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls, substantive testing of major additions and deletions, testing source documentation for capital nature and approval, scrutiny of general ledgers, review of physical verification reports, and review of compliance with the Companies Act, Income Tax Act, Customs duty and GST Act. The auditor concluded that no costs had been inappropriately capitalised. This matter is disclosed in note 4(b) of the consolidated financial statements and is also discussed in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]].
