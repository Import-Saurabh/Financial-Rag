# Financial Instruments

## Definition

A financial instrument is any contract that gives rise to a financial asset of one entity and a financial liability or equity instrument of another entity. This concept covers the recognition, classification, measurement, and derecognition of financial instruments, impairment under the expected credit loss (ECL) model, fair value measurement, and the associated risk management disclosures.

## Source

Based on the accounting policies disclosed in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part7]], the standalone financial instrument and risk notes in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]], and the consolidated financial instrument, risk management and capital management notes (Notes 44–46) in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]] of the FY 2025-26 financial statements of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]].

## Initial Recognition and Measurement

- All financial assets are recognised initially at fair value plus, for assets not recorded at fair value through profit or loss, transaction costs attributable to acquisition.
- Regular way trades are recognised on the trade date.
- Trade receivables generally do not contain a significant financing component and are recognised at the transaction price per Ind AS 115.
- All financial liabilities are recognised initially at fair value, net of directly attributable transaction costs for loans, borrowings, and payables.

## Classification of Financial Assets

Financial assets are classified into two broad categories:

### Debt Instruments at Amortised Cost
Measured at amortised cost when both conditions are met:
- The asset is held within a business model whose objective is to collect contractual cash flows, and
- Contractual terms give rise on specified dates to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest (SPPI).

After initial measurement, these are measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate (EIR) method. This category generally applies to trade receivables.

### Financial Assets at FVTOCI (Debt Instruments)
Classified at FVTOCI when held within a business model whose objective is achieved by both collecting contractual cash flows and selling financial assets, and the SPPI test is met. Fair value changes are recognised in other comprehensive income (OCI) and reclassified to profit or loss on derecognition.

### FVTOCI – Equity Instruments
For equity investments not held for trading, the Company may make an irrevocable election at initial recognition to present fair value changes in OCI on an instrument-by-instrument basis. Unlike debt instruments, cumulative gains or losses are not recycled to profit or loss on derecognition. Dividends are recognised in profit or loss when the right to receive payment is established.

### Financial Assets at FVTPL
Classified as FVTPL if they do not meet the criteria for amortised cost or FVTOCI, or are designated at FVTPL to eliminate an accounting mismatch. Fair value changes are recognised in profit or loss.

## Business Model and SPPI Test

- **SPPI Test**: Performed at the instrument level to determine whether contractual cash flows represent solely payments of principal and interest, where "interest" is consideration for time value of money, credit risk, and other basic lending risks and costs plus a profit margin.
- **Business Model Assessment**: Determined at portfolio level, reflecting how groups of financial assets are collectively managed to achieve a specific business objective—collecting contractual cash flows, selling, or both.

## Investments in Subsidiaries and Associates

In the standalone financial statements, investments in subsidiaries and associates are carried at cost, less accumulated impairment losses. They are reviewed for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate the carrying amount may not be recoverable, in accordance with Ind AS 36. Related entities include [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]], [[entities/ananya_sip_rf_technologies_private_limited]], and [[entities/apollo_strategic_technologies_private_limited]]. As at 31 March 2026, investments carried in the balance sheet amounted to ₹133.65 lakhs (31 March 2025: ₹193.65 lakhs).

## Derecognition of Financial Assets

A financial asset is derecognised when:
- The rights to receive cash flows have expired, or
- The Company has transferred its rights to receive cash flows (including pass-through arrangements) and either transferred substantially all risks and rewards, or neither transferred nor retained substantially all risks and rewards but transferred control.

## Impairment of Financial Assets

The Company recognises loss allowances using the ECL model for financial assets not fair valued through profit or loss:
- **Simplified approach** for trade receivables, recognising lifetime ECLs at each reporting date from initial recognition.
- **12-month ECL** for all other financial assets, unless there has been a significant increase in credit risk, in which case lifetime ECL is used.
- A provision matrix based on historical default rates adjusted for forward-looking estimates is used as a practical expedient for trade receivables.
- ECL impairment loss (or reversal) is recognised under 'other expenses' in the statement of profit and loss.

For FY 2025-26, expected credit loss charges recognised in other expenses comprised ₹133.50 lakhs on trade receivables, ₹58.71 lakhs on loans, and ₹69.17 lakhs on trade advances (FY 2024-25: ₹10.31 lakhs, ₹37.99 lakhs, and ₹3.77 lakhs respectively).

## Financial Liabilities

### Loans and Borrowings
Initially recognised at fair value net of transaction costs, then subsequently measured at amortised cost using the EIR method. Gains and losses are recognised in profit or loss. See [[concepts/credit-rating]] and [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]].

### Financial Liabilities at FVTPL
Include liabilities held for trading and those designated at FVTPL, primarily derivative instruments. Gains or losses are recognised in the statement of profit and loss. The Company has not designated any financial liability as FVTPL.

### Derecognition
A financial liability is derecognised when the obligation is discharged, cancelled, or expires. Substantially modified terms result in derecognition of the original liability and recognition of a new liability, with the difference in carrying amounts recognised in profit or loss.

## Offsetting

Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amount reported when there is a currently legally enforceable right to set off and the Company intends to settle on a net basis or realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.

## Fair Value Hierarchy

Fair values are categorised into three levels:
- **Level 1**: Quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.
- **Level 2**: Observable inputs other than quoted prices, directly or indirectly derived.
- **Level 3**: Unobservable inputs not based on observable market data.

When inputs fall into different levels, the measurement is categorised in its entirety at the lowest level input significant to the measurement. Transfers between levels are recognised at the end of the reporting period in which the change occurs.

## Financial Risk Management

The Company's and Group's principal financial liabilities comprise loans and borrowings, trade and other payables, used to finance and support operations. Its principal financial assets include trade and other receivables, cash and cash equivalents, and refundable deposits. The senior management oversees risk management, and the Board reviews and agrees policies for each risk category. See [[concepts/financial-risk-management]].

### Market Risk
Market risk is the risk that the fair value of future cash flows of a financial instrument will fluctuate because of changes in market prices, comprising interest rate risk and other price risk (equity price risk and commodity/real estate risk).

- **Interest rate risk**: Arises primarily from long-term debt obligations with floating interest rates. The Company manages this through a balanced portfolio of fixed and variable rate loans and borrowings and does not enter into interest rate swaps. On a standalone basis, a ±1% change in interest rates affects profit before tax by approximately ₹485 lakhs for FY 2025-26 (FY 2024-25: ₹291 lakhs). On a consolidated basis, a +1% change reduces profit before tax by ₹532.41 lakhs and a −1% change increases it by ₹532.41 lakhs (FY 2024-25: ±₹330.76 lakhs).
- **Foreign currency risk**: Arises primarily from operating activities where revenue or expense is denominated in a foreign currency. The Company evaluates its exposure to exchange rate fluctuations on an ongoing basis.

### Credit Risk
Credit risk is the risk that a counterparty will not meet its obligations under a financial instrument or customer contract, leading to a financial loss. It arises principally from operating activities (primarily trade receivables) and investing activities (deposits with banks and financial institutions). Credit risk is influenced mainly by the individual characteristics of each customer, and also by the demographics of the customer, including the default risk of the industry and country in which the customer operates. The Group has not provided any provision for expected credit losses on trade receivables.

Credit risk is controlled by analysing credit limits and the creditworthiness of customers on a continuous basis, and collections are monitored continuously. The Group establishes an allowance for credit losses based on past and recent collection trends.

## Capital Management

Capital includes issued capital and all other equity reserves, and the primary objective is to maximise shareholder value. The Company manages its capital structure and makes adjustments in light of changes in the economic environment and financial covenant requirements. For the debt-to-equity ratio, debt is considered as long-term and short-term borrowings. See [[concepts/capital-management]] and [[concepts/financial-ratios]].

### Standalone (Note 45)
As at 31 March 2026, total equity attributable to equity shareholders was ₹1,32,233.36 lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹60,861.89 lakhs), representing 73.2% of total capital, with total borrowings of ₹48,505 lakhs (26.8%). The debt-to-equity ratio improved to 0.73 from 1.07, primarily due to an increase in equity during the year.

### Consolidated (Note 46)
As at 31 March 2026, total equity attributable to the equity shareholders was ₹1,30,822.03 lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹60,756.89 lakhs), representing 71.1% of total capital (FY 2024-25: 64.7%). Total borrowings were ₹53,241.21 lakhs (28.9% of capital; FY 2024-25: 35.3%), comprising long-term borrowings including current maturities of ₹16,312.56 lakhs and short-term borrowings of ₹36,928.65 lakhs. Total capital (equity and borrowings) stood at ₹1,84,063.25 lakhs (FY 2024-25: ₹93,833.19 lakhs).
