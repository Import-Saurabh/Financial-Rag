# Internal Financial Controls Over Financial Reporting

Internal Financial Controls with reference to financial statements (IFC-FR) are the policies and procedures a company maintains to provide reasonable assurance about the reliability of its financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles. In India, the concept is codified in **Section 143(3)(i) of the Companies Act, 2013**, which requires the statutory auditor to report on whether the company has adequate internal financial controls with reference to its financial statements in place, and on the operating effectiveness of such controls.

## Definition under the Act

ICAI's Guidance Note on Audit of Internal Financial Controls Over Financial Reporting sets out three core objectives of IFC-FR:

1. **Record maintenance** — records that, in reasonable detail, accurately and fairly reflect transactions and dispositions of assets.
2. **Authorisation and recording** — transactions are recorded as necessary to permit preparation of financial statements in accordance with GAAP, and receipts and expenditures are made only in accordance with authorisations of management and directors.
3. **Safeguarding assets** — reasonable assurance regarding prevention or timely detection of unauthorised acquisition, use or disposition of assets that could materially affect the financial statements.

## Inherent Limitations

Because of the inherent limitations of any control system — including the possibility of collusion, forgery, improper management override of controls, and human error — material misstatements due to error or fraud may occur and not be detected. Any evaluation of IFC-FR also speaks only to the period assessed: controls may become inadequate due to changes in conditions, or the degree of compliance with policies and procedures may deteriorate.

## Evidence from the FY2025-26 Annual Report

For [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], the audit of IFC-FR was carried out by [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] (Chartered Accountants; CA. Hima Bindu Sagala, Membership No. 231056, ICAI FRN 011410S) and reported in **Annexure 'B'** to the Independent Auditor's Report dated 18 May 2026, for the year ended 31 March 2026. See [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]].

Key points from that report:

- **Management's responsibility**: the Board of Directors is responsible for establishing and maintaining internal financial controls based on criteria established by the Company, considering the essential components of internal control stated in the ICAI Guidance Note.
- **Auditor's responsibility**: to express an opinion on the design and operating effectiveness of IFC-FR, based on an audit conducted in accordance with the Guidance Note and the Standards on Auditing issued by ICAI and deemed prescribed under Section 143(10) of the Act.
- **Opinion delivered**: the Company has, **in all material respects, an adequate internal financial controls system with reference to standalone financial statements, and such controls were operating effectively as at 31 March 2026**. This is an unqualified (clean) IFC opinion.
- **Scope and procedures**: the audit involved understanding IFC over financial reporting, assessing the risk that a material weakness exists, and testing and evaluating design and operating effectiveness based on assessed risk.

### Supporting evidence in the notes to the standalone financial statements

The Notes to the standalone financial statements (Notes 18–53) provide further corroboration that specific control activities operated during FY2025-26. See [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]].

- **Accounting software and audit trail (Note 51)**: the Company maintains its books of account in Tally accounting software, which has an audit trail (edit log) facility that **operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions**, with no instance of the audit trail feature being tampered with. Audit trails of prior years were preserved per statutory record-retention requirements to the extent enabled. This is the operational counterpart to the [[concepts/audit-trail-rule-11g]] requirement, and needs to be read alongside the qualification on books of account in the main auditor's report.
- **Reconciliation of bank submissions to books (Note 49.2(9))**: the Company borrows from banks and financial institutions against security of current assets, and the quarterly returns / statements of current assets filed with those lenders are **in agreement with the books of accounts** — a direct test of the reliability of the underlying accounting records.
- **Management's liability judgements**: provisions, contingent liabilities and commitments (Note 33) reflect the Company's assessment that pending litigations, guarantees (₹4,832.63 lakhs) and contractual commitments (₹1,211.05 lakhs for the Shamshabad factory) are appropriately provided for or disclosed, and that the likelihood of material obligation under guarantees given is remote. These judgements depend on controls over legal, treasury and project authorisation.
- **Regulatory representations (Note 49.2)**: management has represented that title deeds are held in the Company's name except for TSIIC land and a Chandigarh flat pending registration; that the Company is not a wilful defaulter; that there is no benami property, no struck-off-company relationship, no charges pending registration beyond the statutory period, no unrecorded income surrendered in tax assessments, and no crypto / virtual currency transactions. Each representation is an assertion about the completeness and accuracy of the books.
- **Tax and statutory reconciliations (Notes 19, 32, 39)**: the deferred tax movement, current tax liability, effective tax rate reconciliation, related-party disclosures and MSME interest computations form part of the disclosure control framework that supports the IFC-FR conclusion.

## Relationship to the Financial Statement Audit

The IFC opinion is distinct from, but complementary to, the opinion on the financial statements themselves. In the same report, the auditor issued an unqualified true-and-fair opinion while noting **[[concepts/key-audit-matters]]** — revenue recognition, capital work-in-progress, inventories, related-party transactions and the [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]] accounting for the [[entities/idl_explosives]] acquisition — in each case describing the internal controls tested rather than control failures.

Related reporting obligations that interact with IFC-FR include:

- **[[concepts/audit-trail-rule-11g]]** — the audit trail (edit log) requirement under Rule 11(g) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014, which operated throughout FY2025-26 for all relevant transactions recorded in the software and was not found tampered with, though the main report carries a qualification on books of account in this regard.
- **CARO 2020 (Annexure A)** — reporting on internal audit systems, which the auditor found commensurate with the size and nature of the business, and whose reports were considered in determining the nature, timing and extent of audit procedures.
- **[[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]]** — audit committee oversight of the financial reporting process, which the Board is stated to exercise.
- **Risk and capital management (Notes 44–45)** — the Company's policies for market, credit and liquidity risk, and its [[concepts/financial-ratios]] and debt-to-equity monitoring, are an extension of the control environment into [[concepts/financial-instruments]] and treasury operations.

## Practical Significance

The IFC-FR framework matters because it links governance to numbers: a clean IFC opinion signals that the reported figures were produced by a controlled process, while a qualified or adverse IFC opinion signals that the reliability of the financial statements themselves may be compromised. For a defence-electronics manufacturer with large inventories, ongoing capital works and significant related-party lending, IFC over inventory valuation, capitalisation judgements and related-party authorisation is especially material.

The FY2025-26 disclosures reinforce this: with revenue recognition governed by the point-in-time performance obligations in [[concepts/revenue-recognition]], lease accounting under Ind AS 116 ([[concepts/leases]]), deferred tax estimation ([[concepts/deferred-tax]]), and a growing web of related-party loans to subsidiaries such as [[entities/ananya_sip_rf_technologies_private_limited]], [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]], [[entities/apollo_strategic_technologies_private_limited]] and [[entities/idl_explosives]], the design and operating effectiveness of IFC-FR is tested across nearly every significant accounting estimate and judgement.
