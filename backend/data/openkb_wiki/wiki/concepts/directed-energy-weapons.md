# Directed Energy Weapons

Directed Energy Weapons (DEWs) are weapon systems that damage or disable a target using concentrated electromagnetic energy — most commonly lasers — rather than kinetic projectiles. In the defence context, they are attractive because they offer a near-unlimited "magazine," low cost per engagement, precise targeting with minimal collateral damage, and the ability to engage small, fast, low-cost threats such as drones and drone swarms that would be uneconomical to intercept with conventional munitions.

## Apollo Micro Systems' Position

According to [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]], DEWs sit within the Radio Frequency Systems technology pillar of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], one of the company's three stated focus areas alongside Artificial Intelligence and Inertial Navigation:

- The report describes "Multi-Channel 55 kW Laser DEW" as part of the portfolio covering "the nerve system of every modern combat platform," grouped with RF tracking, electronic warfare sensors and advanced communication suites.
- Apollo received a **Transfer of Technology for directed energy weapons**, explicitly covering **laser-based DEW** and **advanced electro-optical tracking systems**. This is a significant step because it moves the company from supplying components into the DEW domain itself.
- DEWs are listed among the vehicle-mounted product families in Apollo's diversified portfolio, alongside Missiles, Underwater Weapons, Explosives, Drones, Counter-Drone Systems, Rockets, Aerial Bombs and Ammunitions.
- The company frames these as part of positioning "at the forefront" of next-generation warfare defined by autonomy, artificial intelligence, network-centric operations, precision and electronic dominance.

[[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]] records the **Transfer of Technology for Directed Energy Weapons (DEWs), including laser-based variants** as a formal FY 2025-26 milestone for the company, confirming that the capability was received during the year. The same document pairs this with a Transfer of Technology for advanced electro-optical tracking systems, "strengthening next-generation capability integration" — the cueing layer on which a laser effector depends. It also confirms that **vehicle-mounted counter-drone systems (VMCDS) are advancing, with field trials scheduled for FY 2026-27**, providing the first concrete route to a fielded DEW-adjacent product.

Because DEW was received as a ToT rather than developed in-house, the milestone should be read alongside Apollo's broader strategic attributes: a 41-year indigenous engineering base, more than 700 technologies developed and over 150 active defence programmes, and a fully integrated capability spanning design, development, testing and scale manufacturing — the profile required to convert a received design into a producible system.

## Relationship to Counter-Drone Capability

The most immediate operational application described is in [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]]. Counter-UAS is framed as a layered "detect, identify, decide and defeat" problem:

1. **Detection and classification** — acoustic sensing, passive RF sensing and direction finding for drone datalinks.
2. **Cueing** — radar and electro-optical tracking, the latter directly linked to the electro-optical tracking systems received by Apollo.
3. **Soft-kill** — graduated RF jamming and denial.
4. **Hard-kill integration** — extending to kinetic or directed energy defeat effectors, all fused into a common command-and-control layer.

The report positions Apollo's edge as delivering counter-drone capability as an integrated system — sensors, effectors and command-and-control — built on indigenous hardware, qualified to military standards and scalable from vehicle-mounted and fixed-site configurations to man-portable variants, supportable across the full lifecycle within India. The vehicle-mounted (VMCDS) configuration is the specific variant now moving into field trials.

## Technology Absorption and Industrialisation

DEW is a clear example of the [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]] model Apollo describes: converting a received [[entities/drdo]] or partner design into a deployable, inductable system requires design-for-manufacture, qualified manufacturing and test infrastructure (environmental stress screening, EMI/EMC, vibration, shock and climatic testing), a defence-grade supply chain, quality and certification systems aligned to defence quality assurance agencies, and lifecycle support including spares, obsolescence management and mid-life upgrades.

The part 2 report frames execution and technology risk on "complex, qualification-driven systems" as a managed risk, addressed through stage-gated development, rigorous testing and the 41-year indigenous engineering base — precisely the discipline DEW industrialisation demands. It also notes that the DPIIT licence secured by the company covers decoys, chaffs and flares alongside UAVs, INS systems, radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, ATGMs, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions, broadening the licensed effector portfolio in which a DEW capability can be positioned.

Because directed energy systems depend on precision optics, power management, beam control and ruggedised electronics, they also draw on the same engineering disciplines the company applies to its other programmes — see [[concepts/inertial-navigation-systems]] for the guidance and stabilisation technologies, and [[concepts/underwater-warfare]] for its other high-reliability qualification environments.

## Strategic Context

The report places DEW within the broader global trend of **technological escalation**, where massive investments are being directed towards artificial intelligence, unmanned platforms and drones, cyber warfare capabilities and space-based defence systems. This sits against a backdrop of rising [[concepts/global-military-spending]] and national pushes for [[concepts/defense_indigenization]], including India's Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat initiatives.

Demand-side support comes from the government's own programmes. [[concepts/government-defence-funding-schemes]] such as the Technology Development Fund and Mission DefSpace are explicitly aimed at dual-use and defence-space technologies, and the stated opportunity set in the annual report includes "expanding demand for space-grade electronics and dual-use technologies under Mission DefSpace and related programmes." The company also targets entry into major heavy defence domains — armament electronics, fire control systems, battle tanks, infantry combat vehicles and artillery platforms — a portfolio in which directed energy effectors are one element.

The DPIIT licence architecture described in [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]] and the company's growing platform portfolio position DEW as one of the capability areas feeding Apollo's ambition to become a global OEM, with export relevance captured under [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]]. The threat side of the same framing — rapid technology evolution requiring continuous R&D investment to avoid obsolescence — applies with particular force to DEW, where beam power, optics and thermal management advance quickly.

## Key Takeaways

- Apollo has secured Transfer of Technology for laser-based DEW and advanced electro-optical tracking systems, formally recorded as an FY 2025-26 milestone in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]].
- A Multi-Channel 55 kW Laser DEW is described as part of the company's RF and sensor technology pillar.
- The electro-optical tracking ToT provides the cueing layer; DEW serves primarily as the hard-kill layer of an integrated counter-drone architecture, fused with detection, cueing and soft-kill jamming under unified command-and-control.
- Vehicle-mounted counter-drone systems are advancing toward field trials in FY 2026-27, the nearest-term path to fielding.
- Capability depends on the same ToT-absorption and industrial depth — testing, certification, supply chain and lifecycle support — that underpins Apollo's other weapon electronics programmes.
- DEW aligns with global rearmament and technological escalation trends, with India's indigenisation and export ambitions, and with government funding schemes such as the Technology Development Fund and Mission DefSpace.
