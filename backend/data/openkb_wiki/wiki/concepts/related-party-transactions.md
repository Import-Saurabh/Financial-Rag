# Related Party Transactions

## Definition
A **related party transaction (RPT)** is any transaction between a company and a party connected to it — such as its promoters, directors, key managerial personnel, subsidiaries, associates, joint ventures, or their relatives and entities in which they have an interest. Because related parties can influence terms, RPTs are subject to heightened disclosure, approval and audit-committee oversight requirements.

In India, the framework is set by two overlapping regimes:

- **Companies Act, 2013** — Section 188 and the related rules, which require board or shareholder approval for specified transactions that are not in the ordinary course of business or not at arm's length. Sections 177, 185 and 186 separately govern audit-committee oversight, loans to directors/interested parties, and loans and investments generally.
- **SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015** — Regulation 23, which requires a listed entity to formulate an RPT policy, seek prior approval of the Audit Committee, and make periodic disclosures. Regulation 46(2)(b)–(i) requires the policy to be published on the company's website.

Ind AS 24 *Related Party Disclosures* supplies the accounting-standard definition. The Companies Act/Schedule III instructions to Division II require that, for RPT note disclosures, related parties are identified using the Ind AS 24 meaning.

Two concepts sit at the centre of the RPT test:

- **Ordinary course of business** — the transaction is a normal, recurring part of what the company does.
- **Arm's length basis** — the transaction is priced and conducted as it would be between two unrelated parties.

## RPTs at Apollo Micro Systems Limited
[[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] reports on RPTs in its Corporate Governance Report, in its Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report and, for FY 2025-26, in the notes to the standalone financial statements (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part5]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part7]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]]). The **consolidated financial statements mirror these disclosures** in their own related-party note (Note 35), which lists the group's subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries alongside its key managerial personnel and entities in which a director has significant influence (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]]). The key governance disclosures are:

- **No materially significant related party transactions** and no pecuniary transactions that could potentially conflict with the interests of the company at large.
- **All transactions** with related parties as defined under the Companies Act, 2013 and the SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015 during the financial year were in the **ordinary course of business and on an arm's length basis**.
- **No transaction** was in conflict with the company's interest, and the company did **not** enter into any transaction with a person or entity from the promoter/promoter group holding 10% or more of the company's shareholding.
- The **Audit Committee reviews RPTs periodically**.

At the annual-report level, the statutory auditor [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] confirmed that the Company is in compliance with **Sections 177 and 188 of the Companies Act, 2013** for applicable related-party transactions, and that the required Ind AS disclosures (note 34 of the standalone financial statements, note 35 of the consolidated financial statements) have been made.

## RPT Policy
Apollo Micro Systems has formulated a **Related Party Transaction Policy** to ensure proper approval and reporting of transactions between the company and its related parties. The policy was considered and approved by the Board, is uploaded on the company's website, and is reviewed and amended by the Audit Committee and Board from time to time. This satisfies the website-disclosure obligation under Regulation 46(2)(b)–(i).

## Quantitative Disclosures (BRSR, Principle 1)
The BRSR open-ness-of-business disclosure quantifies the **share of RPTs**:

| Metric | Current FY | Previous FY |
|---|---|---|
| Purchases from related parties / total purchases | Nil | 0.04% |
| Sales to related parties / total sales | Nil | Nil |
| Loans & advances to related parties / total loans & advances | 97.83% | 67.82% |
| Investments in related parties / total investments made | 100% | 100% |

Two observations follow from these figures. First, the concentration of **loans and advances** and **investments** in related parties is very high — expected for a holding company funding its subsidiaries, but a figure that keeps RPT governance material even when no single transaction is flagged as "materially significant." Second, purchases and sales with related parties are nil in the current year, confirming the arm's-length, ordinary-course character of trading activity described in the Corporate Governance Report.

The BRSR also notes that **no external agency assessment** of policy working was carried out, but that policies relating to statutory compliance — including RPT compliance — are scrutinised by internal auditors, statutory auditors and secretarial auditors.

## Audit and Regulatory Scrutiny
RPTs were elevated to a **Key Audit Matter** in the FY 2025-26 standalone audit (see [[concepts/key-audit-matters]]), covering unsecured loans, sale and leasing of properties, and investments in the share capital of subsidiaries — a designation driven by transaction volume and materiality, together with the regulatory sensitivity of RPTs under the Companies Act, 2013 and the SEBI (LODR) Regulations.

The auditor's stated procedures were to:

- Understand, evaluate the design of and test the operating effectiveness of controls over capturing related-party transactions and management's process for ensuring all transactions and balances are disclosed.
- Understand the Company's policies and procedures for evaluating **arm's-length pricing** and the approval process by the Audit Committee and Board.
- Agree disclosed amounts to underlying documentation, read the relevant agreements, and evaluate management's arm's-length assessment on a sample basis.
- Assess compliance with Sections 177, 185, 186 and 188 of the Act and rely on experts' reports for SEBI (LODR) 2015 compliance.
- Evaluate disclosures against statutory information, books, records and other documents obtained during the audit.

The same audit also tested the internal control environment more broadly, with an unqualified opinion on [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]].

## Governance Linkages
RPT oversight is one pillar of the broader [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]] framework at the company. It connects to:

- The **whistle-blower policy** — the vigil mechanism provides a channel for reporting abuses, including RPT-related misconduct or conflicts of interest. No whistle-blower complaints were received during FY 2025-26.
- [[concepts/insider-trading]] — the Code of Internal Procedures and Conduct to Regulate, Monitor and Report Trading by Insiders governs dealings by insiders, who are also the related parties most likely to appear in RPT disclosures.
- [[concepts/business-responsibility-and-sustainability-reporting]] — BRSR Principle 1 (ethics, transparency and accountability) requires the RPT concentration metrics disclosed above.

## Points to Watch
The company reports zero complaints relating to conflicts of interest of Directors or KMPs in both FY 2025-26 and FY 2024-25, and no disciplinary action against Directors, KMPs, employees or workers for bribery or corruption. The only penalties in the recent past were unrelated to RPTs: a Regulation 33 filing lapse penalised by [[entities/national_stock_exchange_of_india]] and a Companies Act Section 148(6) compounding fee for a delayed cost audit report.

Items worth monitoring in future filings include the ageing profile of the ₹31,555.75 lakhs of subsidiary loans and the size of the IDL Explosives exposure (₹18,757.61 lakhs, now the single largest related-party loan), the documentation quality flagged by the auditor, the treatment of the ₹52.41 lakhs overdue interest, whether the guarantee-commission income pattern resumes after the corporate guarantee was released on repayment, and the eventual arm's-length validation of the IDL Explosives acquisition once the purchase price allocation is completed. The related-party notes also show **dividends and rent flows to KMPs and their relatives** alongside the funding of subsidiaries — a mix of shareholder returns and property transactions that the Audit Committee continues to review under the arm's-length test. The consolidated statements add the small **loan from a Director** (₹36.21 lakhs) and the **loan payable to a Director** (₹3.21 lakhs) to the list of balances to track.
