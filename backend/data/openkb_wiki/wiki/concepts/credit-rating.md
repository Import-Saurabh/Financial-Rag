# Credit Rating

A **credit rating** is an independent assessment, issued by a credit rating agency, of an entity's ability to meet its financial obligations. Ratings are assigned separately for long-term and short-term borrowings and are typically accompanied by an outlook (Stable, Positive, Negative) that signals the likely direction of the rating over the medium term. Ratings matter because they affect borrowing cost, lender confidence, counterparty due diligence, and the ease of raising both debt and equity capital.

## Scale and Terminology

- **Long-term ratings** (typically instruments maturing beyond one year) are expressed on a letter scale such as AAA, AA, A, BBB, and so on, often with modifiers (+/-) and a suffix indicating the instrument type.
- **Short-term ratings** (working-capital facilities, commercial paper, etc.) use a shorter scale such as A1+, A2+, A3.
- A **"reaffirmed"** notation indicates that the agency has reviewed the rating and kept it unchanged.
- **"Nil" international rating** means no global rating agency has been engaged or no cross-border rating exists.

## Disclosure Obligations

For listed companies in India, rating actions are material information and must be intimated to the stock exchanges — for example, under Regulation 30 of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015. Rating rationales are usually published on the agency's website and are often reproduced in the annual report's corporate governance section, creating a public, auditable record of the company's credit profile.

## Case Example: Apollo Micro Systems Limited (FY 2025-26)

In FY 2025-26, [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] obtained a credit rating from [[entities/acuite_rating_and_research_limited]] covering a ₹707.50 crore Line of Credit — split into ₹150.00 crore short term and ₹557.50 crore long term:

| Facility | Amount | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Long-Term (₹557.50 cr) | Line of Credit | ACUITE A- |
| Short-Term (₹150.00 cr) | Line of Credit | ACUITE A2+ (reaffirmed) |

The outlook on the long-term rating was assigned as **"Stable"**. The company reported **Nil** international rating. The rating was disclosed to the stock exchanges as required by Regulation 30 of the SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015.

Interpreted in context, an A- long-term rating with a Stable outlook indicates a moderate-to-strong credit profile with low near-term default expectation but sensitivity to adverse business or economic conditions — consistent with a defence electronics manufacturer carrying working-capital-intensive order execution. The split between long-term and short-term facilities mirrors the company's need for both capex/product-development funding and short-term working capital.

## Why It Matters in This Wiki

Credit ratings are one of the recurring financial-governance disclosures that appear in annual reports alongside [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]], [[concepts/related-party-transactions]], and [[concepts/insider-trading]]. Tracking ratings across years reveals changes in leverage, liquidity, and lender perception. For a defence manufacturer such as [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], a stable investment-grade-equivalent rating also supports participation in large, long-cycle programmes where customers and partners assess balance-sheet strength.
