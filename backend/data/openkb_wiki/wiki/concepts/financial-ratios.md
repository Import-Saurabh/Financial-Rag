# Financial Ratio Analysis

Financial ratio analysis is the practice of computing relationships between line items in the financial statements — liquidity, solvency, profitability, efficiency and capital-structure metrics — to evaluate a company's financial performance, stability and trends over time. Ratios standardise absolute figures so that performance can be compared across periods, against peers, or against internal benchmarks, and are a standard disclosure required under Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. For a group, ratios are presented at both the standalone and consolidated level, and the two sets can diverge because of subsidiaries acquired during the year.

## Ratio Definitions and Components

Ratios are built from a numerator and denominator drawn from the financial statements. In the FY2025-26 financial statements of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]] for the standalone notes and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]] for the consolidated notes), the following formulas are disclosed:

- **Current ratio** = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities (a liquidity measure).
- **Debt-equity ratio** = Total debt ÷ Shareholders' equity (a leverage measure).
- **Debt service coverage ratio** = Earnings available for debt service ÷ Debt service.
- **Return on equity (ROE)** = Net profit after tax ÷ Average shareholders' equity.
- **Inventory turnover ratio** = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average inventory.
- **Trade receivable turnover ratio** = Net sales ÷ Average trade receivables.
- **Trade payable turnover ratio** = Net purchases ÷ Average trade payables.
- **Net capital turnover ratio** = Net sales ÷ Average working capital.
- **Net profit ratio** = Net profit after tax ÷ Revenue from operations.
- **Return on capital employed (ROCE)** = Earnings before interest and taxes ÷ Average capital employed.
- **Return on investment (ROI)** = Non-operating income from investment ÷ Average investment.

The notes clarify the underlying aggregates identically in the standalone and consolidated statements: total debt = borrowings + lease liabilities; earnings available for debt service = net profit after tax + non-cash operating expenses + interest − non-cash operating incomes; debt service = borrowings + lease liabilities; and capital employed = tangible net worth + lease liabilities. ROI is stated as "not applicable" where the company holds no traded investments. The consolidated disclosures also flag the reason for the most material variance — the fall in the debt-equity ratio is attributed to the increase in equity during the year.

## Standalone vs Consolidated

The comparison between the two sets illustrates how consolidation reshapes the ratio picture:

- **Leverage:** the consolidated debt-equity ratio (0.81) is higher than the standalone ratio (0.73) because acquired subsidiaries brought borrowings onto the group balance sheet, while consolidated equity includes the group's enlarged capital base. Consolidated total borrowings were ₹53,241.21 lakhs and total equity ₹1,30,822.03 lakhs, giving total capital of ₹1,84,063.25 lakhs — equity at 71.1% and borrowings at 28.9% of that capital, versus 64.7% / 35.3% a year earlier.
- **Profitability:** the consolidated net profit ratio (11.87%) is lower than the standalone net profit ratio (15.76%), and consolidated ROE (11.21%) is below standalone ROE (12.49%), reflecting the drag from subsidiaries that were loss-making during their part-year of ownership.
- **Efficiency:** consolidated turnover ratios are higher than standalone (inventory 0.87 vs 0.73; receivables 2.51 vs 2.21) because the acquired businesses operate with leaner working-capital intensity relative to revenue.
- **Absolute scale:** consolidated revenue from contracts with customers was ₹90,432.38 lakhs and consolidated net profit ₹11,292.04 lakhs (which underpins the consolidated [[concepts/earnings-per-share]] of ₹3.31 diluted).

## Interpretation

At the standalone level, the debt-equity ratio falling from 1.07 to 0.73 reflects equity growth outpacing borrowings, supported by capital management policy (total equity rose to ₹1,32,233.36 lakhs from ₹60,861.89 lakhs, while total borrowings rose to ₹48,505 lakhs from ₹29,069 lakhs). The net profit ratio rising to 15.76% from 10.18% and ROE improving to 12.49% from 10.15% both point to stronger profitability on a growing revenue base of ₹76,485.32 lakhs. Softer inventory, receivable and payable turnovers indicate balance-sheet expansion — higher inventory, receivables and payables — rather than deterioration in operational performance.

At the consolidated level, the same leverage improvement is visible (debt-equity 1.14 → 0.81, the note citing increased equity) but profitability gains are more muted (ROE 10.00% → 11.21%; net profit ratio 10.03% → 11.87%), consistent with the group integrating a large acquisition during the year. The consolidated group also carries a larger balance sheet — total financial assets of ₹64,247.68 lakhs and total financial liabilities of ₹95,028.46 lakhs at 31 March 2026, against ₹30,850.11 lakhs and ₹62,080.68 lakhs a year earlier — which mechanically affects turnover and coverage metrics.

## Relationship to Other Analysis Areas

Ratio analysis draws directly on the underlying notes:

- Liquidity and solvency ratios depend on borrowings, lease liabilities and capital structure — see [[concepts/leases]], [[concepts/financial-instruments]], [[concepts/capital-management]] and [[concepts/financial-risk-management]].
- Profitability ratios depend on revenue and cost recognition — see [[concepts/revenue-recognition]].
- Efficiency ratios depend on inventory, trade receivables and trade payables balances, and on expected credit loss provisioning — see [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]] and expected credit loss.
- Profitability and asset metrics are underpinned by depreciation and amortisation of property, plant and equipment, right-of-use assets, capitalised project work and acquisition accounting — see [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]], [[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]], [[concepts/goodwill]] and [[concepts/employee-benefits]].
- Consolidated ratios are driven by the group composition and acquisition accounting — see [[concepts/consolidated-financial-statements]] and [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]].
- Returns to shareholders are also expressed through per-share metrics — see [[concepts/earnings-per-share]].

Ratio analysis forms part of the broader [[concepts/financial-ratios]] disclosure framework and supports corporate governance and compliance reporting — see [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]]. Related statutory disclosures in the same notes include supplier payment metrics such as those covered under [[concepts/msmed-supplier-disclosures]].
