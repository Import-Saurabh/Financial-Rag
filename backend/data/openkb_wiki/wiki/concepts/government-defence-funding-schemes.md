# Government Defence and Space Funding Schemes

## Overview

India operates a layered set of government instruments designed to finance defence and space technology development outside the traditional public-sector research establishment. These schemes aim to mobilise private capital, startups, MSMEs, and research institutions into strategic domains, combining direct grants, seed funding, venture capital, and procurement-side policy reform. Together they form the financial backbone of India's indigenisation agenda, complementing [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] and the [[concepts/indian-space-programme]].

## Technology Development Fund (TDF)

The TDF is a Ministry of Defence instrument that extends financial and technical support to public and private enterprises, with particular emphasis on startups and MSMEs, for the development of critical defence technologies. Key features:

- Grants of up to ₹50 crore per project.
- Several technologies have been successfully developed under TDF, and two project systems have flown in space as part of PSLV missions.
- As of June 2026, 80 projects with a total cost of ₹334 crore were under implementation.

The TDF is notable for bridging the gap between early-stage technology risk and production, an intermediate step before programmes graduate into the [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].

## Mission DefSpace and iDEX

Mission DefSpace operates under the iDEX framework to promote indigenous development of dual-use technologies for India's defence-space ecosystem. The programme introduced 75 Defence Space Challenges across five domains:

- Launch systems
- Satellite systems
- Communication and payload systems
- Ground systems
- Software systems

This has enabled startups, MSMEs, and research institutions to co-develop technologies aligned with the operational requirements of the Defence Space Agency and the Indian armed forces. Mission DefSpace represents a deliberate effort to mobilise private sector capability in a domain that had previously been the exclusive preserve of government agencies.

## Private Sector Incentivisation

Beyond challenge-based funding, the government has deployed a mix of policy liberalisation and capital instruments:

- **Liberalised Foreign Direct Investment policy** for the defence and space sectors.
- **IN-SPACe Seed Fund Scheme** supporting early-stage space ventures.
- **Pre-incubation Entrepreneurship Programme** for nascent technology founders.
- **₹1,000 crore Venture Capital Fund** to scale promising defence and space enterprises.
- **₹500 crore Technology Adoption Fund** to accelerate uptake of developed technologies.

These instruments sit alongside procurement-side reforms such as the Positive Indigenisation Lists, the Development cum Production Partner (DcPP) framework, and Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) reforms, which channel procurement toward domestic manufacturers.

## Related Procurement-Side Policies

The funding schemes do not operate in isolation. They are reinforced by:

- **Positive Indigenisation Lists**, which prohibit or restrict import of specified items.
- **DcPP framework**, which qualifies developers for production-phase supply.
- **Defence Industrial Licences**, which govern what firms may manufacture — relevant to [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]].
- **Targets of ₹3 lakh crore of defence production and ₹50,000 crore of exports by 2029**, which shape demand expectations and connect to [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]].

## Relevance to Apollo Micro Systems

[[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] operates directly within this funding and policy ecosystem. The company is an approved vendor for DRDO and DPSUs, holds a Defence Industrial Licence, is AS9100 Rev D certified, and has solutions qualified for production-phase supply under the DcPP framework. Its growth into space-grade electronics and dual-use technologies is supported by Mission DefSpace and related programmes.

The group's expansion into explosives and energetics through [[entities/idl_explosives]], secured via an industrial licence for high explosives including TNT and RDX, reflects how licensing and funding policy combine to open adjacent revenue pools. Its acquisition of [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]], tripartite alliance with the [[entities/indian_navy]] and [[entities/iit_madras]], and MoU with [[entities/grse]] illustrate the collaborative structures these schemes encourage.

## Strategic Significance

The schemes reflect a structural shift: strategic defence and space capability is no longer treated as the exclusive remit of government laboratories such as [[entities/drdo]] and [[entities/isro]]. By funding private development, certifying production partners, and guaranteeing procurement pathways, the government seeks to build a competitive domestic industrial base capable of sustaining long development cycles and competing internationally. The instruments also support specific technology domains where private participation is being actively courted, including [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]], [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]], [[concepts/inertial-navigation-systems]], [[concepts/underwater-warfare]], and [[concepts/defence-energetics]].
