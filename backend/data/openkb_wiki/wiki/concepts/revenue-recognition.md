# Revenue Recognition

Revenue recognition is the accounting process of determining when and for how much income from the transfer of goods or services is recorded in the financial statements. Under Indian Accounting Standards, the governing standard is **Ind AS 115 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers**, which is based on the principle that revenue is recognised when an entity satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service to the customer.

## The Five-Step Model

The source documents ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part7]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]], and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]]) set out the five-step approach applied by the Company:

1. **Identify the contract with the customer**
2. **Identify the performance obligations** in the contract
3. **Determine the transaction price**
4. **Allocate the transaction price** to the performance obligations
5. **Recognise revenue** when a performance obligation is satisfied

## Application at Apollo Micro Systems Limited

As described in the financial statements of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], revenue is derived mainly from the supply of electronics and electro-mechanical systems and components, including design, research and development of systems used in missile programmes (weapon systems electronics), underwater missile programmes, avionics systems, ship-borne systems, and submarine systems. Revenue is recognised when control of the promised goods or services has been transferred to the customer, in an amount reflecting the consideration the Company expects to be entitled to.

### Reported revenue

For the year ended 31 March 2026, **standalone** revenue from operations was **₹76,485.32 lakhs**, comprising sale of products of ₹75,313.83 lakhs and sale of services of ₹1,171.49 lakhs, against ₹56,206.92 lakhs (products ₹55,559.86; services ₹647.06) in the prior year. The business operates as a **single reportable segment** (manufacture and sale of electro-mechanical components and systems and allied components and services), and standalone revenue is **entirely domestic (within India)** with no export revenue.

At the **consolidated** level ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]]), revenue from operations for the year ended 31 March 2026 was **₹90,432.38 lakhs**, against ₹56,206.92 lakhs in the prior year — the increase reflecting both organic growth and the consolidation of newly acquired subsidiaries, including the step-down subsidiary IDL Explosives Limited. Note 23 of the consolidated financial statements ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]]) breaks this into **sale of products of ₹89,189.21 lakhs** (prior year ₹55,559.86 lakhs) and **sale of services of ₹1,243.17 lakhs** (prior year ₹647.06 lakhs). Consolidated other income was ₹627.35 lakhs, giving total income of ₹91,059.73 lakhs.

The dedicated Ind AS 115 disclosure note in Part 11 ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]], Note 48) confirms the same **total revenue from contracts with customers of ₹90,432.38 lakhs** (prior year ₹56,206.92 lakhs), split into **product revenue of ₹89,189.21 lakhs** and **service income of ₹1,243.17 lakhs**, with no separate "other operating revenue". The acquisition of IDL Explosives contributed revenue of ₹13,764.39 lakhs from the acquisition date (15 November 2025) to 31 March 2026; had the acquisition been completed on 1 April 2025, Group revenue for the year would have been ₹1,14,647.77 lakhs. These business-combination effects sit under [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]] and [[concepts/goodwill]].

### Transaction price

Revenue from the sale of goods is measured at the transaction price: the consideration received or receivable, **net of expected returns, taxes, and applicable trade discounts and allowances**. The transaction price excludes amounts collected on behalf of third parties. Estimated returns are identified as a key estimate. In Note 46, revenue is reconciled from the **contract price** (₹76,485.32 lakhs) less **discounts and disallowances** (Nil) to revenue from contracts with customers. The Part 11 Note 48 reconciliation performs the same exercise at the consolidated level: **contract price of ₹90,432.38 lakhs** less **discounts and disallowances of Nil** equals revenue from contracts with customers of ₹90,432.38 lakhs, confirming that no discounts or disallowances were deducted in either year.

### Over-time recognition

For performance obligations satisfied over time, revenue is recognised using the **input/output method** based on performance completed to the reporting date. Progress is measured as the proportion of actual costs incurred to date against the total estimated cost attributable to the performance obligation, because cost incurred best depicts the transfer of control. This is fundamentally a **percentage-of-completion** approach applied to long-duration defence and engineering contracts.

Control is considered to transfer over time if any one of the following criteria is met:

- (a) the customer simultaneously consumes the benefit of the Company's performance; or
- (b) the customer controls the asset as it is created or enhanced by the Company's performance; or
- (c) there is no alternative use of the asset and the Company has an explicit or implicit right to payment considering legal precedents.

### Point-in-time recognition

In all other contracts, revenue is recognised at the point in time when the customer obtains control of the promised goods or services. Indicators of control transfer include:

- the entity has a present right to payment;
- the customer has legal title to the asset;
- the entity has transferred physical possession of the asset;
- the customer has the significant risks and rewards of ownership and has accepted the asset.

The Part 8 notes confirm that both **goods and services are transferred at a point in time**: sale of products is satisfied when control of the goods is transferred to the customer (generally on delivery), and sale of services is satisfied at a point in time and on acceptance by the customer, with payment generally due as per the contract terms. The Part 11 Note 48 **timing-of-recognition** disaggregation reinforces this for the Group: goods transferred at a point in time of ₹89,189.21 lakhs and services transferred at a point in time of ₹1,243.17 lakhs, with no revenue recognised over time.

### Performance obligations

Note 48 describes the performance obligations in terms of two categories:

- **Sale of products** — satisfied when control of the goods is transferred to the customer, generally on delivery, with payment generally due as per the terms of the contract.
- **Sale of services** — satisfied at a point in time and on acceptance by the customer, with payment generally due upon completion of the work and acceptance.

## Contract Balances and Disaggregation

Part 8 provides detailed **revenue from contracts with customers** disclosures (Note 46), and the consolidated notes in Part 10 ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]]) present the corresponding group-level disaggregation under Note 36, now corroborated by the dedicated Ind AS 115 note (Note 48) in Part 11:

- **Disaggregation by product/service** — Electronical components/goods of ₹90,432.38 lakhs (prior year ₹56,206.92 lakhs); no "Others" category. Note 48 splits this into product revenue ₹89,189.21 lakhs and service income ₹1,243.17 lakhs.
- **Disaggregation by geography** — Domestic (India) only at ₹90,432.38 lakhs; Export (outside India) Nil. The Group therefore has no foreign-currency-denominated revenue exposure of significance, although the wider business remains subject to the foreign currency risk discussed under [[concepts/financial-risk-management]].
- **Timing of recognition** — Goods and services each transferred at a point in time, with no over-time revenue.

Related **contract balances** are disclosed alongside revenue:

- **Trade receivables** (Note 8) from contracts with customers — ₹45,441.35 lakhs on a standalone basis (prior year ₹23,843.26 lakhs). The consolidated balance sheet (Part 9) and Note 8 of the consolidated statements (Part 10), together with the contract-balance disclosure in Part 11 Note 48, show group trade receivables of ₹48,093.46 lakhs (prior year ₹23,832.95 lakhs).
- **Contract liabilities** (Note 21/22) — short-term **advances received from customers** for products to be delivered within the next 12 months, recognised as revenue upon delivery. On a standalone basis this balance was ₹560.16 lakhs (prior year ₹653.29 lakhs); the consolidated movement in Note 21 runs from an **opening balance of ₹653.29 lakhs**, plus amounts received in advance of ₹1,536.27 lakhs, less revenue recognised of ₹653.29 lakhs, to a **closing balance of ₹1,536.27 lakhs**. Part 11 Note 48 confirms the same closing contract liability of ₹1,536.27 lakhs (prior year ₹653.29 lakhs).

These balances connect revenue recognition directly to the credit-loss and receivables-ageing discussion under [[concepts/financial-instruments]]. The consolidated ageing of trade receivables disclosed in Part 11 shows ₹35,296.12 lakhs within the credit period, ₹6,966.42 lakhs 91–180 days past due, and ₹5,830.92 lakhs more than 180 days past due.

## Significant Judgements and Estimates

Significant judgement and estimates are used in:

- determining the selling price of goods or services that lack an observable selling price;
- maximising the use of observable inputs while making estimates;
- measuring progress toward complete satisfaction of performance obligations;
- determining expected credit losses; and
- determining the method to arrive at variable consideration requiring adjustments to the transaction price.

These judgements interact closely with [[concepts/financial-instruments]] (particularly the expected credit loss model applied to trade receivables) and are a recurring matter of audit focus, discussed in [[concepts/key-audit-matters]].

### Revenue as a Key Audit Matter

The independent auditor's report on the consolidated financial statements ([[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]]) identifies **revenue recognition as a Key Audit Matter**, because the application of the revenue accounting standard involves key judgements relating to the identification of distinct performance obligations, the determination of the transaction price of those obligations, and the appropriateness of the basis used to measure revenue recognised over a period. Revenue is only recognised to the extent that it is highly probable a significant reversal will not occur.

The auditor's principal procedures included: reviewing the implementation of Ind AS 115 (including recognition of the effect on opening equity and changes to procedures, accounting guidelines, disclosures and systems); reviewing and discussing the accounting policy and the key estimates and judgements made by management; testing internal controls over the completeness, accuracy and timing of revenue; reading a sample of contracts to assess whether the recognition method was relevant and consistent with Ind AS 115 (focusing on contract classification, allocation of income and cost to individual performance obligations, and timing of transfer of control); evaluating significant judgements and estimates against supporting evidence (contractual agreements, delivery records) and historical outturns of prior-period estimates; and applying audit techniques to establish whether any revenue had been recognised where no corresponding receivable or cash was recorded in the general ledger.

### Customer concentration

Since revenue sits within a single segment, both the standalone (Part 8) and consolidated (Part 10) notes disclose **major customers**: on a standalone basis, for the year ended 31 March 2026 **two customers individually contributed more than 10% of revenue**, together representing **22.05%** of revenue. The consolidated Note 36 (Part 10) states that for the year ended 31 March 2026 **no single customer individually contributed more than 10%** of the Group's revenue, while for the year ended 31 March 2025 **one customer contributed 20.03%** of revenue. This concentration is a relevant factor when assessing the reliability of revenue and the risk profile of trade receivables.

## Related Categories of Income

The document distinguishes revenue from other income categories, each recognised on its own basis:

- **Interest income** — accrued on a time basis using the effective interest rate, provided there is no uncertainty of realisation. The consolidated notes record interest on term deposits and loans of ₹421.18 lakhs (prior year ₹258.78 lakhs).
- **Government grants** — revenue grants toward qualifying costs recognised as other income; grants compensating additional depreciation recognised systematically over the asset's useful life. See [[concepts/government-grants]].
- **Dividend income** — recognised when the right to receive payment is established.
- **Export incentives** — recognised based on approval and reliability from the Director General of Foreign Trade; the consolidated notes include duty drawback on exports of ₹1.49 lakhs (prior year Nil).
- **Rental income** — recognised per the relevant rental agreements. As a lessor under Ind AS 116 ([[concepts/leases]]), the Company recognised lease income on buildings of ₹38.45 lakhs in the year (prior year ₹0.80 lakhs).
- **Other income** — recognised when the right to receive arises and receipt is probable and reliably measurable. In Part 8, standalone other income of ₹1,733.71 lakhs includes interest on bank deposits and others, interest on loans, recoveries from amounts previously written back, liabilities no longer required written back, rental income, discounts and credit card rewards, corporate guarantee commission, and recoveries from employees. In the consolidated notes (Part 10), other income of ₹627.35 lakhs (prior year ₹288.54 lakhs) comprises interest on term deposits and loans ₹421.18 lakhs, liabilities no longer required written back (net) ₹177.41 lakhs, recoveries from amounts previously considered non-recoverable ₹1.27 lakhs, duty drawback on exports ₹1.49 lakhs, and miscellaneous income ₹26.00 lakhs.

## Exceptional Items

An item of income or expense that, by its size, type, or incidence, requires disclosure to improve understanding of performance is treated as an **exceptional item** and disclosed separately in the financial statements. Both the standalone (Part 8) and consolidated (Part 9) statements report no exceptional items for either year.

## Why It Matters

Revenue recognition is central to the credibility of reported results for long-cycle defence and engineering businesses. The mix of point-in-time and over-time contracts, the use of cost-based progress measures, the reliance on estimated returns and variable consideration, and the audit's designation of revenue as a Key Audit Matter all make revenue a key area of estimation uncertainty. Disaggregated revenue feeds directly into the Group's single-segment reporting ([[concepts/segment-reporting]]), while the related disclosures on receivables ageing and credit loss allowances appear alongside revenue in [[concepts/financial-instruments]]; inventory build-up linked to contract execution is covered under [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]] and [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]]; goodwill and business-combination effects sit under [[concepts/goodwill]] and [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]]; and the tax consequences of reported profits run through [[concepts/deferred-tax]] and [[concepts/earnings-per-share]]. Related-party dealings that interact with revenue and receivables are covered in [[concepts/related-party-transactions]], and the consolidated results are set out under [[concepts/consolidated-financial-statements]].
