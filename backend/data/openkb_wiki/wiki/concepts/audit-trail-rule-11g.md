# Audit Trail Under Rule 11(g)

Rule 11(g) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014 (as amended) requires the statutory auditor to report on the use of accounting software that has a **feature of recording audit trail (edit log)** and that this feature has operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions recorded in the software. Where the audit trail has been tampered with, or where the feature was not enabled for part of the year, the auditor must say so. The rule is one of the newer "other legal and regulatory requirements" disclosures in the auditor's report and it sits alongside the broader assessment of [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]].

## Key requirements

- The audit trail (edit log) facility must be **enabled and operated throughout the year** for every relevant transaction recorded in the accounting software.
- The audit trail must be **preserved** in line with statutory record-retention requirements.
- The auditor must **test-check** the feature and report whether any instance of tampering was identified.
- Non-compliance, partial operation, or tampering is reported as a qualification or "other matter" in the auditor's report.
- Reporting under Rule 11(g) interacts with the auditor's statement under Section 143(3)(b) on whether proper books of account have been kept.

## Application in the FY 2025-26 audit of Apollo Micro Systems Limited

In the standalone financial statements, the clause appears in the Independent Auditor's Report issued by [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] (CA. Hima Bindu Sagala, ICAI Firm Registration Number 011410S, UDIN 26231056YFSVJL3298, dated 18 May 2026) on [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] for the year ended 31 March 2026.

The substance of the reporting was positive: based on examination including test checks, and in accordance with the implementation guide on reporting on audit trail, the auditor reported that the audit trail feature **operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions** recorded in the software. The auditor further stated that during the course of the audit **no instance of the audit trail feature being tampered with** was encountered, and that the audit trail had been **preserved by the Company** as per statutory record-retention requirements.

However, the same report contains a connected qualification. Under paragraph 2(b), dealing with maintenance of books of account, the auditor stated that proper books of account had been kept "except for the matters stated in paragraph 2(i)(vi) below on reporting under rule 11(g)". Paragraph 2(f) also cross-refers to that qualification. The net effect is an audit report that is unqualified in its opinion on the financial statements, yet carries a **Rule 11(g)-linked exception in the legal and regulatory reporting section**.

### The Company's own disclosure — Note 51 (standalone) and Note 53 (consolidated)

The notes forming part of the standalone financial statements reinforce the auditor's finding. Note 51, *Audit Trail and Back up*, states that the Company has used **Tally accounting software** for maintaining its books of account, that the software has a feature of recording audit trail (edit log), and that this feature **operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions** recorded in the software. The note further records that there were **no instances of the audit trail feature being tampered with**, and that the **audit trail of prior years has been preserved** in accordance with statutory record-retention requirements, to the extent it was enabled and recorded in the respective years.

The consolidated financial statements carry the same confirmation, in Note 53, *Audit Trail and Back up*. That note repeats, in materially identical terms, that the Company has used Tally accounting software, that the edit-log feature operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions, that there were no instances of tampering, and that prior-year audit trails have been preserved per statutory retention requirements to the extent they were enabled and recorded. The consolidated notes are signed by the same management team — Karunakar Reddy Baddam (Managing Director), Krishna Sai Kumar Addepalli (Director), Sudarshan Chiluveru (Chief Financial Officer) and Rukhya Parveen (Company Secretary) — and certified by [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] (CA. Hima Bindu Sagala, UDIN 26231056QQVHAH6926, dated 18 May 2026). The messaging across the standalone and consolidated sets is therefore **consistent at both reporting levels**, which strengthens the reliability signal for readers who rely on the group accounts.

The management notes are consistent with the auditor's Rule 11(g) statement — both describe full-year operation, no tampering and compliant preservation. The qualification that remains is confined to the books-of-account wording rather than to the functioning of the edit log itself, so the exception is a **reporting-form exception** rather than an indication that the edit log was disabled or altered. Note 51 and Note 53 sit alongside other regulatory confirmations, including the absence of benami property, wilful default, struck-off-company transactions and crypto-currency dealings, which together provide context for the reliability of the underlying records.

## Why this matters

- **Reliability of the audit trail over time.** A clean Rule 11(g) statement is the main assurance that ledger entries were not retroactively altered, which supports the integrity of the reported numbers underlying [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] such as revenue recognition and related-party balances.
- **Interaction with judgement-heavy areas.** Where a company carries large [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]]-sensitive inventory or several [[concepts/related-party-transactions]], the edit log provides the evidentiary base for cut-off and completeness testing.
- **Governance signal.** Rule 11(g) reporting is now a standard lens used by analysts and proxy advisers when assessing [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]]; a qualification invites questions about the completeness of the underlying records even when the financial statements themselves are unmodified.
- **Escalation path.** Repeated or unaddressed Rule 11(g) exceptions can move from a reporting note into the auditor's broader conclusions on internal controls and, over time, could affect the reliability of the books-of-account statement itself.
- **Group-level consistency.** Because the consolidated statements repeat the same edit-log confirmation for the parent and its subsidiaries, a clean group-level Rule 11(g) position also supports confidence in the consolidation adjustments that bridge the standalone and consolidated results.

## Practical observations

1. Companies are generally expected to enable the edit log in the core accounting/ERP software and to apply it to all modules touching financial transactions, not only the general ledger.
2. If the feature is enabled mid-year, the auditor must report the period for which it did not operate — a common cause of qualification.
3. Preservation obligations mean the edit log must survive system migrations, version upgrades and vendor changes.
4. Test checks generally involve examining edit log entries for selected transactions, corroborating with source documents, and testing that entries cannot be modified without a logged trail.
5. A useful corroboration is the management disclosure (such as Note 51 in a company's own standalone notes and Note 53 in the consolidated notes) stating the software used, the period of operation, and whether any tampering was detected; this should be read alongside the auditor's Rule 11(g) paragraph.
