# Corporate Governance and Compliance

Corporate governance and regulatory compliance form a core pillar of operations for [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], ensuring transparency, accountability, and adherence to statutory mandates under the Companies Act, 2013, and SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015. Detailed statutory findings are documented in [[summaries/2025_Financial_Year_2025_from_bse_part2]]; the FY 2025-26 governance philosophy, board composition and group-wide compliance architecture are set out in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]]; the shareholder resolutions and AGM notice are captured in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]]; the FY 2025-26 Directors' Report, related party transaction disclosures, CSR annexure and Secretarial Audit Report are detailed in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part3]]; the formal statutory annexures — Form AOC-1, Form AOC-2, the Dividend Distribution Policy, ESOS-2018 disclosures, remuneration disclosures and the Corporate Governance Report — are set out in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part4]]; the closing Corporate Governance Report disclosures, the CFO certification and the full Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report are set out in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part5]]; and the tail of the BRSR (Principles 6–9), the Independent Auditor's Report and the audited standalone financial statements are set out in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]].

The Independent Auditor's Report on the standalone financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026 was issued by [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]], Chartered Accountants (ICAI Firm Registration No. 011410S), signed by [[entities/hima_bindu_sagala]], Partner (Membership No. 231056; UDIN 26231056YFSVJL3298), dated 18 May 2026 at Hyderabad. The auditor identified five [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] for the year:

1. **[[concepts/related-party-transactions]]** — significant unsecured loans, sale and leasing of properties, and investments in subsidiary share capital, assessed against the Companies Act, 2013 and the SEBI LODR Regulations. Procedures included testing the operating effectiveness of controls over capturing related-party transactions, evaluating arm's-length pricing approvals by the Audit Committee and Board, agreeing disclosed amounts to underlying documentation, and assessing compliance with Sections 177, 185, 186 and 188 of the Act and SEBI (LODR) 2015.
2. **Capital work-in-progress** — ₹17,059.72 lakhs (after additions and capitalisation), comprising testing tools and instruments and manufacturing-facility expansion of ₹2,341.49 lakhs and civil works of ₹14,718.23 lakhs, with ₹7,512.88 lakhs specifically judged against the recognition criteria of Ind AS 16. Procedures included reviewing business plans, substantive testing of additions and deletions, ledger scrutiny and review of management's physical verification reports; the auditor did not identify any inappropriately capitalised costs.
3. **Inventories** — ₹76,000.75 lakhs, constituting 50.53% of total current assets and 33.13% of total assets, carrying risk of obsolescence given the customer-specific nature of the inventories. Procedures covered controls over physical verification, identification of obsolete and slow-moving inventory, inventory ageing and provisioning, sample testing of orders, purchase and cost accumulation in work-in-progress, comparison of cost with net realisable value, and a detailed analysis of the order book. See [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]].
4. **Revenue recognition** — judgements relating to identification of distinct performance obligations, determination of transaction price, the appropriateness of the basis used to measure revenue recognised over a period, and the extent to which revenue is recognised only where a significant reversal is highly probable. Procedures included reviewing the Company's implementation of Ind AS 115, testing controls over completeness, accuracy and timing of revenue, reading sample contracts, and applying audit techniques to establish whether any revenue was recognised without corresponding accounts receivable or cash.
5. **Accounting for business combination** — the acquisition of 100% of the equity shareholding of [[entities/idl_explosives]], which became a wholly-owned step-down subsidiary and was accounted for under Ind AS 103. Because identifiable liabilities exceeded identifiable assets at the acquisition date, provisional [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]] goodwill of ₹14,517.66 lakhs was recognised, being the excess of consideration transferred over the net identifiable assets (negative) acquired. The purchase price allocation exercise, including fair valuation of assets and liabilities and consequent reassessment of goodwill, remains incomplete as at the balance sheet date and is proposed to be completed within the twelve-month measurement period permitted under paragraphs 45–50 of Ind AS 103; the goodwill is accordingly provisional and based on book values. The auditor tested the acquisition journal entries, evaluated the identification of the acquirer, assessed management's future profitability assumptions supporting the commercial rationale, and obtained a written representation that no impairment indicators exist for the provisional goodwill.

## Group-Wide Compliance and Risk Governance

With the acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] and the entry into energetics, compliance oversight has been extended across a larger multi-site footprint. Statutory and secretarial compliance across Group entities runs on a structured compliance calendar supported by a responsibility matrix and maker-checker-approver controls, with periodic certification to the Board. Environmental governance includes a comprehensive review of consents and licences, covering Pollution Control Board consents to establish and operate, PESO licences and hazardous-waste authorisations. Each unit undergoes risk assessment of process-safety and environmental hazards with mitigation plans in place, and periodic EHS audits are conducted with corrective-action tracking. IDL's environmental compliance reporting is being integrated into the Group's compliance certification framework, with outcomes reviewed by management and the Board.

The Company has established a risk management framework under which a Committee comprising the senior executives of the Company periodically identifies potential risks to the Company's strategy and takes effective measures to mitigate them, with the Board periodically updated on the key risks, steps and processes initiated to reduce and, where feasible, eliminate them. The expanded footprint brings licensing and compliance obligations under the Explosives Act, 1884 and the Explosives Rules, 2008, met through dedicated compliance functions and statutory audits. A unified SAP S/4HANA digital thread links materials, batches, production orders and compliance records across locations, giving real-time visibility and batch traceability from raw material to delivered system. Adherence to applicable labour legislation—covering wages, working hours and social security—is monitored through the same compliance framework and reported in the Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report. Human rights principles requiring fairness, dignity, equal opportunity and ethical treatment are embedded in the Code of Conduct and HR policies, and extend to the supply chain through vendor onboarding requirements. The Company has also complied with the provisions of the Maternity Benefit Act, and confirmed there were no applications or proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, no one-time settlements with banks or financial institutions, and no significant and material regulatory orders impacting going-concern status. ## Compliance Oversight Summary

The FY 2025-26 reporting cycle shows a company whose governance architecture is compliant on all mandatory SEBI LODR requirements, with a clean statutory audit opinion and an unmodified opinion on internal financial controls, but whose disclosure quality remains mixed — the internal inconsistency between the stated number of trade chamber affiliations and those listed, the absence of a web-link to the cyber-security and data privacy policy, the CARO observation on related-party loan documentation, the audit-trail reserve on books of account, the pending ₹834.36 lakhs of income-tax disputes, and the shortfall in CSR spend against the annual obligation together temper an otherwise strong compliance record. The two Secretarial Audit observations (the ROC CRA-4 show-cause notice and the NSE penalty) were both remediated through compounding and payment. The most judgement-heavy audit matters for the year — provisional goodwill on the IDL Explosives acquisition, capital work-in-progress capitalisation, inventory obsolescence provisioning and revenue recognition — sit at the intersection of governance, financial reporting and the group's expanding defence portfolio, and will remain under Board and Audit Committee scrutiny as the purchase price allocation and the Premier Explosives acquisition progress.
