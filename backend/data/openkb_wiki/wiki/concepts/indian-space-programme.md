# Indian Space Programme

The Indian Space Programme encompasses the nation's civilian and strategic space activities, including lunar exploration, satellite-based navigation, human spaceflight, and a growing private-sector ecosystem. It is led by the Indian Space Research Organisation ([[entities/isro]]) and increasingly supported by government funding schemes and private participation.

## Lunar Exploration: Chandrayaan

After the success of Chandrayaan-3, which made India the first country to achieve a soft landing near the Moon's south pole in 2023, India's lunar ambitions have continued to expand:

- **Chandrayaan-4** is scheduled for 2027 with the objective of collecting lunar samples and returning them to Earth.
- **Chandrayaan-5** will explore water and other volatile materials near the lunar south pole.

## Satellite-Based Navigation: GAGAN

GAGAN (GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation) is an indigenous Satellite-Based Augmentation System co-developed by [[entities/isro]] and the Airports Authority of India. It enhances GPS accuracy and provides integrity information for safer aircraft navigation. Globally certified, GAGAN facilitates precise satellite-based landings and supports operations across sectors beyond commercial aviation. It strengthens sovereign navigation infrastructure and supports India's strategic push toward technological self-reliance.

## Human Spaceflight and the National Space Station

The **Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS)** is a key pillar of Space Vision 2047 — India's planned five-module space station in Low Earth Orbit, engineered for long-duration human spaceflight and advanced scientific research. In September 2024, the Union Cabinet formally approved the development and launch of its first module, **BAS-01**, by 2028 as part of the expanded Gaganyaan programme. The platform is set to enable research across life sciences, medicine, and pioneering technologies while supporting long-term deep-space exploration goals.

## Private Sector Participation and Defence-Space Integration

Government initiatives continue to support the private sector's contribution to the space ecosystem. Notable programmes include the liberalised Foreign Direct Investment policy, the IN-SPACe Seed Fund Scheme, the Pre-incubation Entrepreneurship Programme, the ₹1,000 crore Venture Capital Fund, and the ₹500 crore Technology Adoption Fund. These sit alongside broader indigenisation instruments such as the [[concepts/government-defence-funding-schemes]].

On the defence side, **Mission DefSpace** operates under the iDEX framework to promote indigenous development of dual-use technologies. It has introduced 75 Defence Space Challenges across five domains — launch systems, satellite systems, communication and payload systems, ground systems, and software systems — enabling startups, MSMEs, and research institutions to co-develop technologies aligned with the operational requirements of the Defence Space Agency and the Indian armed forces. This represents a deliberate effort to mobilise private sector capability in a domain previously reserved for government agencies.

## Relevance to Apollo Micro Systems

[[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] identifies expanding demand for space-grade electronics and dual-use technologies under Mission DefSpace and related programmes as a key opportunity. The company produces Commercial Off-the-Shelf (COTS) solutions customised for the defence and space sectors, and two project systems developed under the Technology Development Fund have been flown in space as part of PSLV missions. Its capabilities span satellite and space technology, alongside missile systems, naval systems, aircraft electronics, and homeland security — supporting its positioning within [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]] and [[concepts/defense_indigenization]].

## Strategic Significance

Together these missions and programmes reflect India's broader push toward technological self-reliance in space: sovereign navigation through GAGAN, lunar scientific leadership through Chandrayaan, human spaceflight capability through Gaganyaan and BAS, and the cultivation of a private defence-space industrial base through Mission DefSpace and seed funding. These efforts intersect with adjacent domains such as [[concepts/inertial-navigation-systems]], [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]], and [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]], where space-derived technologies and precision capabilities overlap with defence applications.
