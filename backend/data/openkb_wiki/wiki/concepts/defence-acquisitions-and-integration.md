# Defence Acquisitions and Integration

**Defence Acquisitions and Integration** refers to the strategy by which defence companies acquire complementary businesses and integrate capabilities vertically — from raw energetics and subsystems through to complete weapon platforms — in order to compete as prime contractors rather than discrete subsystem suppliers.

## Core Idea

As the Indian defence market matures under [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] and [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]], capable private-sector players increasingly seek end-to-end responsibility for complete weapon platforms. This requires capabilities that rarely exist within a single firm: energetics, guidance electronics, seekers, actuation, warhead integration, safety-and-arming mechanisms, and platform-level systems engineering. Acquisition is the fastest route to assembling those pieces.

## Case Study: Apollo Micro Systems Limited

In FY 2025-26, [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] pursued a deliberate acquisition-and-integration strategy, positioning itself as a system-of-systems integrator rather than a component house.

### Group Structure

- [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] — 76% subsidiary
- [[entities/ananya_sip_rf_technologies_private_limited]] — 51% subsidiary
- [[entities/apollo_strategic_technologies_private_limited]] — step-down subsidiary
- [[entities/idl_explosives]] — step-down subsidiary (acquisition completed 17 November 2025)
- [[entities/shauryastra_defence_systems_private_limited]] — related entity

### The Premier Explosives Acquisition

The most significant integration move is the acquisition of a 41.33% stake in [[entities/premier_explosives_limited]] for approximately ₹1,550 crore, executed via a Share Purchase Agreement on 9 July 2026. The transaction triggered open-offer obligations under the SEBI SAST Regulations:

- Public announcement: 10 July 2026
- Detailed Public Statement: 16 July 2026
- Draft Letter of Offer filed with SEBI: 23 July 2026
- Offer size: up to 26% of equity at ₹698 per share (~₹975.64 crore)

Completion is subject to regulatory approvals including the Competition Commission of India. The stated rationale is the combination of complementary capabilities in defence systems and energetic materials — enabling operational efficiencies, enhanced R&D, and broader participation in defence and space programmes. This is a textbook example of [[concepts/defence-energetics]] acquisition as a route to [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]].

### The IDL Explosives Acquisition and Its Accounting

The IDL acquisition is the consolidation event that gave the group a wholly-owned step-down subsidiary in energetics. Because the acquisition was completed during FY 2025-26, the FY 2025-26 standalone financial statements record it as a business combination under [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]]. The identifiable liabilities of the target exceeded its identifiable assets (negative net assets) while the fair value of consideration transferred was positive, resulting in the recognition of provisional goodwill of ₹14,517.66 lakhs.

Critically, management had not completed a fair valuation of the acquired assets and liabilities as at the balance sheet date; the purchase price allocation exercise — including reassessment of goodwill — is to be completed within the twelve-month measurement period permitted under Ind AS 103. The recognised goodwill is therefore **provisional and based on book values rather than fair values**. Auditors [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] flagged this as a [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] item, noting that any subsequent fair valuation could produce a significant retrospective adjustment to goodwill with consequential impact on the opening balance sheet and comparatives.

### Financing the Integration: Balance Sheet and Cash Flow

The integration strategy is visible as much in the financing architecture as in the corporate structure. Integration is funded through sustained lending to group entities and heavy capital expenditure rather than through operating cash generation.

- **Loans to subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries**: ₹29,769.14 lakhs granted during FY 2025-26, with ₹31,555.75 lakhs outstanding at the balance sheet date (net, after adjustments) — reported under non-current financial assets at ₹31,615.82 lakhs, up from ₹1,827.09 lakhs a year earlier. Loans to other parties added a further ₹699.55 lakhs outstanding.
- **Capital work-in-progress**: ₹17,059.72 lakhs, comprising testing tools and instruments and expansion of manufacturing facilities (₹2,341.49 lakhs) and civil works (₹14,718.23 lakhs). Of this, ₹7,512.88 lakhs was specifically assessed against the recognition criteria of Ind AS 16, becoming a second [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] item.
- **Cash flows**: investing activities absorbed ₹(57,583.88) lakhs, including ₹(29,788.72) lakhs of loans to subsidiaries and ₹(23,756.24) lakhs of PPE and CWIP purchases. Operating activities *used* ₹(15,298.78) lakhs, driven by inventory build-up of ₹(15,941.92) lakhs and trade receivables of ₹(21,460.81) lakhs.
- **Funding**: financing activities provided ₹74,885.68 lakhs, from share capital and securities premium, money received against warrants, and both long-term and short-term borrowings. Share capital rose to ₹3,572.92 lakhs following conversion of 2,37,59,986 warrants and a preferential allotment of 2,70,42,894 equity shares, both at a ₹113 premium per share.

These intra-group loans and investments are the subject of a fourth [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] item: [[concepts/related-party-transactions]], covering unsecured loans, sale and leasing of properties, and investments in subsidiary share capital, evaluated for arm's-length pricing and compliance with Sections 177, 185, 186 and 188 of the Companies Act, 2013.

### Backward Integration

By acquiring energetics capability, AMS gains in-house access to qualified energetic materials — critical for end-to-end realisation of products such as [[entities/limpet_mines]] and [[entities/moored_mine]]. This backward integration is a recurring theme in [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]] and [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]].

## Integration as Strategic Positioning

AMS describes its transition as moving from a development-centric electronics house to a vertically integrated weapon systems enterprise. Its target verticals are:

- Precision-guided munitions
- Underwater weapon systems — see [[concepts/underwater-warfare]]
- Embedded guidance and control
- Smart energetics

Flagship integrated products include the [[entities/mini_torpedo]] and the [[entities/asw_weapon_platform]].

## Why It Matters

1. **Scarce capabilities** — Energetics and specialised ordnance are tightly licensed and hard to build organically.
2. **Prime contractor ambition** — Acquisitions unlock bidding for complete platforms rather than subsystems.
3. **Strategic alignment** — Supports national self-reliance objectives and reduces import dependence.
4. **Scale and resilience** — Combined entities can serve larger defence and space programmes.
5. **Accounting and integration risk** — Serial acquisition imposes real costs: provisional fair values, measurement-period adjustments, goodwill impairment exposure, related-party scrutiny, and a cash-flow profile where operations and investments consume cash while financing supplies it.
