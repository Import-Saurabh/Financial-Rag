# Clean Energy and Fuel Cells

Clean energy is one of [[entities/mtar-technologies]]'s three core verticals alongside civil nuclear power and aerospace & defense. In practice the vertical is anchored in **fuel cell** manufacturing — principally hot-box assemblies for a large global fuel cell customer — supplemented by a widening set of adjacent products (ball screws, enclosures, cable harnesses, and other qualified assemblies) and by nascent positions in hydropower, wind energy, and data center infrastructure.

Within the company's disclosure, clean energy is presented as the **largest and fastest-compounding revenue driver**, expected to dominate the revenue mix by the end of FY27.

## Demand Signal

As reported in [[summaries/2026_Aug_2026_Transcript]] (Q1 FY27 earnings call, 30 July 2026), the vertical recorded **record order inflows** during the quarter, reinforcing an already robust order book and giving strong forward visibility. Management repeatedly characterised demand from the anchor customer as exceeding MTAR's present ability to supply — the binding constraint is capacity, not demand.

Specific capacity figures and customer volumes are withheld under a signed **non-disclosure agreement**, so the company communicates expansion only in relative terms ("multifold") rather than in absolute hot-box counts.

## Three-Phase Capacity Expansion

The fuel cell capacity augmentation programme is being executed in three phases:

| Phase | Scope | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Initial expansion | Commissioned (complete in all respects) |
| Phase 2 | Further expansion | Commissioned by September–October 2026 |
| Phase 3 | Multifold expansion | Commissioned by March 2027; volume ramp-up from April 2027 |

The facility being developed is designed to support **multiple products under one roof**, giving greater scale and operational efficiency than a single-product line. Phase 3 commissioning in March 2027 marks the end of the build and the start of the ramp; management was explicit that commissioning and production ramp are distinct milestones.

Critically, expansion is not limited to physical capacity: **manpower training and automation initiatives** were initiated well ahead of capacity coming on stream, so that the organisation is operationally prepared for the volume ramp. This sequencing — people and automation before assets — is presented as a deliberate execution-risk mitigation.

## Products Beyond the Hot Box

The "products" line — non-hot-box manufactured content — grew to roughly **INR100 crore in Q1 FY27**, close to 50% of the clean energy segment's quarterly revenue run-rate. Management expects this to continue growing, with H2 FY27 stronger than H1.

Products cited as driving this growth include:

- **Ball screws**, where export orders are strong and a major contract exists with an MNC customer for aerospace applications;
- Additional fuel cell assemblies beyond the hot box;
- Enclosures and cable harnesses, pursued to enlarge the addressable wallet with the anchor customer;
- Further qualified assemblies in progress, with disclosure deferred until completion.

Management framed this as an innovation pipeline rather than a one-off: new products are developed continuously, with volume results typically visible six to twelve months later. The medium-term aspiration offered on the call was for the products business to exceed **INR1,000 crore** by around FY30.

## Adjacent Clean Energy Segments

- **Hydropower and wind energy** — described as showing good traction, with MTAR pursuing select products rather than full platforms.
- **Data center infrastructure solutions** — a newly entered segment, export-oriented, with an initial INR45 crore order to be executed by February–March 2027 and a customer requirement sized at roughly eight times that level on a repeating annual basis. It leverages the same precision-manufacturing and assembly capabilities, and a dedicated facility is being set up.
- **Fuel cells for behind-the-meter and data center power** — the demand driver most often linked by analysts to global data center build-outs.

## Economics and Capital Intensity

- Clean energy absorbs about **70%** of the ~INR500 crore FY27–FY28 capex programme, with the remaining ~30% for other verticals; some clean energy capacity is fungible across sectors.
- Target asset turnover on incremental capex is at least **6x**, with 4–5x overall.
- Q1 FY27 capex spend was ~INR30–35 crore, with INR80 crore capitalised out of capital work in progress — see [[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]] and [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]].
- Gross margin dilution in Q1 (45.61% versus 47.65%) was attributed to revenue mix, and clean energy's scale-up is therefore a mix variable to watch against the 24% ±100 bps EBITDA guidance.

## Execution Horizon

The clean energy order book is **short-cycle** — orders are to be executed over the current year and the next, with individual tranches running from two months to two years. This contrasts with the multi-year civil nuclear book (see [[concepts/civil-nuclear-power]]) and means the vertical converts order inflows to revenue quickly, making capacity availability the gating factor rather than contract tenure. See [[concepts/order-book-and-revenue-visibility]] and [[concepts/revenue-recognition]].
