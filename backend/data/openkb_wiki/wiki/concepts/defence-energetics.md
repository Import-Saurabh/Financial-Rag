# Defence Energetics and High Explosives

**Defence energetics** is the branch of the defence industrial base concerned with high-energy materials — high explosives, propellants, warheads, fuzes, and Safety and Arming (S&A) mechanisms — that convert a weapon platform into lethal effect. Where defence electronics constitute the "brain, nerves and eyes" of a weapon, energetics constitute its punch. Control of energetics is therefore treated by nations as a matter of strategic sovereignty, because a country that can design a missile but cannot fill it is not truly self-reliant.

## The Two Segments

### 1. Industrial / Commercial Explosives
Bulk and packaged explosives engineered for mining and infrastructure: coal and mineral extraction, overburden removal at opencast mines, quarrying, and road and irrigation projects. Demand is anchored in coal production (Coal India Limited, Singareni Collieries and private miners), with institutional and export channels providing additional growth. Rising overburden removal and government focus on domestic coal output give this segment medium-term volume visibility.

### 2. Defence Energetics
High explosives such as **TNT, RDX, HMX and PETN**; propellants; warheads; fuzes; and allied Safety and Arming mechanisms. These are a strategic priority under [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] and [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]], because the Positive Indigenisation Lists place a wide range of munitions and their sub-assemblies under import embargo.

## Why It Matters Strategically

- **Vertical completeness.** Electronics, integration and energetics under one roof is the architecture of a complete weapon-system OEM — a combination very few companies globally possess.
- **Import substitution.** Global ammunition restocking plus India's export ambitions expand the addressable market for domestically manufactured energetic materials, reducing exposure to export-control regimes and foreign supply shocks.
- **Supply-chain resilience.** Energetics sit at the end of a long chain of critical raw materials and compounds; backward integration into these inputs protects delivery obligations to the Armed Forces and DPSUs. See [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].
- **OEM transition.** Energetics capability supports the move described in [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]] from sub-system supplier to platform and weapon-system producer.
- **Adjacent revenue pools.** Entering energetics opens demand in both industrial and defence markets, diversifying a portfolio otherwise tied to electronics procurement cycles.
- **Working-capital intensity.** Energetic materials are customer-specific and long-dated, which shifts the balance sheet toward inventory and work-in-progress and makes obsolescence assessment a live audit question — see [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]].

## Apollo Micro Systems' Position

According to [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]], FY 2025-26 marked Apollo's decisive entry into energetics:

- **Acquisition of IDL Explosives Limited.** Apollo Defence Industries Private Limited (ADIPL), a subsidiary of [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], completed a 100% equity acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] for **₹107 crore in an all-cash transaction**, completed on 15 November 2025. See [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] and [[entities/entities-organization-apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]]. The acquisition marked the Group's formal entry into the explosives and energetics value chain, and IDL has since been integrated into the Group's operations, financial reporting and safety framework. The related-party disclosure in the FY26 annual report lists the Group's economic interest in IDL at roughly 76% through ADIPL, reflecting the layered holding structure.
- **Accounting for the acquisition.** The auditors of [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] confirmed that the transaction was accounted for as a business combination under [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]], with the Group treated as the accounting acquirer. Because IDL's identifiable liabilities exceeded its identifiable assets at the acquisition date (negative net assets) while consideration transferred was positive, the Company recognised **provisional goodwill of ₹14,517.66 lakhs**. The purchase price allocation — fair valuation of assets and liabilities acquired and consequent reassessment of goodwill — remains outstanding as at the balance sheet date and is to be completed within the twelve-month measurement period permitted under Ind AS 103. Any subsequent fair valuation could therefore produce a significant retrospective adjustment to goodwill and opening comparatives. This was one of the report's [[concepts/key-audit-matters]].
- **What IDL brings.** A manufacturer of the full range of packaged and bulk explosives for mining and infrastructure, an extensive range of cartridge explosives, established manufacturing units across India's key mining belts, long-standing relationships with Coal India subsidiaries and institutional customers, and a growing dealer and export franchise. IDL's standalone turnover for FY 2025-26 was ₹37,979.77 lakhs, with a small loss, reflecting an under-utilised industrial explosives base that defence high-explosives work is intended to absorb.
- **Consolidation and segment reporting.** Apollo operates in a single reportable segment — electromechanical components and systems and allied components and services — while the consolidated Group additionally reports the manufacture of industrial explosives through IDL Explosives with effect from 15 November 2025. Consolidated revenue for FY 2025-26 of ₹90,432.38 lakhs reflects IDL's partial-year contribution; at the standalone level Apollo Micro Systems Limited reported revenue from operations of ₹76,485.32 lakhs and profit before tax of ₹17,462.69 lakhs.
- **Industrial licences.** IDL Explosives received an **Industrial Licence for HMX and TNT** (RDX is also referenced by the Managing Director), marking the Group's entry into specialised defence energetics. Alongside this, Apollo secured a **lifetime Arms Manufacturing Licence from DPIIT**, authorising manufacture, assembly, integration and proof-testing of complete strategic weapon systems — Missiles, ATGMs, Torpedoes, Underwater Mines, Aerial Bombs, Rockets, Loitering Munitions, Chaffs, Flares and Decoys — alongside UAVs, inertial navigation systems and radars. Regulated facilities of this kind sit squarely within [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]].
- **Greenfield capacity.** A Memorandum of Understanding with the Government of Telangana covering a Defence and High-Energetic Explosives project with a proposed investment of approximately **₹1,500 crore**. A separate ₹300 crore investment at TSIIC Hardware Park Phase-II (Unit III, ~10.68 acres) will manufacture, integrate and test Grad Rockets, ASW Rockets, Anti-Tank Mines and Artillery Munitions. See [[entities/tsiic]]. The annual report describes the new Hardware Park-II integrated facility variously as 350,000 sq. ft. (company overview) and 600,000 sq. ft. (FY26 milestones), and identifies **fuzes, mines and inertial navigation systems** as the critical components the expanded unit is designed to produce. The build-out is visible in the standalone balance sheet as **capital work-in-progress of ₹17,059.72 lakhs**, comprising ₹2,341.49 lakhs of testing tools and instruments for expansion of manufacturing facilities and ₹14,718.23 lakhs of civil works — itself a [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] in which the auditors tested the appropriateness of capitalisation against Ind AS 16 and, as at 31 March 2026, found no costs inappropriately capitalised.
- **Fuzes and S&A.** Apollo's existing strengths in fuzes and weapon electronics allow forward integration from electronics into complete energetic systems. FY26 R&D produced advanced fuzes and Safety & Arming mechanisms for air, land and naval munitions.
- **Capacity scale.** The IDL acquisition expanded Group manufacturing capacity to **six plants**.
- **Group financing of energetics.** The FY26 related-party resolutions contemplate inter-corporate loans and corporate guarantees to IDL Explosives of up to **₹300 crore**, alongside a guarantee of up to ₹300 crore toward the raising or renewal of IDL's working capital debt, with IDL indemnifying the Company against losses on invocation. The FY26 financial statements record loans granted during the year to subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries of **₹29,769.14 lakhs**, with ₹31,555.75 lakhs outstanding at the balance sheet date, while loans to other parties stood at ₹699.55 lakhs. Interest of ₹52.41 lakhs was overdue on one loan to a company, against which a provision of ₹58.71 lakhs was carried. The statutory auditors noted that the Company raised share capital from members of the public partly to meet the obligations of its subsidiaries — total FY26 equity raising included conversion of 2,37,59,986 warrants into equity shares (₹27,086.38 lakhs) and a preferential allotment of 2,70,42,894 equity shares (₹30,828.90 lakhs) — and confirmed compliance with Sections 185 and 186 of the Companies Act, 2013 in respect of loans, investments and guarantees. Related-party transactions, including unsecured loans, sale and leasing of properties and investments in subsidiary share capital, were themselves a [[concepts/key-audit-matters]] and are discussed further in [[concepts/related-party-transactions]].

## Safety, Environment and Governance Overlay

Energetics introduces hazards that do not exist in electronics assembly, and the reports describe a corresponding governance overlay. Following the IDL acquisition, the Group's safety framework extends to the **manufacture, storage and transportation of explosives**, governed by stringent licensing and process-safety requirements under the **Explosives Act, 1884 and the Explosives Rules, 2008**.

- **Physical safety.** Safety committees, drills, personal protective equipment and on-site medical facilities; significantly expanded safety training covering explosives handling, process safety and emergency response. IDL's plants operate institutionalised safety management systems including **permit-to-work controls, regular mock drills, statutory inspections and safety audits**, and the unit's long safety track record and industry recognition reinforce the Group's safety culture.
- **Environmental stewardship.** Emissions and effluent management for chemical processes at IDL's plant sites; green design embedded in upcoming greenfield capacity, including the planned energetics complex.
- **Consents and licences.** A comprehensive review of all environmental consents — Pollution Control Board consents to establish and operate, PESO licences and hazardous-waste authorisations. See [[concepts/corporate-governance-and-compliance]].
- **Reported environmental footprint.** The FY 2025-26 disclosures under [[concepts/business-responsibility-and-sustainability-reporting]] record a very light environmental footprint: air emissions of NOx, SOx, particulate matter, persistent organic pollutants, volatile organic compounds and hazardous air pollutants all reported as **Nil**, Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions reported as **Nil**, plastic and e-waste generated being negligible, and total water withdrawal and consumption of **3,589.01 kilolitres** drawn entirely from third-party water with **nil discharge**. The Company stated it has not implemented a Zero Liquid Discharge mechanism because its manufacturing processes do not produce significant liquid waste, and confirmed compliance with the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act and the Environment Protection Act, with no operations in or around ecologically sensitive areas and no Environmental Impact Assessments undertaken during the year. These figures relate to the entity's own operating profile rather than the chemical-process footprint of IDL's explosives plants, which is governed separately by the consents and licences described above.
- **Risk assessment.** Each unit undergoes assessment of process-safety and environmental hazards with mitigation plans, reviewed by management and the Board. The Group's formal risk framework now explicitly flags regulatory and licensing obligations as a monitored risk, noting that they have **widened with the addition of explosives manufacturing**, and addresses them through dedicated compliance functions and statutory audits. See [[concepts/corporate-social-responsibility]].
- **Controls and assurance.** The statutory auditors issued an unqualified opinion on the standalone financial statements and reported adequate [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]], with the audit trail under Rule 11(g) operating throughout the year and found not to have been tampered with. No fraud, no whistle-blower complaints and no cash losses were reported.
