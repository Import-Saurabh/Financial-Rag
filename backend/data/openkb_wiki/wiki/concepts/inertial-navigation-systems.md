# Inertial Navigation Systems (INS)

Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) are self-contained navigation and guidance technologies that determine a platform's position, velocity and orientation by continuously integrating measurements from motion sensors — gyroscopes for rotation and accelerometers for acceleration — without relying on external signals such as GNSS. Because they do not depend on satellite reception or radio links, inertial systems remain functional in GNSS-denied or electronically contested environments, a property that makes them foundational to modern guided weapons, unmanned platforms and submersibles.

## INS at Apollo Micro Systems

For [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]], Inertial Navigation is one of three declared technology pillars, alongside Radio Frequency Systems and Artificial Intelligence. The company frames INS as "the foundational technology of every guided weapon, every UAV, every submersible" — positioning it as an enabling capability that sits beneath many of its platform and sub-system offerings.

The technology stack described spans three sensor generations:

- **MEMS-based INS** — micro-electro-mechanical systems sensors suited to compact, cost-sensitive platforms.
- **Fiber Optic Gyro (FOG) systems** — higher-precision rotation sensing using interferometry in optical fibre.
- **Ring Laser Gyro (RLG) systems** — high-accuracy inertial sensing for demanding guidance applications.

In FY 2025-26, Apollo reported the successful development of **FOG-based INS systems** among its principal R&D outcomes, alongside the Mini Torpedo and sensor suites for underwater autonomous vehicles. The company's strategic attributes emphasise a 41-year indigenous engineering base of more than 700 technologies developed and over 150 active defence programmes, within which guidance and navigation electronics are a recurring thread.

## Licensing and Portfolio Position

The grant of a **Lifetime Arms Manufacturing Licence by DPIIT** to Apollo explicitly covers Inertial Navigation Systems as part of its licensed portfolio, together with Unmanned Aerial Systems and Radar Equipment. The FY 2025-26 DPIIT licence is described in the Annual Report as a comprehensive authorisation spanning UAVs, INS systems, radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, ATGMs, decoys, chaffs and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions. This places INS within the same licensed capability envelope as complete strategic weapon systems. See [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]].

Separately, the group's acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] brought an industrial licence for the manufacture of high explosives including TNT and RDX — an adjacent but distinct licensing track under the Explosives Act, 1884 and the Explosives Rules, 2008. See [[concepts/defence-energetics]].

## Role in Weapon and Platform Programmes

INS is closely tied to the guidance and navigation work that underpins Apollo's participation in indigenous missile and munitions programmes. The company describes itself as a trusted partner and supplier of guidance and navigation systems across several programmes, including the [[entities/pinaka]] artillery rocket programme. Its solutions are qualified for inclusion in the production-phase supply chain under the Development cum Production Partner (DcPP) framework, and the company is an approved vendor for [[entities/drdo]] and the DPSUs.

INS sits naturally alongside three adjacent capability areas:

- **Underwater warfare** — inertial sensing supports torpedo homing, autonomous underwater vehicles and naval mines where GNSS is unavailable. See [[concepts/underwater-warfare]].
- **Counter-drone systems** — stealthy, jam-resistant navigation is relevant to both drone platforms and the effectors that counter them. See [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]].
- **Directed energy and RF systems** — precision pointing and tracking depend on stable inertial reference. See [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]].

## Industrialisation and Transfer of Technology

Apollo's INS capability is developed in-house and also matured through absorption of DRDO designs. The company describes a five-part industrialisation chain for converting transfer-of-technology receipts into repeat production orders: design-for-manufacture, qualified manufacturing and test infrastructure, a defence-grade supply chain, quality and certification systems aligned to DGQA/DGAQA/DGNAI, and lifecycle support. INS products benefit directly from this chain, since guidance electronics require environmental stress screening, EMI/EMC, vibration, shock and climatic qualification. See [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]].

Manufacturing capacity for INS is being expanded materially. AMS operates two facilities in Hyderabad (Mallapur and Adibatla) and is building a new integrated facility at Hardware Park-II in Telangana, described variously in the FY 2025-26 report as 350,000 sq. ft. and 600,000 sq. ft. The new unit is explicitly intended to expand capacity to manufacture critical components including **fuzes, mines and inertial navigation systems**. This co-location of fuzing, mine and INS production signals that INS is being treated as a scalable production line rather than a bespoke development activity — a prerequisite for the defence-grade supply chain described above. See [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].

## Strategic Significance

Inertial navigation illustrates a broader pattern in India's defence industrial policy: a component-level capability that is indispensable to a wide range of platforms, difficult to substitute, and therefore a candidate for indigenous development. Government instruments such as the Technology Development Fund — which supports critical defence technologies with grants of up to ₹50 crore per project — and Mission DefSpace, with its 75 Defence Space Challenges, reinforce demand for domestic guidance, navigation and dual-use electronics. For [[entities/drdo]] and the [[entities/indian_navy]], inertial sensing is embedded in guided weapons, naval platforms and autonomous systems. For Apollo, mastering MEMS, FOG and RLG variants widens its addressable role from electronics supplier to guidance sub-system provider — a step on the path described as [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]].

INS also reflects the working-capital and qualification realities of the sector: development is stage-gated, testing is exhaustive, and revenue recognition follows milestone-linked billing. The company's stated risk framework flags technology evolution requiring continuous R&D investment to avoid obsolescence — a risk that applies with particular force to inertial sensor generations.
