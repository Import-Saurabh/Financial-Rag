# Ind AS 103 Business Combinations

**Ind AS 103** is the Indian Accounting Standard that governs the accounting for **business combinations** — transactions in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses. It applies to [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] and is the framework behind the most judgement-intensive item in its FY2025-26 statutory audit.

## Core mechanics

Ind AS 103 requires the **acquisition method**, applied in four steps:

1. **Identify the acquirer** — the entity that obtains control of the acquiree. In a share-purchase transaction the legal acquirer is normally also the accounting acquirer.
2. **Determine the acquisition date** — the date control transfers.
3. **Recognise and measure identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any non-controlling interest** — generally at fair value at the acquisition date.
4. **Recognise and measure goodwill or a gain from a bargain purchase.**

Goodwill is the residual: consideration transferred, plus non-controlling interest, plus fair value of previously held interests, *less* the net identifiable assets acquired. Where the identifiable liabilities assumed **exceed** the identifiable assets (negative net assets), the residual is still goodwill — the excess of consideration over the negative net assets acquired (paragraph 32).

## Measurement period and provisional accounting

Paragraphs 45–50 permit the initial accounting for a business combination to be **provisional** where fair valuation cannot be completed by the reporting date. The acquirer has a **measurement period of up to twelve months** from the acquisition date to finalise the purchase price allocation (PPA). Adjustments within that window are made **retrospectively**, with consequential restatement of comparatives and opening balances.

Goodwill recognised on a provisional basis rests on the **book values** of the acquiree's assets and liabilities rather than their fair values, and is therefore subject to change. Ind AS 103 also requires disclosure that the initial accounting is incomplete for the price allocation and identifies the assets, liabilities and goodwill recognised on a provisional basis.

A distinct branch of the standard covers **business combinations involving entities under common control**, which are accounted for using the **pooling of interest** method rather than the acquisition method — assets and liabilities are reflected at carrying amounts, prior-period financial information is restated as if the combination had occurred from the beginning of the preceding period, and any difference is transferred to a capital reserve. This is the treatment set out in the Group's own accounting policy note.

## Relevance in the Apollo Micro Systems FY2025-26 audit

As disclosed in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]], [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]], the Company's subsidiary **[[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]]** entered into a Share Purchase Agreement dated 02 May 2025 to acquire **100% of the equity share capital of [[entities/idl_explosives]]**. The transaction completed on **15 November 2025** (the acquisition date), on which date control passed, making IDL a wholly-owned **step-down subsidiary** of the Company. IDL is engaged in the manufacture of industrial explosives, accessories and allied products and qualifies as a business under Ind AS 103. The acquisition was undertaken to expand the Group's defence and explosives manufacturing capability and to obtain access to IDL's manufacturing licences and infrastructure (see [[concepts/defence-energetics]] and [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]]). The transaction is disclosed at note 47 of the consolidated financial statements.

At the acquisition date, the identifiable liabilities of the acquiree exceeded its identifiable assets — **negative net assets** — while the fair value of consideration transferred was positive. This produced:

- **Provisional goodwill of ₹14,517.67 lakhs**, being the excess of consideration transferred over the net identifiable (negative) assets acquired, recognised under paragraph 32 of Ind AS 103.
- Pending completion of the PPA, no fair valuation of the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed had been carried out as at the balance sheet date. Management proposed that the PPA exercise — including fair valuation of assets and liabilities and consequent reassessment of goodwill — would be completed within the **twelve-month measurement period** from the acquisition date, i.e. no later than **15 November 2026**, as permitted under paragraphs 45–50.
- The consolidated balance sheet at 31 March 2026 accordingly carries **goodwill of ₹14,519.55 lakhs**, up from ₹1.89 lakhs a year earlier, combining the newly recognised provisional goodwill on the IDL acquisition with pre-existing goodwill on earlier combinations (see [[concepts/goodwill]]).

Negative net assets in the acquiree also contribute to a **negative non-controlling interest** of ₹(487.46) lakhs on the face of the consolidated balance sheet — profit or loss and each component of OCI are attributed to owners and to non-controlling interests even where this results in a deficit balance.

This was elevated to a **[[concepts/key-audit-matters]]** item because:

- The determination of the fair value of consideration transferred involves significant estimation and judgement.
- The quantum of goodwill recognised is material to the financial statements.
- Any subsequent fair valuation during the measurement period could result in a **significant retrospective adjustment to goodwill**, with consequential impact on the opening balance sheet and comparatives.

Audit procedures in response included understanding and evaluating the terms of the share agreement and the basis on which the transaction was accounted for as a business combination under Ind AS 103, evaluating the identification of the acquirer and confirming that the legal acquirer was also the accounting acquirer, agreeing the provisional goodwill to the underlying acquisition-date balance sheet of the acquiree, testing the acquisition journal entries and the recognition of the investment/net assets of the target, evaluating the adequacy of Ind AS 103 disclosures, testing management's future profitability assumptions (including projected cash flows, revenue growth, margins and synergies) supporting the commercial rationale for the acquisition, and obtaining a written representation that no impairment indicators existed for the provisional goodwill at the reporting date.

### Consideration transferred and net cash outflow

Note 47 states the total purchase consideration for the acquisition of IDL at **₹10,700.00 lakhs**, paid in cash on completion. After deducting the cash and cash equivalents acquired (₹1,734.78 lakhs), the **net cash outflow on acquisition was ₹8,965.22 lakhs**. Acquisition-related costs of **₹2.73 lakhs** — legal, due diligence and advisory fees — were recognised as an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss for FY2025-26, rather than capitalised into the cost of the combination.

The direction of travel is telling: the acquisition brought in **substantial assumed debt** (over ₹20,000 lakhs of borrowings) alongside operating assets. That is the structural reason the negative-net-assets arithmetic produces a large goodwill residual rather than a bargain-purchase gain — the consideration paid is pitted against a balance sheet carrying more liabilities than assets. The assumed borrowings also reshape the Group's financing reconciliation (note 15) long before any fair-value uplift is finalised.

The **goodwill of ₹14,517.67 lakhs** is attributed to the assembled workforce, manufacturing know-how and **statutory licences** held by IDL, and to synergies expected from integration with the Group's defence manufacturing business — none of which qualify for separate recognition as an identifiable intangible asset. The goodwill so recognised is **not expected to be deductible for income tax purposes**.

### Provisional accounting and measurement period

As at 31 March 2026 the initial accounting for the business combination remained **incomplete** and the amounts above are accordingly provisional. This is because the Group was still finalising:

- the **independent valuation of property, plant and equipment and identifiable intangible assets**; and
- the assessment of **provisions and contingent liabilities** relating to statutory, regulatory and environmental matters specific to an explosives manufacturing licence holder (see [[concepts/provisions-and-contingent-liabilities]]).

In accordance with Ind AS 103.45–50, the Group will **retrospectively adjust** the provisional amounts recognised as at the acquisition date, with a corresponding adjustment to goodwill, to reflect new information obtained about facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition date. The measurement period will not exceed one year from the acquisition date and will conclude no later than **15 November 2026**. Because the purchase price allocation is provisional, the goodwill itself is provisional and remains subject to adjustment.

### Contribution to Group results and pro forma information

IDL contributed **revenue of ₹13,764.39 lakhs** and a **loss after tax of ₹(262.82) lakhs** to the Group for the period from the acquisition date to 31 March 2026, as included in the consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss for the year. Had the acquisition been completed on **1 April 2025** (the first day of the financial year), the Group's revenue for FY2025-26 would have been **₹1,14,647.77 lakhs** and the Group's profit for the year would have been **₹7,936.79 lakhs**.

### The wider Group structure

Ind AS 103 is also the mechanism through which the Group's subsidiary architecture was assembled. As set out in note 37 of the consolidated financial statements, the Group now comprises:

- **[[entities/ananya_sip_rf_technologies_private_limited]]** — subsidiary, India, 51% ownership. This was the holding Company's FY2020-21 acquisition of control through purchase of equity shares, which was accounted for as a business combination and generated the pre-existing goodwill of ₹1.89 lakhs still on the balance sheet at 31 March 2025.
- **[[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]]** — subsidiary, India, 76% ownership; the acquirer of IDL.
- **[[entities/apollo_strategic_technologies_private_limited]]** — step-down subsidiary, India, 51% ownership.
- **[[entities/idl_explosives]]** — step-down subsidiary, India, 100% ownership, acquired in FY2025-26.

Successive acquisitions explained through Ind AS 103 are what convert a single-segment defence electronics manufacturer into a group with step-down subsidiaries, minority interests and consolidated balance-sheet complexity — the same structure that makes the segment and related-party disclosures meaningful ([[concepts/segment-reporting]], [[concepts/related-party-transactions]], [[concepts/consolidated-financial-statements]]).

## Why it matters

Ind AS 103 is where accounting meets strategy. It is the point at which an acquisition's **price, structure and target quality** are converted into numbers on the balance sheet. Sectors undergoing consolidation — such as Indian defence electronics and energetics (see [[concepts/defence-acquisitions-and-integration]] and [[concepts/defence-energetics]]) — frequently generate:

- **Material goodwill balances** that later become impairment testing obligations (see [[concepts/goodwill]]).
- **Step-down subsidiary structures** with attendant [[concepts/related-party-transactions]] and cross-entity funding exposure.
- **Opening-balance volatility** in the year following acquisition while the PPA remains open.
- **Negative non-controlling interests** where acquired entities carry net liabilities.
- **Large tranches of assumed debt** that reshape the Group's financing reconciliation long before any fair-value uplift is finalised.

Investors should therefore treat provisional goodwill as **soft capital** until the measurement period closes, and watch for restatement risk in the following year's comparatives. Integrity of the acquisition-date numbers is also an [[concepts/internal-financial-controls]] matter, and the revenue and inventory judgements that surround a newly consolidated business link back to [[concepts/revenue-recognition]] and [[concepts/inventory-obsolescence]]. Employee-benefit obligations assumed on acquisition are a further integration item (see [[concepts/employee-benefits]]).
