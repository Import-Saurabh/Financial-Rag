# Defense Indigenization and Make in India

Defense indigenization is the strategic effort to design, develop, and manufacture defense systems within India, reducing dependence on foreign imports. The Make in India initiative amplifies this by positioning India as a global manufacturing hub, with defense as a core sector.

## Policy Context

India has long been one of the world's largest defense importers. The Make in India initiative, launched by the Government of India, prioritizes self-reliance (Atmanirbhar Bharat) in defense production. Policies such as the Defense Acquisition Procedure (DAP), positive indigenization lists, and foreign direct investment liberalization encourage private-sector participation and technology transfer.

The structural shift is now measurable. According to the Annual Report 2025-26 (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]] and [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part3]]), nearly 65% of defense equipment is now produced domestically, compared with an import dependence of 65–70% earlier. Indigenous defense production has expanded nearly fourfold from ₹43,746 crore in FY 2013-14 to approximately ₹1.78 lakh crore in FY 2025-26, a 15.6% year-on-year increase. The Directors' Report for FY 2025-26 reiterates that this figure is an all-time high and more than double the output of five years ago, while defense exports reached a record ₹38,424 crore, registering growth of 62.66% over the previous fiscal, with Indian defense equipment now reaching more than 80 countries. The defense budget for 2026-27 stands at ₹7.85 lakh crore, an unambiguous signal of the nation's long-term commitment to military modernisation. The Government has set a target of ₹3 lakh crore in defense production and ₹50,000 crore in exports by 2029, reflecting confidence that the trajectory established under Atmanirbhar Bharat is sustainable and scalable.

The draft Defense Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2026 proposes simpler acquisition categories, stronger support for indigenous design and development, and higher indigenous content requirements of up to 60%, further strengthening domestic manufacturing capabilities.

## Key Pillars

- **Domestic R&D and manufacturing** – building indigenous design and production capacity
- **Private sector participation** – enabling companies beyond state-owned entities
- **Technology transfer** – collaborative development with agencies like [[entities/drdo]] and the absorption of DRDO designs into deployable, inducible systems
- **Export promotion** – leveraging domestic capabilities for global markets, see [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]]

### Budget Support and Indigenization Mandates

The Union Budget for FY 2026-27 allocated ₹7.8–7.85 lakh crore to the Ministry of Defence, the highest across all ministries and approximately 14.28–14.7% of total central government expenditure. Of this, ₹2.19 lakh crore is capital outlay under Defence Services, with roughly **75% of the capital acquisition budget reserved for domestic industry**. The indigenization lists issued by the Ministry of Defence are no longer aspirations on paper but mandates backed by timelines and budgets.

The sixth Positive Indigenisation List (PIL) notified by the Department of Defence Production comprises 405 strategically important items with an estimated business potential of ₹3,070 crore, covering Line Replaceable Units, sub-systems, sub-assemblies, spares, components and raw materials. More than 15,700 defense items have been successfully indigenised over the last five years, yielding an estimated import substitution value of about ₹9,000 crore.

The Annual Report 2025-26 stresses that these mandates now channel procurement toward domestic manufacturers. Deepening indigenisation through the Positive Indigenisation Lists, the Development cum Production Partner (DcPP) framework and DAP reforms is explicitly listed among the sector's principal opportunities, alongside the Government's target of ₹3 lakh crore of defence production and ₹50,000 crore of exports by 2029.

### Government Funding and Technology Development Schemes

Indigenization is underwritten by a widening set of state funding instruments (see [[concepts/government-defence-funding-schemes]]). The **Technology Development Fund (TDF)**, a Ministry of Defence instrument, extends financial and technical support to public and private enterprises — with particular emphasis on startups and MSMEs — for critical defence technologies, providing grants of up to ₹50 crore per project. Several technologies have been developed under TDF, and two project systems have flown in space as part of PSLV missions. As of June 2026, 80 projects with a total cost of ₹334 crore were under implementation.

**Mission DefSpace** operates under the iDEX framework to promote indigenous development of dual-use technologies for India's defence-space ecosystem. It has introduced 75 Defence Space Challenges across five domains — launch systems, satellite systems, communication and payload systems, ground systems, and software systems — enabling startups, MSMEs and research institutions to co-develop technologies aligned with the operational requirements of the Defence Space Agency and the Indian armed forces. Mission DefSpace represents a deliberate effort to mobilise private-sector capability in a domain that had previously been the exclusive preserve of government agencies.

### Privatisation of Production

A significant policy shift is underway: India is undertaking a complete transfer of DRDO-developed conventional missile technology — covering air-to-air, anti-ship and tactical ballistic missiles — to private companies through competitive bidding, aimed at scaling affordable mass production capacity. In parallel, [[entities/isro]] is handing over production of launch vehicles such as the PSLV and LVM-3 to private industry, allowing both organizations to concentrate on research and advanced technology development.

## Space and Dual-Use Programmes

Indigenization increasingly extends beyond conventional defence into civil and dual-use space capability (see [[concepts/indian-space-programme]]). **GAGAN** (GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation), an indigenous Satellite-Based Augmentation System co-developed by [[entities/isro]] and the Airports Authority of India, enhances GPS accuracy and provides integrity information for safer aircraft navigation. Globally certified, it facilitates precise satellite-based landings and supports operations beyond commercial aviation, strengthening sovereign navigation infrastructure and India's push toward technological self-reliance in satellite navigation.

The lunar programme continues to expand after Chandrayaan-3 made India the first country to achieve a soft landing near the Moon's south pole in 2023. **Chandrayaan-4** is scheduled for 2027 with the objective of collecting lunar samples and returning them to Earth, while **Chandrayaan-5** will explore water and other volatile materials near the lunar south pole. The **Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS)**, a key pillar of Space Vision 2047, is India's planned five-module space station in Low Earth Orbit engineered for long-duration human spaceflight and advanced scientific research; the Union Cabinet formally approved development and launch of the first module, BAS-01, by 2028 as part of the expanded Gaganyaan programme.

On the demand side, expanding requirements for space-grade electronics and dual-use technologies under Mission DefSpace and related programmes constitute a growing opportunity for private suppliers.

### Private Sector Incentivisation

Strategic government initiatives continue to support private-sector contribution to the ecosystem. Notable programmes include the liberalised Foreign Direct Investment policy, the IN-SPACe Seed Fund Scheme and the Pre-incubation Entrepreneurship Programme, alongside the rollout of a ₹1,000 crore Venture Capital Fund and a ₹500 crore Technology Adoption Fund.

## Apollo Micro Systems as a Case Study

[[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] exemplifies the new wave of private defense manufacturers. In its Q1 FY26 earnings call (see [[summaries/2025_Aug_2025_Transcript]]), the company highlighted:

- Contribution of roughly 63% of electronics and electromechanical work for DRDO missile programs
- Involvement in key programs: [[entities/migm]], [[entities/qrsam]], [[entities/project_kusha]], [[entities/pinaka]], and [[entities/brahmos]]
- Acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] to localize explosives and warhead manufacturing
- A maiden export order for advanced avionics, demonstrating that indigenization can lead to global competitiveness
- Expansion of in-house RF capabilities and cargo drone development

The FY 2025-26 Annual Report marks a further step change in Apollo's indigenization journey (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1]]):

- **Lifetime Arms Manufacturing Licence from DPIIT**, with no expiry, authorising the manufacture, assembly, integration and proof-testing of complete strategic weapon systems — Missiles, ATGMs, Torpedoes, Underwater Mines, Aerial Bombs, Rockets, Loitering Munitions, Chaffs, Flares and Decoys — alongside licensed Unmanned Aerial Systems, Inertial Navigation Systems and Radar Equipment (see [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]]).
- **Completion of the 100% acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]]** through [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] for ₹107 crore in an all-cash transaction, extending participation into [[concepts/defence-energetics]] with an industrial licence for HMX and TNT.
- **Greenfield and brownfield expansion**, including additional land allotted by [[entities/tsiic]] taking the Hardware Park footprint to ~10.68 acres with a ₹300 crore integrated weapon-platform facility, and an MoU with the Government of Telangana for a ~₹1,500 crore high-energetics complex.
- **First export order** received during the year, positioning Apollo as an emerging Tier-I OEM able to convert domestic capability into international revenue.
- **Tripartite alliance with the [[entities/indian_navy]] and [[entities/iit_madras]]**, plus an approved collaborative R&D partnership with [[entities/bharat_electronics_limited]] and MoUs with [[entities/munitions_india_limited]], [[entities/bharat_dynamics_limited]] and [[entities/grse]].
- **Indigenous R&D outcomes** including the Mini Torpedo, a Safety and Detonation Device (SDD) for the Indian Navy, and underwater technologies tied to [[concepts/underwater-warfare]].

Part 2 of the Annual Report 2025-26 (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part2]]) adds further detail on the company's deepening indigenisation footprint. The integration of [[entities/idl_explosives]] — completed on 15 November 2025 through [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] — has been fully absorbed into the Group's operations, financial reporting and safety framework, ending the year as a consolidated business from that date. A comprehensive DPIIT licence now covers UAVs, INS systems, radars, torpedoes, underwater mines, ATGMs, decoys, chaffs and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions, while a separate industrial licence supports the manufacture of high explosives including TNT and RDX through the IDL Explosives subsidiary. The Company received Transfer of Technology for advanced electro-optical tracking systems and for Directed Energy Weapons (DEWs), including laser-based variants (see [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]]). Vehicle-mounted counter-drone systems (VMCDS) are advancing, with field trials scheduled for FY 2026-27 (see [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]]).

Part 3 of the Annual Report 2025-26 (see [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part3]]) details the Company's deepening R&D pipeline and its self-described transition from a development-centric electronics house into a vertically integrated weapon systems enterprise operating as a system-of-systems integrator. The Directors' Report states the strategic focus as the shift from qualified subsystem supplier to prime contractor in select verticals — precision-guided munitions, underwater weapon systems, embedded guidance and control, and smart energetics — positioning AMS to take end-to-end responsibility for complete weapon platforms rather than discrete subsystems. During FY 2025-26 the Company invested ₹7,253.39 lakhs in R&D, with several programmes maturing from development into realisation:

- **[[entities/mini_torpedo]]** — a compact, lightweight underwater weapon integrating in-house acoustic homing, guidance electronics, actuation and safety-and-arming technologies into a single platform, deployable from surface ships, helicopters and unmanned systems.
- **[[entities/vehicle_mounted_counter_drone_system]]** — a mobile, integrated solution combining radar, RF detection and electro-optical sensors with soft-kill and hard-kill countermeasures for convoy protection, forward-deployed formations and mobile air-defence gaps (see [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]]).
- **[[entities/limpet_mines]]** — compact underwater explosive devices for covert attachment to ship hulls by combat divers and special forces, integrating advanced safety-and-arming, programmable time-delay fuzing and anti-removal features.
- **[[entities/moored_mine]]** — an indigenously developed sea mine anchored to the seabed with a multi-influence signal processing system, programmable target logic and secure arming, positioning AMS as an offeror of all types of underwater mines in naval warfare.
- **[[entities/asw_weapon_platform]]** — the Company's first complete weapon system designed entirely in-house, integrating acoustic homing, guidance and control, actuation, warhead integration and safety-and-arming for anti-submarine warfare, developed in alignment with the operational requirements of the [[entities/indian_navy]].

The R&D division continued its engagement with the Ministry of Defence's indigenisation frameworks, carrying forward technology development under a Make-II project awarded by the Indian Army and participating in further Make proposals across the Tri-Services, while expanding its internal technology roadmap in terminal guidance, seekers, safety-and-arming mechanisms and jam-resistant navigation.

The Group's corporate perimeter now comprises two subsidiaries — [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] and [[entities/ananya_sip_rf_technologies_private_limited]] — and two step-down subsidiaries, [[entities/apollo_strategic_technologies_private_limited]] and [[entities/idl_explosives]], with [[entities/shauryastra_defence_systems_private_limited]] also within the related-party perimeter. A material post-year event was the execution of a Share Purchase Agreement on 9 July 2026 to acquire 2,22,21,735 equity shares — representing 41.33% of paid-up equity — of [[entities/premier_explosives_limited]] for an aggregate cash consideration of approximately ₹1,550 crore, which triggered an open offer for a further 26% at ₹698 per share (aggregating about ₹975.64 crore). The transaction, subject to regulatory approvals, combines complementary capabilities in defense systems and energetic materials, enabling operational efficiencies, enhanced R&D and broader participation in defense and space-related programmes — deepening sovereign expertise in energetic materials and strengthening the Group's defence manufacturing capability.

At the group level, AMS is involved in over 150 indigenous defence programmes, with solutions qualified for inclusion in the production-phase supply chain under the DcPP framework, and is an approved vendor for [[entities/drdo]] and the DPSUs. It currently operates two Hyderabad facilities (Mallapur and Adibatla) and is building a new integrated facility at Hardware Park-II to expand capacity for critical components including fuzes, mines and inertial navigation systems. Diversification across the land, air and sea domains — together with industrial explosives and a growing export franchise — reduces dependence on any single programme or customer.

These efforts align with the government's goal of building a self-sufficient defense ecosystem, with private firms increasingly acting as tier-1 suppliers and system integrators (see [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]]).

## Supply Chains and Industrial Integration

Successful indigenization requires robust [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]]. Apollo's partnerships with [[entities/bharat_dynamics_limited]] and [[entities/drdo]] illustrate how private companies integrate into complex missile and torpedo programs. The company's capex expansion and working-capital improvements reflect the industrial investment needed to scale domestic production. Apollo's procurement strategy is explicitly indigenisation-first: imports are substituted with qualified Indian vendors and in-house development, dual-sourcing and strategic inventory guard against supply disruption, localisation clauses are built into partnerships with foreign OEMs, and the Group is integrating backward into critical raw materials and compounds through its entry into energetics.

A recurring enabler of this integration is [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]]. Converting a DRDO design into a deployable, inducible system requires building the complete industrialisation chain around it — design-for-manufacture, qualified manufacturing and test infrastructure, a defense-grade supply chain, quality certification aligned to defense assurance agencies, and lifecycle support. This is what converts technology receipts into repeat production orders. Apollo's electro-optical tracking and directed-energy weapon receipts illustrate the pattern: a technology transfer is the entry point to a much larger industrialisation and qualification effort.

## Challenges and Outlook

Despite progress, indigenization faces hurdles: technology gaps, funding constraints, and regulatory complexity, alongside dependence on export-control regimes and exposure to currency swings. The sector's own risk register highlights dependence on government procurement cycles, where order finalisation and execution timelines can be long and uneven; intensifying competition from established DPSUs and new private-sector entrants; volatility in raw material, component and energy prices that can compress margins; rapid technology evolution requiring continuous R&D investment to avoid obsolescence; cybersecurity and data-protection risks inherent to defence programmes; and geopolitical developments that may disrupt supply chains or alter procurement priorities. Regulatory and licensing obligations have widened as firms move into explosives manufacturing under the Explosives Act, 1884 and allied rules.

However, with sustained policy support and private-sector commitment, India is steadily building credible indigenous defense capabilities. The previous decade focused on laying the foundation of Atmanirbharta; the next decade is geared towards vertical velocity, with defense exports reaching a record ₹38,424 crore in FY 2025-26 and extending to over 80 countries, and a Government target of ₹50,000 crore in defense exports by 2029. The strategic ambition for leading domestic firms is to ascend the value chain from component and sub-system supply toward becoming global Original Equipment Manufacturers, entering heavy defence domains such as armament electronics, fire control systems, battle tanks, infantry combat vehicles and artillery platforms, while converting indigenisation expertise into export-ready systems.
