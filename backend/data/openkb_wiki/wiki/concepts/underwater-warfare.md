# Underwater Warfare and Naval Weapons

Underwater warfare covers the systems, sensors and munitions used to detect, deter, track and destroy threats beneath the sea — and to protect one's own waters from the same. It spans naval mines (bottom, moored and limpet), torpedoes and their homing systems, anti-submarine warfare (ASW) rockets and weapon platforms, autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and the ruggedised electronics that make all of them work in a pressure-tolerant, shock-hardened environment.

For a defence electronics company, underwater warfare is one of the hardest qualification environments in the industry: it demands underwater acoustics, sonar signal processing, sea-trial regimes and naval armament inspection, and the global pool of qualified vendors is extremely narrow. This makes success in the domain a durable competitive moat and a natural extension of a Tier-I electronics supplier into platform-level [[concepts/tier_1_defense_oem_integration]]. During FY2025-26 the centre of gravity shifted further: the group now describes itself as moving from qualified subsystem supplier to a **complete underwater weapon systems house**, with several products maturing from development into realisation.

## Naval Mines

Naval mines are an "unseen threat" — a relatively low-cost capability that lets any maritime nation defend its waters regardless of naval strength, forcing hostile submarines and surface ships away from the coastline and out toward open sea. The [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part1|Apollo Micro Systems FY2025-26 annual report]] describes four families:

- **Multi-Influence Ground Mine (MIGM)** — [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]] is the approved production agency for the MIGM "Vighna", developed under the DRDO DcPP (Development cum Production Partner) route. See [[entities/migm]] and [[entities/drdo]]. MIGM is designed to sharpen the [[entities/indian_navy]]'s edge against modern stealth ships and submarines. India reportedly joins the United States, Russia, China and Italy in holding this capability.
- **Moored mines** — indigenously developed, anchored to the seabed and suspended at a pre-determined depth for area denial against surface and sub-surface targets, used where waters are too deep for bottom mines. At its core is the company's advanced **multi-influence signal processing** system, capable of detecting acoustic, magnetic and pressure signatures and discriminating genuine targets from countermeasures. The design incorporates programmable target logic, secure arming and extended underwater endurance for prolonged deployment, and is intended for defensive mining of harbours, chokepoints and strategic waters — a potent sea-denial capability. Recent conflicts illustrated the concept's real-world leverage: the Maham-3 moored mine, using magnetic and acoustic sensors, was reportedly among mines identified in the Strait of Hormuz, where fewer than a dozen mines were enough to disrupt commercial shipping, raise insurance costs and draw naval forces from multiple countries into clearance operations. See [[entities/moored_mine]].
- **Land mines (anti-tank)** — PRACHAND, an anti-tank munition built for full-width attack, described as the first line of defence against enemy infantry, armoured columns and infiltrators. New variants are in development using AI sensor fusion.
- **Limpet mines** — compact underwater explosive devices designed to be covertly attached to the hulls of ships and maritime assets by combat divers and special forces, held in place by magnets; man-portable and built in several mission-specific variants. The company's indigenous design integrates advanced **safety-and-arming** mechanisms, **programmable time-delay fuzing** and **anti-removal features** to ensure operational effectiveness and handler safety, with reliability under extreme underwater conditions and secure arming protocols. Backward integration within the Apollo Group provides access to qualified energetic materials, enabling end-to-end realisation of the weapon. The product addresses the specialised requirements of naval special operations and harbour-denial missions. See [[entities/limpet_mines]].

With this portfolio the company positions itself as a unique player offering **all types of underwater mines** in naval warfare, and as a key indigenous partner in mine warfare. Combat trials for the Indian Navy have been completed successfully. The group's expanded manufacturing footprint is being built to support this portfolio directly: the new integrated facility at Hardware Park-II (in addition to the two existing Hyderabad units at Mallapur and Adibatla) is explicitly intended, alongside fuzes and inertial navigation systems, for the manufacture of **mines**. A DPIIT industrial licence secured during FY2025-26 broadens the licensed product envelope to cover **torpedoes, underwater mines**, UAVs, INS systems, radars, ATGMs, decoys, chaffs and flares, aerial bombs, rockets, missiles and loitering munitions — see [[concepts/defence-manufacturing-licensing]].

## Torpedoes and Torpedo Homing

Torpedo homing is singled out as one of the toughest qualification environments in defence electronics, requiring:

- Underwater acoustics and sonar signal-processing expertise;
- Pressure-tolerant, shock-hardened electronics packaging and extreme reliability engineering;
- Qualification through naval armament inspection and sea-trial regimes;
- Years of joint development with DRDO laboratories and the Indian Navy.

The company reported development of a **Mini Torpedo** during FY2025-26. The Mini Torpedo is a compact, lightweight underwater weapon designed for engagement of small and mid-size sub-surface threats, including midget submarines and underwater swimmer delivery vehicles. Developed indigenously by the company, the system integrates in-house **acoustic homing, guidance electronics, actuation and safety-and-arming technologies** into a single platform. Its reduced form factor enables deployment from a variety of launch platforms — surface ships, helicopters and unmanned systems — and the programme leverages the group's proven building blocks in signal processing and underwater sensing. It represents a critical capability in littoral and shallow-water warfare, an area of growing operational priority, and underscores the company's evolution into a complete underwater weapon systems house. See [[entities/mini_torpedo]].

The company also continued development of further underwater electronics including **torpedo homing systems** and **autonomous maritime platforms**, and during FY2025-26 specifically invested in the development of a **Homing System for Light Weight Torpedoes**, in addition to actuators for SWARM and missiles, seeker signal-processing systems and moored-mine signal-processing systems. A specialised facility for **acoustic profile testing** is being built to accelerate progress. The DPIIT licence noted above now formally covers the manufacture of torpedoes, aligning licensing with the development roadmap.

There is also a large replacement opportunity: the Indian Navy currently operates a substantial inventory of torpedoes imported from Western and Eastern-origin countries, creating scope for indigenous homing systems in an area where the company already holds a strong presence.

## Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW)

The **ASW Weapon Platform** is described as the group's first complete weapon system designed entirely in-house — a ground-up indigenous development, not a reverse-engineering effort. The programme integrates the company's core competencies in acoustic homing, guidance and control, actuation, warhead integration and safety-and-arming into a unified anti-submarine weapon intended for engagement of sub-surface threats. It is being developed in alignment with the operational requirements of the [[entities/indian_navy]], and significant progress was achieved during the year across design, development and subsystem realisation phases. The programme stands as a defining demonstration of the transition from subsystem supplier to complete weapon platform developer, anchoring the company's long-term ambition in the underwater warfare domain. See [[entities/asw_weapon_platform]].

The report flags a "modern threat frontier": missile-system demand is expected to rise with the induction of large numbers of ships, submarines and aircraft, and there is a critical need to advance indigenous **ASW rockets** to counter increasingly sophisticated underwater threats. ASW rockets are among the products slated for the new Unit III manufacturing, integration and testing facility in Telangana.

## Adjacent Underwater Capabilities

- **Autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs)** and sensor suites for underwater autonomous platforms.
- **Safety and Detonation Device (SDD)** designed indigenously for the Indian Navy under Atmanirbhar Bharat problem statements and formally handed over to the Navy.
- **Inertial navigation for underwater platforms** — MEMS-based INS, Fiber Optic Gyro (FOG) and Ring Laser Gyro systems as foundational guidance technology for guided weapons, UAVs and submersibles. See [[concepts/inertial-navigation-systems]].
- **Explosives and energetics** — warheads, high explosives (TNT, RDX, HMX, PETN), fuzes and Safety & Arming mechanisms. The group's acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] (completed 17 November 2025, when the 76%-owned [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]] acquired 100% of its equity and it became a step-down subsidiary) and the industrial licence obtained for high explosives including TNT and RDX extend this base directly into naval munitions. The subsequent move to acquire a controlling stake in [[entities/premier_explosives_limited]] (41.33% via a July 2026 Share Purchase Agreement, plus a 26% open offer) further deepens sovereign expertise in energetic materials for defence and space programmes. See [[concepts/defence-energetics]] and [[concepts/transfer-of-technology]].

## Strategic Significance

Underwater warfare sits at the intersection of several recurring themes in the defence knowledge base:

- **Indigenisation** — the MIGM "Vighna" is described as India's first indigenous naval mine of its class, developed under a DRDO DcPP arrangement; the ASW Weapon Platform is the group's first fully in-house weapon system; and the wider push is captured in [[concepts/defense_indigenization]] and [[concepts/concepts-defense_indigenization]].
- **Energetics integration** — the acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] and the pending acquisition of [[entities/premier_explosives_limited]] extend the group into the high-energy materials that give underwater munitions their effect, with backward integration into qualified energetics enabling end-to-end realisation of weapons such as the limpet mine.
- **Supply chains and qualified vendors** — extremely narrow global vendor pools make this domain a textbook case of [[concepts/defense-program-supply-chains]].
- **Exports** — mines, torpedoes and underwater platforms are among the categories eligible for export under recent licensing, tying into [[concepts/defense-exports-and-global-market-entry]].
- **Counter-drone and directed energy** are the above-surface parallels: layered sensing, effectors and command-and-control, as described in [[concepts/counter-drone-systems]] and [[concepts/directed-energy-weapons]]. The company's [[entities/vehicle_mounted_counter_drone_system]] applies the same sensor-fusion and real-time-processing strengths to mobile air defence.
- **R&D as the value engine** — the group invested ₹7,253.39 lakhs in R&D in FY2025-26, with underwater programmes (torpedo homing, moored-mine signal processing, ASW platform) among the principal recipients. It also engages the Ministry of Defence's indigenisation frameworks, including a Make-II project awarded by the Indian Army and participation in further Make proposals across the Tri-Services.
- **Partnership ecosystem** — development depends on DRDO laboratories, the Indian Navy, and partners such as [[entities/bharat_dynamics_limited]], [[entities/bharat_electronics_limited]] and [[entities/grse]]. During FY2025-26 the group deepened this ecosystem with a tripartite alliance with the [[entities/indian_navy]] and [[entities/iit_madras]], and a signed Memorandum of Understanding with [[entities/grse]] — reinforcing the naval-systems collaboration layer behind the underwater portfolio.
