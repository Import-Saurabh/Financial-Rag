# Inventory Obsolescence and Valuation Risk

Inventory obsolescence and valuation risk is the risk that inventory carried on the balance sheet is overstated because goods have become obsolete, slow-moving, or worth less than their recorded cost. In defence electronics — where stock is often built to specific customer specifications and long programme timelines — this risk is heightened: an item that is perfectly good inventory for one missile or avionics programme may have no alternative buyer if that programme is cancelled, deferred or redesigned.

## Why it matters

- **Valuation is judgement-based.** Ind AS requires inventory to be carried at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV). Assessing NRV demands management estimates about future selling prices, order books and the likelihood of programme continuation.
- **Obsolescence is technology-driven.** Electronics components can be superseded by newer designs, and customer-specific stock cannot easily be redeployed.
- **Physical verification is difficult.** Work-in-progress and specialised stock are hard to count and value accurately, especially where components are embedded in assemblies.
- **Provisioning affects profit.** Under-provisioning flatters earnings and net assets today but creates a larger write-down later.

## Key details from the FY2025-26 annual report

In [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part6]], the statutory auditor [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] identified inventories as a **[[concepts/key-audit-matters]]** for [[entities/apollo_micro_systems_limited]]:

- Inventories stood at **₹76,000.75 lakhs as at 31 March 2026**, representing **50.53% of total current assets** and **33.13% of total assets** — a very large balance relative to the balance sheet (total assets ₹2,29,423.66 lakhs).
- The prior-year balance was ₹60,058.83 lakhs, so inventory grew by roughly ₹15,942 lakhs during the year.
- The auditor flagged that "with such a huge volume of inventories there is a risk of obsolescence. Since the inventories are specific for customers."
- The matter is covered in note 7 of the standalone financial statements.

### Composition and movement of inventory (Note 26–27, [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part8]])

The FY2025-26 notes to the standalone statements give more granularity on how the balance is composed and how it moved during the year — all of which shapes the obsolescence exposure:

- **Raw materials** closed at **₹40,075.10 lakhs** (opening ₹33,679.27 lakhs), after purchases of ₹64,007.29 lakhs and directly attributable costs of ₹1,635.06 lakhs. **Cost of materials consumed** for the year was **₹59,246.52 lakhs** (prior year ₹43,567.13 lakhs), showing sharply higher input consumption alongside the accumulated raw-material buffer.
- **Work-in-process** closed at **₹35,470.99 lakhs** (opening ₹26,356.93 lakhs) and **finished goods** at **₹428.98 lakhs** (opening ₹22.63 lakhs). Together, closing finished goods plus WIP was **₹35,899.97 lakhs** versus ₹26,379.56 lakhs at the start.
- The **increase in inventories of finished goods and work-in-progress** charged to the statement of profit and loss was **₹(9,520.40) lakhs** (prior year ₹(3,870.53) lakhs) — i.e. the company tied up a further ~₹9,520 lakhs in semi-finished and finished stock during the year. The prior-year comparative figure is comparatively modest, so the build-up accelerated materially in FY2025-26.

The heavy concentration of value in **WIP** — rather than finished goods — is significant: semi-finished defence electronics carry the highest obsolescence and estimation risk, since they cannot be sold as-is and their recoverability depends entirely on the underlying programme being completed at the expected cost and price.

### Consolidated group inventory

The consolidated financial statements in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part9]] confirm that the same matter is a **[[concepts/key-audit-matters]]** at group level. Consolidated inventories stood at **₹79,528.92 lakhs as at 31 March 2026** (prior year ₹60,063.61 lakhs), equivalent to **47.76% of total current assets** and **33.58% of total assets** (consolidated total assets ₹2,36,812.03 lakhs). The consolidated figure exceeds the standalone balance because it brings in the subsidiaries — including the inventory of the newly acquired [[entities/idl_explosives]] — and the auditor again cited the same reasoning: "Given the significant judgment and estimates involved in the management assessments, the inventories are identified as key audit matter." The matter is covered in note 7 of the consolidated financial statements. On the consolidated cash flow statement, the movement in inventories absorbed **₹(16,348.63) lakhs** of working capital, reinforcing the group-wide working-capital drag.

## Auditor's procedures

The auditor's response to this risk, broadly the same in the standalone and consolidated reports, included:

- Assessing the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls over physical verification, identification of obsolete and slow-moving inventory, inventory ageing monitoring and provisioning.
- Reviewing management's judgements on obsolescence risk at the time of material procurement, incorporating technical expertise and assessment of present and future inventory condition.
- Testing controls over classification, valuation and valuation models.
- Reviewing the physical verification policy and management's count documentation.
- Sample testing orders received, purchases and cost accumulation in the value of stock in process.
- Comparing cost of inventory with estimated net realisable value using actual selling prices around and after year-end.
- Detailed analysis of the **order book** to check whether work-in-progress plus finished goods are aligned with the value of pending orders.
- Assessing adequacy of disclosures.

The auditor concluded that the procedures did not identify costs that were inappropriately capitalised in the related [[concepts/capital-work-in-progress]] matter, but the inventory balance remains inherently estimate-sensitive.

## Practical implications

For readers of the annual report, the size and specificity of the inventory balance means the quality of earnings depends materially on whether provisioning is adequate. A future programme deferral, design change or customer cancellation could trigger significant write-downs, directly hitting profitability and net worth. Watch for:

- The level and trend of inventory as a percentage of revenue and total assets, and the split between raw materials, WIP and finished goods — the growing WIP balance is the sharpest signal of accumulating estimation risk.
- Consistency between the standalone and consolidated inventory balances, and the incremental stock brought on by subsidiaries.
- The pace of the finished-goods-and-WIP charge to the P&L (₹9,520.40 lakhs in FY2025-26 versus ₹3,870.53 lakhs a year earlier), which reflects how fast stock is being built ahead of revenue recognition.
- Any change to the provisioning policy or ageing thresholds.
- Commentary on order-book coverage of work-in-progress.
- Whether the IDL Explosives acquisition brings further customer-specific inventory onto the group balance sheet.
