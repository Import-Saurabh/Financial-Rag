# Capital Work in Progress (CWIP)

**Capital Work in Progress (CWIP)** represents capital expenditure incurred on tangible assets that are under construction, installation, testing or commissioning and are **not yet ready for their intended use** at the balance sheet date. CWIP is the transitional balance-sheet category between incurring expenditure and capitalising it into [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]].

For a capital-goods manufacturer scaling capacity rapidly — as both the Apollo Micro Systems group and [[entities/mtar-technologies]] are doing — the size and turnover of the CWIP balance is one of the clearest indicators of how far a capacity-expansion programme has actually progressed, as distinct from how much has merely been budgeted or committed.

## Recognition and Measurement

CWIP is carried at cost, which comprises:

- Directly attributable acquisition and construction costs
- Borrowing costs eligible for capitalisation under Ind AS 23
- Other expenses directly attributable to bringing the asset to its intended use

**No depreciation is charged** on CWIP — accumulated depreciation on CWIP is nil in every period presented. Once an asset is ready for its intended use, the accumulated cost is transferred to the appropriate category of property, plant and equipment and depreciation commences (straight-line method over the useful lives prescribed in Schedule II to the Companies Act, 2013).

Advances paid towards acquisition of property, plant and equipment that are outstanding at the reporting date are disclosed separately as **capital advances** under other non-current assets, not within CWIP. For the Apollo Micro Systems group these amounted to ₹1,682.57 lakhs as at 31 March 2026 (31 March 2025: ₹830.87 lakhs).

The **capitalisation event itself** is a discrete accounting decision, not a gradual one: expenditure accumulates in CWIP at cost, and the whole balance attributable to a completed asset moves into property, plant and equipment in the period the asset becomes ready for use. This can make a single quarter's balance sheet look materially different from the preceding one without any new cash having been spent in that quarter.

## CWIP Movements

The Apollo Micro Systems group classifies CWIP into two streams: **testing tools and instruments / projects in progress**, and **civil work in progress**.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| As at 1 April 2025 | 2,600.98 | 4,627.26 | 7,228.24 |

CWIP is carried at cost only — accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment are nil as at 31 March 2026 and 31 March 2025, and the net carrying amount therefore equals cost in each period.

The addition of ₹2,868.43 lakhs to civil work in progress arose from acquisitions and forms part of the group's wider business-combination activity during FY2025-26, which also produced [[concepts/goodwill]] of ₹14,517.66 lakhs under [[concepts/ind-as-103-business-combinations]]. That activity centred on [[entities/apollo_defence_industries_private_limited]]'s acquisition of [[entities/idl_explosives]] on 15 November 2025; the **provisional** purchase price allocation disclosed in the FY2025-26 notes attributes ₹2,309.28 lakhs of CWIP to the identifiable assets acquired, a figure that remains subject to adjustment within the measurement period (ending no later than 15 November 2026).

In the consolidated notes the same amounts are presented under a single **"Projects in progress"** line — ₹15,002.14 lakhs aged under 1 year, ₹3,444.56 lakhs at 1–2 years, ₹508.22 lakhs at 2–3 years and ₹674.48 lakhs beyond 3 years, totalling **₹19,629.40 lakhs** as at 31 March 2026 (31 March 2025: ₹6,045.54 lakhs, ₹508.22 lakhs, ₹165.22 lakhs and ₹509.26 lakhs, totalling **₹7,228.24 lakhs**).

No project in CWIP had completion overdue or cost exceeding its original plan as at either reporting date, and there were no temporarily suspended projects. The group also confirmed it held **no intangible assets under development** as at the reporting date.

## Commitments and Capitalised Borrowing Costs

- Under agreements to purchase property, plant and equipment, the group was committed to spend **₹2,130.91 lakhs** as at 31 March 2026 and ₹561.55 lakhs as at 31 March 2025 (net of capital advances already paid). The FY2025-26 commitment relates principally to construction of a new factory building at Shamshabad, disclosed as a commitment under [[concepts/provisions-and-contingent-liabilities]].
- Interest cost capitalised on qualifying assets was **₹1,158.96 lakhs** in FY2025-26 and ₹433.44 lakhs in FY2024-25, at average capitalisation rates of approximately **7.38%** and 10.18% respectively.
- All property, plant and equipment (including CWIP eventually capitalised) are under charge created by the company for loans availed from banks and non-banking financial companies.

Committed-but-unspent capex, and capex that is planned but not yet contracted, sit entirely outside CWIP. A multi-year expansion programme therefore spans three distinct disclosure layers: **announced or guided spend** (management commentary), **contractual commitments** (commitments note), and **CWIP** (expenditure actually incurred on assets not yet in use).

## CWIP in the Apollo Micro Systems Group (FY2025-26)

Per [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part10]] and confirmed in [[summaries/2026_Annual_Report_2026_part11]], the Apollo Micro Systems group recorded CWIP of **₹19,629.40 lakhs** as at 31 March 2026 (up from ₹7,228.24 lakhs as at 31 March 2025), comprising:

| Component | Amount (₹ lakhs) |
|---|---:|
| Testing tools and instruments / projects in progress | 2,341.49 |
| Civil work in progress | 17,287.91 |
| **Total** | **19,629.40** |

The sharp year-on-year increase in CWIP (alongside growth in property, plant and equipment from ₹17,227.13 lakhs to ₹31,525.89 lakhs) reflects significant capital expenditure during the year tied to manufacturing capacity expansion.

## CWIP and Capitalisation at MTAR Technologies (Q1 FY27)

[[entities/mtar-technologies]] provides a complementary illustration of the *exit* from CWIP rather than the build-up. On the Q1 FY27 earnings call (30 July 2026), CFO [[entities/gunneswara-rao]] clarified that **₹80 crore of capex was capitalised during the quarter**, but that this amount was *not spent* in the quarter — it had been sitting in the capital work-in-progress line item in earlier periods and became capitalised (i.e. transferred into fixed assets) in Q1 FY27. Actual cash capex in the quarter was only **₹30–35 crore**, a figure separately corroborated in response to an analyst question.

This distinction matters for reading the accounts: the ₹80 crore appears as an addition to [[concepts/property-plant-and-equipment]] and the start of depreciation, while the ₹30–35 crore is the true cash outflow. Depreciation commences on the full ₹80 crore from the point the assets are ready for use, regardless of when the cash was disbursed.

The capitalisation was the visible symptom of a phased capacity programme described by MD [[entities/srinivas-reddy]]:

- **Fuel cell capacity** — Phase 1 fully commissioned; Phase 2 to be commissioned by September–October 2026; Phase 3, a multifold expansion, to be commissioned by **March 2027**, with volume ramp-up from April 2027.
- **Dedicated facilities** being set up for data center infrastructure solutions and for oil & gas (the latter operational by October 2026).
- Multiple sub-units being created inside the existing dedicated aerospace facility.

The aggregate programme is **~₹500 crore across FY27 and FY28** (roughly 70% clean energy, 30% other verticals, with fungibility between them), of which only part falls in the current year and the remainder spills into the first one or two quarters of the next financial year. Management targets asset turnover of 4–5x overall and at least 6x on incremental capex — the return test that justifies carrying a large CWIP balance ahead of revenue. Funding is a blend of internal accruals and debt, with gross debt of ₹423.6 crore offset by ₹379 crore of investments, leaving effective net debt of only ₹20–30 crore (see [[concepts/capital-management]]).

The pattern is instructive: CWIP rises during construction, depresses reported ROCE while it sits idle, then converts into revenue-generating fixed assets. MTAR reported ROCE of 17.2% in Q1 FY27 against a target of ~23% next year, consistent with a balance sheet that has recently absorbed a large capitalisation.

## Why CWIP Is a Key Audit Matter

CWIP was identified as a **[[concepts/key-audit-matters]]** for the Apollo Micro Systems FY2025-26 consolidated audit by [[entities/s_t_mohite_and_co]] (partner: [[entities/hima_bindu_sagala]]), because of:

- The **significance of capital expenditure** incurred during the year
- Judgement required to assess whether incurred costs meet the recognition criteria of Ind AS 16

The auditor's procedures included:

- Assessing the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls over capital expenditure
- Detailed discussions with management on business plans and assumptions about future technology products and demand
- Reviewing capital expenditure, business plans and supporting documentation
- Substantive testing of major additions and deletions against capital-expenditure characteristics and classification policy
- Testing source documentation for capital nature, approval and segregation into appropriate categories
- Scrutiny of general ledgers for correct accounting
- Review of physical verification reports for CWIP
- Review of compliance with the Companies Act, Income Tax Act, Customs duty and GST for CAPEX additions, deletions, depreciation and carrying amounts
- Ensuring adequacy of disclosures in the [[concepts/consolidated-financial-statements]]

The auditor concluded that the procedures **did not identify any costs that had been inappropriately capitalised**. The movement analysis supports this: FY2025-26 additions of ₹17,045.61 lakhs and ₹2,868.43 lakhs from business combinations were partly offset by deletions of ₹7,512.88 lakhs, leaving the closing CWIP balance shown above.

The two risks auditors focus on are therefore (i) costs capitalised that do not meet the recognition criteria, and (ii) the **timing** of transfer out of CWIP — capitalising too early flatters current profit by deferring depreciation, while too late overstates assets and depresses return ratios. Management commentary on when an asset was "commissioned" versus when its cost was "capitalised" (as with MTAR's ₹80 crore) is exactly the evidence an auditor tests against.
